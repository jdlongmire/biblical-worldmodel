# Book Constitution

## God's Word, God's World

This document governs manuscript development. It is subordinate to Scripture and to the authority boundaries established in WP-BWM-0031 and WP-BWM-0032.

1. **Define before arguing.** Establish categorical meaning before asking the reader to accept an argument.
2. **Concrete before abstract.** Begin with the recognizable problem, observation, example, or question before introducing formal machinery.
3. **Scripture interprets Scripture first.** Canonical warrant governs theological claims; historical-grammatical work follows; scholarly and scientific considerations are engaged in their proper domains.
4. **Maintain epistemic categories.** Distinguish Revelation, Observation, Inference, Model, Hypothesis, and Speculation.
5. **Apply methodological scrutiny symmetrically.** Conventional, naturalistic, design, and author-preferred explanations face the same questions about assumptions, auxiliary hypotheses, explanatory burden, evidence, and loss conditions.
6. **State uncertainty rather than conceal it.** Use proportionate confidence. “I don't know” is an admissible conclusion.
7. **Introduce programme machinery only when the reader needs it.** The insight precedes the acronym, notation, or research-programme architecture.
8. **Primary sources before secondary sources.** Never fabricate citations or represent a secondary source as primary. Use “as cited in” when appropriate and record source limitations.
9. **Never protect one of our hypotheses from evidence.** DFM, PFH, Flood mechanisms, historical reconstructions, and other models remain open to evidentiary and exegetical challenge.
10. **Never confuse our worldmodel with God's world.** A model represents reality; it does not constitute reality.
11. **Christ the Logos is the ultimate point of coherence.** The uncreated Son is never reduced to a vector, operator, logical structure, informational primitive, or component within a created model.
12. **Word-count ranges govern proportion and pacing, not content.** Do not pad to a target or remove necessary argument merely to satisfy one.
13. **Architectural review precedes arbitrary cutting.** Materially oversized chapters should be examined for splitting, relocation to notes, or disposition to the technical corpus.
14. **Reader comprehension governs programme exposure.** A reader should not need mastery of BWM, DFM, Methodological Designism, LRT, TRT, PFH, CHFM, or their acronyms to understand the book.
15. **The manuscript must model the epistemic conduct it advocates.** Claims about our own preferred explanations must be categorized and tested with the same discipline applied to alternatives.
16. **Distinguish worldview from worldmodel.** Foundational commitments and corrigible representations are not the same epistemic category.
17. **Do not overclaim scientific or historical evidence.** State what was observed, what was inferred, and which assumptions connect them.
18. **Do not use consensus as a substitute for argument.** Consensus may be evidentially relevant; identify its domain, basis, and limitations.
19. **Engage serious opposing arguments in their strongest sourced form.** Avoid strawmen and rhetorical caricature.
20. **The book culminates in wisdom and the Logos.** Method is instrumental. The final coherence of truth, creation, revelation, rationality, and redemption is Christological.

## Working editorial maxim

> **Introduce the insight. Demonstrate its usefulness. Leave the machinery in the corpus.**

21. **Every chapter must face its strongest relevant objections.** State each objection fairly in a form a serious advocate would recognize; classify what it challenges; answer only as far as the evidence warrants; and explicitly identify any explanatory burden that remains. The same standard applies to author-preferred models.

## Objection dispositions

- **Resolved** — the objection rests on a demonstrable factual, categorical, exegetical, or logical error.
- **Answered** — the objection raises a legitimate concern, but the available evidence or argument supplies an adequate response.
- **Burden Remains** — **“This objection does not defeat the argument, but it does identify a genuine explanatory burden that remains.”**
- **Unresolved** — no presently satisfactory response is available; the objection remains evidence against the relevant claim or model.

## Objection classification

Classify objections according to the principal locus of challenge: **exegesis; observation/evidence; inference; model; philosophy; worldview/presupposition.** Mixed objections may receive multiple classifications. Do not answer an objection at a different epistemic level merely to evade its force.
