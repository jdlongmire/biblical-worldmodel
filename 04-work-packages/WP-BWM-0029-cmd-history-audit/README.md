# WP-BWM-0029: CMD Historical Foundations Audit

Status: active research

## Purpose

Audit historical claims held out of WP-BWM-0028 concerning Christian natural philosophy, operational science, uniformity, secondary causes, and the later development of scientific or methodological naturalism.

Primary sources are required for positive claims about named figures. Secondary scholarship may establish historiographic context and orient primary-source retrieval, but must remain labeled secondary.

## Audit targets

- Kepler: astronomy, book-of-nature language, divine glory, intelligibility.
- Boyle: experimental philosophy, Christianity, final causes, divine workmanship.
- Newton: law, divine agency, natural philosophy.
- Faraday: laws of nature, created order, methodological practice.
- Maxwell: uniformity, law, created molecules, design and measurement.
- Huxley and Victorian scientific naturalism: uniformity and naturalistic explanation.
- Medieval and early-modern secondary-cause inquiry.
- History of the term and rule methodological naturalism.

## Initial result

Several narrow claims have support. The broader genealogy remains partly open. In particular, the claim that operational science was later appropriated by methodological naturalism is too compressed for canonical historical use without substantially more evidence.

## Historiographic safeguard: influence without reduction

This audit shall not equate caution with minimization. It must test the positive historical thesis that explicitly Christian design commitments were materially influential in the formation, justification, and practice of early modern and modern natural science.

The governing research question is:

> What role did explicitly Christian design commitments play in the formation, justification, and practice of the methods and expectations that became characteristic of modern natural science?

### Anti-reduction rule

The audit shall neither infer scientific causation merely from a scientist's Christian identity nor treat documented theological commitments as biographical decoration when primary evidence shows those commitments materially informed expectations about nature, law, intelligibility, method, experimental vocation, secondary causation, or the purpose of natural philosophy.

### Influence dimensions

For each figure or movement, distinguish:

1. **Identity**: documented Christian/theological commitments.
2. **Motivation**: whether those commitments supplied reasons to investigate nature.
3. **Expectation**: whether they informed expectations of lawfulness, order, mathematical intelligibility, contingency, or discoverability.
4. **Method**: whether they materially shaped experimental, mathematical, causal, or epistemic practice.
5. **Institutional influence**: whether Christian institutions, networks, patronage, universities, societies, or theological controversies materially affected scientific work.
6. **Transmission**: whether these commitments or practices influenced later investigators.
7. **Retrospective interpretation**: later claims about religion/science relations, kept distinct from evidence about the historical actors themselves.

The audit must assess degree of influence rather than forcing a binary choice between "Christianity created science" and "religion was incidental."

### Evidential symmetry

Positive evidence of theological influence shall receive the same evidential weight as evidence of independence, tension, or naturalistic reinterpretation. The audit shall report mixed causation where the sources support it.
