# Tranche 2: Influence Evidence

Status: research note, 2026-09-26

## Purpose

Test whether theological/design commitments in major natural philosophers functioned only as personal identity markers or entered scientific motivation, expectation, interpretation, or method.

## Robert Boyle

**Primary evidence:** *The Christian Virtuoso* (1690) explicitly argues in its title and opening argument that commitment to experimental philosophy may assist rather than indispose a person to Christian faith.

**Influence dimensions supported:** identity, motivation, interpretation of experimental vocation.

**Confidence:** HIGH.

**Limit:** This establishes Boyle's own integration. It does not by itself establish the causal genealogy of experimental science as a whole.

## Isaac Newton

**Primary evidence:** In the *General Scholium* appended to the *Principia*, Newton argues from the ordered solar system to the counsel and dominion of an intelligent and powerful being and discusses divine governance directly within his natural-philosophical synthesis.

**Influence dimensions supported:** interpretation of cosmic order, explanatory boundary, natural-theological inference.

**Confidence:** HIGH for Newton's explicit integration.

**Limit:** Newton's theology was heterodox on important Christian doctrines. BWM should describe him as a theistic/design-oriented natural philosopher where relevant rather than use his presence as evidence of confessional orthodoxy.

## James Clerk Maxwell

**Primary evidence:** In published scientific discourse on molecules, Maxwell argues that molecular invariance could not be ascribed to the natural causes then known, describes molecules as made as they are from the beginning of their existence, and connects the enduring order with creation. He also explicitly acknowledges the provisional status of the molecular hypothesis.

**Influence dimensions supported:** expectation/interpretation of uniformity, explanatory boundary, methodological humility.

**Confidence:** HIGH.

**Significance:** Maxwell is especially valuable to CMD because his theological interpretation does not eliminate model revisability. The design inference and the admission that the molecular model could be superseded coexist in the same argument.

## Johannes Kepler

**Evidence:** A 26 March 1598 letter to Herwart von Hohenburg is cited in the Kepler critical corpus for the astronomer/priest and book-of-nature formulation, explicitly directing astronomical work toward divine glory.

**Influence dimensions supported if critical-edition verification holds:** motivation, vocation, expectation of intelligibility.

**Confidence:** MEDIUM-HIGH pending direct critical-edition inspection.

## Michael Faraday

**Evidence:** Specialist scholarship attributes to Faraday's 1844 memorandum on matter explicit statements that God works in material creation by laws and that the Creator governs material works by definite laws.

**Influence dimensions supported:** expectation of lawfulness and theological interpretation of natural regularity.

**Confidence:** MEDIUM pending direct manuscript or critical-edition inspection.

## Emerging result

The accumulated evidence is already inconsistent with a historiography in which Christian theology is treated merely as incidental private biography for these figures. For Boyle, Newton, and Maxwell, primary texts place theological/design reasoning inside their discussion of natural philosophy itself.

The evidence does not yet warrant a monocausal thesis that Christian designism alone generated modern science. The historically defensible working thesis is stronger than mere participation and narrower than monocausality:

> Explicit Christian and theistic design commitments materially shaped the motivation, expectations, interpretation, and in some cases methodological self-understanding of influential architects and practitioners of early modern and modern natural science.

Further work must test institutional influence, transmission, medieval continuity, and the later naturalist reinterpretation of shared scientific practices.
