# Romans 5 Pressure Test

Status: Research draft
Work package: WP-BWM-0028
Date: 2026-09-25
Primary text: Romans 5:12-21 (ESV)

## Question

Can the WP-BWM-0028 hypothesis that Adam's descendants inherit the curse and a corrupted condition without inheriting Adam's personal guilt preserve Paul's actual argument in Romans 5:12-21?

The test must allow the text to defeat or revise H1.

## H1 under test

H1 proposes:

1. Adam is personally guilty for Adam's own rebellion.
2. Adam's sin introduces a cursed/disordered human condition and death.
3. Adam's descendants inherit that condition.
4. Adam's personal guilt is not itself transferred to descendants merely by descent.
5. Descendants become personally guilty through their own actual voluntary sin.

## Immediate textual observations

### Romans 5:12

Paul presents a sequence: sin enters the world through one man, death through sin, death spreads to all, and "all sinned."

The verse unquestionably gives Adam's act transgenerational significance. The disputed issue is the relation expressed by "because all sinned."

At minimum, H1 cannot treat Adam as merely the first example of a pattern. Paul gives Adam a unique historical role in the entrance of sin and death.

### Romans 5:13-14

Sin exists before Mosaic law, and death reigns from Adam to Moses even over persons whose sin is not "like the transgression of Adam."

This creates pressure on any account that explains death only as the immediate penalty for each person's Adam-like conscious transgression. Paul explicitly distinguishes later sinners from Adam's transgression while maintaining the reign of death.

H1 can accommodate inherited mortality as curse, but must explain why Paul's argument makes Adam the decisive historical source of death's reign.

### Romans 5:15

The "one man's trespass" results in "many died." Paul then contrasts Adam with Christ and the abundance of grace through the one man Jesus Christ.

This is stronger than a claim that Adam merely creates a bad social environment. Adam's act has representative or corporate consequences for the many.

H1 survives this verse only if inherited curse/death can carry the corporate consequence Paul attributes to Adam without requiring transfer of Adam's personal culpability.

### Romans 5:16

Paul contrasts judgment following one trespass and bringing condemnation with the free gift following many trespasses and bringing justification.

This is a major pressure point. "Condemnation" is judicial language.

H1 must answer: is the condemnation here personal forensic guilt imputed from Adam, or the judicially imposed condemned/cursed state introduced through Adam and inherited by his descendants?

The latter reading is possible as a hypothesis but cannot simply be assumed.

### Romans 5:17

Death reigns through the one man's trespass. Those receiving grace and righteousness reign in life through Christ.

Again Adam's act is causally and corporately decisive.

H1 can affirm this fully if "death reigns" belongs to inherited curse. The verse does not by itself settle whether Adam's personal guilt is imputed.

### Romans 5:18

Paul's parallel becomes maximally compressed:

one trespass -> condemnation for all
one act of righteousness -> justification and life for all

This is the strongest single pressure point against H1.

A simplistic symmetry argument would say that if Christ's righteousness is reckoned representatively, Adam's guilt must be reckoned identically. But Paul need not mean that every feature of the two sides operates identically. Adam and Christ are analogous representative heads with opposite effects; the exact mechanism must be established rather than imported.

H1 nevertheless carries the burden of explaining how "condemnation for all" can arise from Adam's trespass without personal Adamic guilt being imputed.

### Romans 5:19

"By the one man's disobedience the many were made sinners," contrasted with "by the one man's obedience the many will be made righteous."

This is the most difficult wording for H1.

Possible semantic models include:

A. **Direct forensic imputation:** Adam's disobedience is reckoned to descendants as guilt; Christ's obedience is reckoned to the elect as righteousness.

B. **Constitutive/corporate condition:** Adam's disobedience constitutes the many as members of a fallen, sinful humanity; Christ's obedience constitutes the redeemed as righteous in him.

C. **Causal-dispositional consequence:** Adam's disobedience places descendants into K, in which they necessarily yet voluntarily become actual sinners; Christ's obedience establishes the redemptive order in which those united to him are actually justified and transformed.

H1 requires something like B+C. The text must determine whether that is sufficient.

### Romans 5:20-21

Law increases trespass; grace abounds; sin reigns in death; grace reigns through righteousness leading to eternal life through Christ.

Paul's controlling categories are dominion, death, grace, righteousness, and life. The section is larger than a narrow theory of imputation, but forensic categories are undeniably present.

## Structural argument

Paul's structure can be represented:

Adam
-> one trespass/disobedience
-> sin enters
-> death enters and reigns
-> many die
-> judgment/condemnation
-> many constituted sinners

Christ
-> obedience/righteous act
-> grace
-> justification
-> gift of righteousness
-> life
-> many constituted righteous

Any acceptable BWM model must preserve every arrow that Paul actually asserts.

## What H1 explains well

### 1. Historical solidarity

H1 strongly preserves Adam as the historical boundary through whom sin and death enter the human order.

### 2. Universal mortality

Inherited curse directly explains why death reigns beyond Adam and before Mosaic law.

### 3. Distinction between Adam's act and descendants' acts

H1 preserves real individual agency and can naturally account for Paul's recognition that later persons sin differently from Adam.

### 4. Romans 1-3 integration

Paul has already established universal actual culpable sin before reaching Romans 5. H1 integrates that material without making personal guilt depend exclusively upon descent.

### 5. Ezekiel 18 integration

H1 naturally preserves the canonical principle that a son does not bear his father's iniquity as personal culpability, while allowing consequences of ancestral sin to propagate.

## Where H1 is under genuine pressure

### 1. "Condemnation for all" in verse 18

If condemnation necessarily means personal forensic liability for Adam's act, H1 fails.

H1 therefore requires a demonstrated distinction between:
- Adam's personal culpability, and
- the judicially condemned/cursed corporate condition that Adam's trespass brings upon humanity.

That distinction cannot be stipulated.

### 2. "Made sinners" in verse 19

If "made sinners" necessarily means "personally counted guilty of Adam's disobedience," H1 fails.

If it means constituted within the Adamic order as fallen/sinful persons, H1 remains viable, especially if actual personal guilt follows from inevitable voluntary sin within K.

This requires lexical, grammatical, and Pauline-context analysis.

### 3. Adam-Christ symmetry

H1 must avoid asymmetric special pleading.

If Adam's side is reduced to mere causal influence while Christ's side is strongly representative and forensic, Paul's parallel is weakened.

A viable H1 must affirm genuine Adamic corporate representation. The disputed point is narrower: whether corporate representation entails transfer of Adam's personal guilt.

### 4. Infants and persons without developed moral agency

If personal condemnation requires actual voluntary culpable rebellion, the status of those dying without developed capacity for such acts becomes an unavoidable question.

H1 cannot use "all personally sin" as an unexplained universal premise while leaving this class undefined.

This issue requires separate treatment and may interact with the user's age/accountability doctrine.

## Critical distinction: guilt versus judicial consequence

The hypothesis currently needs this distinction:

**Personal guilt:** moral liability because the person's own culpable willing is sinful.

**Inherited judicial consequence:** a condition imposed upon humanity because of Adam's representative failure, including mortality, alienation, corruption, and existence under condemnation/curse.

The key exegetical question is whether Romans 5 permits "condemnation" to denote the second without necessarily entailing inherited personal guilt in the first sense.

If Paul collapses those categories, H1 fails.
If Paul distinguishes them, H1 may survive.

## Christological symmetry test

H1 must also explain the Christ side.

Christ's work includes at least:
- representative obedience;
- objective accomplishment;
- forensic justification;
- union with Christ;
- gift of righteousness;
- regeneration/sanctification;
- resurrection life.

Therefore Adam-Christ symmetry need not mean identity of mechanism at every point. The symmetry may be headship and determinative corporate consequence:

Adam as head -> trespass -> fallen order/death/condemnation -> persons in Adam become sinners.
Christ as head -> obedience -> justified/redemptive order/life -> persons in Christ are constituted righteous.

But this formulation remains provisional until the meaning of "made sinners/made righteous" and "condemnation/justification" is established.

## Provisional verdict

**H1 survives the first textual pressure test, but only provisionally.**

Romans 5 decisively rules out any model in which Adam is merely an example whose sin affects only himself. Adam's one trespass has objective, judicial, corporate consequences for humanity.

Romans 5 does **not yet establish, from this first-pass analysis alone, that Adam's personal culpability must be numerically transferred or imputed to each descendant**. However, verses 18-19 create a substantial burden for the non-imputation hypothesis. H1 must establish a coherent category of Adamic corporate condemnation/curse that is judicially real while remaining distinct from personal culpability for Adam's act.

Accordingly, H1 should be refined:

> Adam's descendants inherit not merely adverse consequences but an objectively condemned Adamic order: mortality, alienation, corruption, and subjection to death arising judicially from Adam's representative trespass. Whether this corporate condemnation includes imputation of Adam's personal guilt remains an open exegetical question. Personal culpability for each morally accountable descendant is certainly established by that person's actual voluntary sin.

This refinement strengthens the seriousness with which H1 takes Romans 5 without prematurely conceding or denying imputed guilt.

## Required next exegesis

Before H1 can be accepted or rejected, investigate:

1. the Greek construction behind "because all sinned" in Romans 5:12;
2. the semantic range and Pauline usage of condemnation in verses 16 and 18;
3. the verb translated "made/constituted" in verse 19;
4. Paul's "in Adam / in Christ" corporate categories, especially 1 Corinthians 15:21-22, 45-49;
5. the relation of Romans 5 to Romans 1-3 and 6;
6. whether Ezekiel 18:20 addresses the same category of liability as Romans 5;
7. Second Temple/Jewish background only after the Pauline and canonical evidence is established;
8. major historical interpretations, clearly labeled secondary, including Augustine, Reformed federal/imputation readings, Eastern/ancestral-sin readings, and non-imputation alternatives.

## Disposition

H1: **OPEN, viable but under substantial canonical pressure.**

No canonical promotion of the non-imputation thesis is warranted yet.
