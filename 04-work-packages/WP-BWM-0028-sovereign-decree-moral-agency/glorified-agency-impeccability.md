# Glorified Agency and Freely Embraced Impeccability

Status: Research draft
Work package: WP-BWM-0028
Date: 2026-09-25

## Purpose

Develop the implication that sanctification and glorification perfect rather than negate moral agency. The redeemed agent may increasingly desire freedom from sin and, in glorification, freely embrace a condition in which sin no longer functions as a live desirable alternative.

This artifact distinguishes conceptual knowledge of sin from deliberative and volitional availability of sin.

## 1. Problem

If glorified persons cannot sin, does glorification remove moral freedom?

The current model answers: inability to sin does not entail absence of freedom. The deeper question is whether the redeemed person's own sanctified will positively desires final impeccability.

## 2. Sanctification as trajectory of desire

Sanctification includes progressive reordering of loves, desires, judgments, and willing.

The redeemed person increasingly:
- recognizes sin as disorder rather than genuine good;
- desires communion with God;
- desires conformity to Christ;
- repudiates remaining rebellion;
- desires deliverance from the presence and power of sin.

Therefore the movement toward impeccability need not be represented as an externally imposed deprivation of a valued liberty.

Working proposition:

> A sanctified person may freely desire the permanent loss of sin as a live option because rebellion contains no intrinsic good worth preserving.

## 3. Three capacities

The model distinguishes:

### C1. Conceptual capacity

Ability to understand or represent what sin, rebellion, falsehood, or disobedience means.

Perfect holiness does not require ignorance of evil. God knows what sin is without sinning. Christ understands sin without being sinful.

Therefore glorification need not eliminate C1.

### C2. Deliberative availability

Ability to entertain sin as a live candidate for one's own action: "perhaps this rebellion would be good for me."

This may disappear in glorification.

### C3. Volitional availability

Ability actually to desire and will rebellion as one's chosen good.

This is incompatible with perfected ordered love if the agent apprehends God and creaturely good truthfully and has no disordered affection by which rebellion could appear as a preferable good.

The working glorification model preserves C1 while denying C2/C3 as live sinful possibilities.

## 4. Sin as false apparent good

Sin does not supply a positive moral good unavailable in holiness. Its attraction depends upon misvaluation or disordered valuation of genuine goods.

Under the earlier first-sin model:

perceived creaturely/self-good
-> autonomous valuation
-> disordered assent
-> rebellion.

Under perfected communion, the false ordering is healed.

Thus the glorified person may understand the proposition "rebellion against God" while never apprehending rebellion as a genuine good-for-me worthy of assent.

## 5. Noetic and affective healing

The fallen model contains:

K_noetic: corrupted epistemic orientation.
K_aff: disordered loves/desires.

Glorification removes the corruption represented by these conditions.

The intended trajectory is:

K_noetic -> Spirit-wrought renewal -> perfected creaturely knowledge/orientation.
K_aff -> sanctification -> perfectly ordered love.
fractured communion -> reconciliation -> consummated communion.

This does not imply creaturely omniscience. Perfected creaturely knowledge remains finite but no longer corrupted by sin.

## 6. Formal model

Let:

- Sanc(P) = P is undergoing sanctification.
- DesImp(P) = P desires final freedom from sin.
- Gl(P) = P is glorified.
- OrdLove(P) = P's loves are perfectly ordered.
- SinConcept(P) = P can understand the concept of sin.
- SinLive(P) = sin appears to P as a live desirable candidate for personal willing.
- SinWill(P) = P wills sin.

Working propositions:

Sanc(P) -> progressively possible/increasing DesImp(P).

Gl(P) -> OrdLove(P).

Gl(P) -> SinConcept(P) may remain true.

OrdLove(P) -> not SinLive(P).

not SinLive(P) under perfected truthful valuation -> not SinWill(P).

Therefore:

Gl(P) -> genuine voluntary agency without sinful alternatives functioning as desirable alternatives.

## 7. Freely embraced impeccability

The redeemed person's desire for impeccability matters.

The model proposes that glorification can fulfill the sanctified will's own increasingly holy desire:

"I want to love God without rebellion."
"I want no remaining sin."
"I want my loves fully ordered."
"I want communion that cannot again be shattered."

The transition is therefore not adequately modeled as:

agent wants sin -> God removes ability.

A more adequate model is:

Spirit sanctifies agent
-> agent increasingly repudiates sin and desires holiness
-> God completes sanctification
-> agent's loves are perfected
-> sin ceases to function as a live desirable alternative
-> agent freely and eternally wills the good.

## 8. Adam and glorified humanity

The model now distinguishes two good states.

### Adamic mutable innocence

Adam is good and unfallen but mutable.

He can:
- understand God's command;
- encounter a contrary proposal;
- consider an alternative;
- perceive created/self-goods;
- culpably reorder those goods;
- fall.

### Glorified perfected communion

The redeemed person is good by grace, restored, sanctified, and glorified.

The person can know what rebellion is but knows it truthfully as rebellion and disorder. Loves are perfected. No disordered self-interest exists by which rebellion can appear preferable to communion.

Thus:

Adam: innocent + mutable + contrary good can appear desirable.
Glorified saint: redeemed + perfected + sin known as destructive disorder + no disordered love making sin desirable.

## 9. Eden and New Creation are not identical states

The final state is not merely a reset to the initial state.

The actual decreed history includes:
- creation;
- innocent agency;
- Fall;
- curse;
- personal sin;
- judgment;
- election;
- redemption;
- regeneration;
- sanctification;
- learned dependence and grace;
- glorification;
- consummated communion.

The redeemed therefore arrive at consummation having been redeemed and sanctified through a history in which the nature and consequence of rebellion, and the mercy and holiness of God, have been manifested.

Working distinction:

**Eden: mutable innocence.**
**New Creation: redeemed, knowledgeable, perfected, immutable communion.**

"Knowledgeable" here does not mean omniscient. It means the redeemed history is genuinely part of the person's identity and apprehension of God's grace, justice, holiness, and the character of sin.

## 10. Theodicy implication

A possible implication is that the decreed history terminates in a form of creaturely communion that initial innocence did not yet instantiate: redeemed persons freely embrace perfected holiness with an informed knowledge of rebellion, judgment, mercy, and redemption.

This proposition must be handled carefully.

The model does **not** yet claim:

- God metaphysically needed evil to achieve glory;
- evil is logically necessary for perfected agency;
- God could not have created impeccable creatures;
- every instance of evil is individually required as a pedagogical instrument.

The narrower claim is:

> Within the history the Father actually decreed, redemption and sanctification culminate in freely embraced perfected communion whose participants know God's holiness, justice, mercy, and grace through the history of redemption.

Whether this supplies a broader theodicy is a later question.

## 11. Canonical controls

### Romans 7:21-25

Potential support for the redeemed person's desire for deliverance from remaining sin. Interpretive context and debates over the identity/state of the speaker must be acknowledged.

### Romans 8:18-30

Creation's liberation, conformity to the Son, and glorification provide a redemptive trajectory.

### Romans 12:1-2

Renewal of mind supports progressive transformed valuation.

### Galatians 5:16-24

Flesh/Spirit conflict and Spirit-produced fruit support reordering of desires in sanctification.

### Philippians 1:21-23; 3:20-21

Desire for Christ and transformation of the body support the trajectory toward completed communion.

### 1 John 3:2-3

Seeing Christ and being like him is a principal control for glorified conformity and present purification.

### 1 Corinthians 15:42-57

Incorruptibility and immortality constrain the final-state anthropology.

### Revelation 21-22

The absence of curse, exclusion of impurity, direct communion, worship, and enduring reign constrain the consummated state.

## 12. Pressure questions

1. Does Scripture warrant full impeccability of glorified saints or only permanent security?
2. Does permanent security itself entail impeccability when combined with absence of corruption?
3. Can a glorified person conceptually represent sin while sin is deliberatively unavailable as a live good?
4. Is the desire for final impeccability itself an effect of sanctification?
5. Does the history of redemption remain epistemically available in the New Creation?
6. How much of the Eden/New Creation asymmetry is explicit Scripture versus theological synthesis?
7. Can the theodicy implication be stated without making evil instrumentally necessary to God?

## 13. No-smuggling constraints

1. Do not equate freedom with ability to sin.
2. Do not equate knowledge of sin with desire for sin.
3. Do not equate conceptual possibility with deliberative availability.
4. Do not assume glorified persons are omniscient.
5. Do not infer that Adam was sinful because he was mutable.
6. Do not infer that God required evil to create perfected agents.
7. Do not make redeemed experience an independent source of authority over Scripture.
8. Do not claim a full theodicy from the existence of this trajectory alone.
9. Do not describe glorification as coercive removal of a desired good without argument.
10. Do not erase the Spirit's sanctifying agency by treating self-renunciation as autonomous human achievement.

## 14. Current disposition

**Genuine agency without ability to sin:** SUPPORTED as coherent and strongly indicated by divine freedom and consummated anthropology.

**Conceptual knowledge of sin after glorification:** PLAUSIBLE and consistent with holiness; requires further canonical development.

**Sin as a live desirable alternative after glorification:** REJECTED within the working model.

**Sanctified desire for final impeccability:** STRONGLY PLAUSIBLE; requires explicit canonical synthesis.

**Eden as mutable innocence versus New Creation as perfected immutable communion:** STRONG SYNTHESIS, further exegetical testing required.

**The actual decreed history as producing freely embraced, informed, perfected communion:** PROMISING THEODICY IMPLICATION, not yet promoted to a necessary explanation of evil.
