# Romans 5:12-19 Greek Exegesis

Status: Research draft
Work package: WP-BWM-0028
Date: 2026-09-25

## Purpose

Deepen the Romans 5 pressure test by examining the Greek clauses carrying the main burden for H1: Romans 5:12, 16, 18, and 19. This is a grammatical-semantic layer, not a final doctrinal verdict.

Primary Greek baseline: SBLGNT. English comparison: ESV.

## Romans 5:12: eph' ho pantes hemarton

Key clause:

`eph' ho pantes hemarton`

SBLGNT parses `hemarton` as aorist active indicative, third plural: "all sinned." The construction `eph' ho` follows Paul's statement that death spread to all people.

### Observations

1. The grammatical subject of `hemarton` is `pantes`, "all."
2. The verb is active. The clause directly predicates sinning of "all."
3. The clause does not say "Adam sinned for all" or explicitly state "Adam's guilt was imputed to all."
4. The larger sentence nevertheless makes Adam historically decisive: sin enters through one man and death through sin.
5. Therefore verse 12 simultaneously supports Adamic causal/corporate priority and actual sin predicated of all.

### Consequence for H1

The Greek of verse 12 does not, by itself, force the proposition "all are personally guilty because all sinned in Adam." That conclusion requires a semantic/exegetical argument about `eph' ho` and the larger Adamic parallel.

Conversely, H1 may not use `pantes hemarton` to erase Adam's representative role. Paul's sentence begins with the one man as the historical entrance point of sin and death.

**Disposition:** compatible with H1, non-decisive.

## Romans 5:16 and 18: katakrima

Paul uses `katakrima` in the judicial contrast between Adam and Christ. In verse 18 the compressed structure is:

one trespass -> all people -> `katakrima`
one righteous act -> all people -> `dikaiosin zoes`

### Observations

1. `katakrima` is plainly judicial/condemnatory language.
2. H1 therefore cannot reduce Adamic inheritance to merely biological mortality, environmental disadvantage, or imitation.
3. Adam's trespass produces an objectively judicial consequence for humanity.
4. The noun itself identifies condemnation; it does not independently specify the complete mechanism by which that condemnation relates to each descendant's personal culpability.
5. Verse 18's parallel with justification requires a robust corporate/representative Adam-Christ structure.

### Required refinement

The model must distinguish, if the text permits it:

- Adam's personal guilt for Adam's act;
- corporate/judicial condemnation of the Adamic order because of Adam's representative trespass;
- inherited corruption and mortality within that order;
- each descendant's personal guilt for actual culpable rebellion.

Calling Adam's effect merely "consequences" is now too weak. **Judicial corporate condemnation** must be represented in H1.

**Disposition:** substantial pressure, but the noun alone does not settle personal imputation.

## Romans 5:19: katestathesan hamartoloi

Key clause:

`dia tes parakoes tou henos anthropou hamartoloi katestathesan hoi polloi`

The corresponding Christ clause uses the future passive:

`dikaioi katastathesontai hoi polloi`

The same verb, `kathistemi`, structures both sides.

### Observations

1. Paul does not merely say that Adam influenced the many to imitate him.
2. The many are "made/constituted" sinners through the one man's disobedience.
3. Correspondingly, the many are "made/constituted" righteous through the obedience of the one.
4. The passive construction gives the head's act constitutive significance for the many.
5. Any H1 account based solely on descendants independently repeating Adam's pattern is insufficient.
6. The lexical/grammatical force does not by itself answer whether "constituted sinners" means:
   - counted personally guilty of Adam's act;
   - constituted members of a fallen/condemned Adamic humanity;
   - or a corporate condition that necessarily issues in actual personal sin and guilt.
7. The parallel constrains the answer: the Adam side cannot be weakened merely to protect H1.

### Symmetry constraint

The same verb on both sides creates a genuine structural symmetry, but structural symmetry does not entail identity of every soteriological and hamartiological mechanism.

Christ's obedience has objective representative force, forensic consequence, covenantal incorporation, and transformative effects. Therefore Adam's disobedience must likewise have objective representative and constitutive force. The open question is whether that force necessarily includes imputation of Adam's personal culpability.

**Disposition:** strongest grammatical pressure on H1; H1 remains possible only in a robust corporate/representative form.

## 1 Corinthians 15 control text

Paul's broader Adam-Christ framework should control against atomistic readings:

`en to Adam pantes apothneskousin`
and
`en to Christo pantes zoopoiethesontai`

The "in Adam / in Christ" structure confirms corporate solidarity and headship. It strengthens the conclusion that Romans 5 cannot be reduced to imitation or merely independent personal choices.

It still requires care: death "in Adam" and life "in Christ" establish corporate location and consequence, but the phrase itself does not lexically state "Adam's personal guilt is imputed."

## Revised H1

The original H1 should now be strengthened:

> Adam's trespass is the representative human rebellion through which sin, death, and judicial condemnation enter the human order. Adam's descendants are born into the objectively condemned and corrupted Adamic order and are therefore mortal, alienated, and dispositionally ordered toward sin. Adam's act has real corporate and constitutive force. The current hypothesis distinguishes this inherited corporate condemnation and corruption from personal culpability for Adam's own intention and act. Each morally accountable descendant becomes personally culpable through actual voluntary rebellion. Whether Paul's language of `katakrima` and `katestathesan hamartoloi` ultimately requires imputation of Adam's personal guilt remains open.

This is materially stronger than the earlier phrase "inherit the curse." It takes Romans 5's judicial and representative language seriously.

## Noetic-effects constraint

The strengthened corporate Adamic model must not collapse inherited noetic corruption into personal culpability. The descendant may inherit a condemned Adamic order that includes epistemic and affective disorder while the ground of that descendant's personal culpability remains personally attributable rebellion. This distinction is formalized in `agency-culpability-model.md` and remains subject to the Romans 5 falsification conditions below.

## Falsification condition

H1 fails if further canonical and lexical analysis establishes either proposition:

1. `katakrima` in Romans 5 necessarily entails that each descendant bears personal culpability for Adam's individual act, rather than corporate judicial condemnation arising from Adam's representative act; or
2. `katestathesan hamartoloi` necessarily means that Adam's personal guilt is forensically reckoned to the many, excluding a constitutive corporate-condemnation reading.

H1 survives only if the distinction between **corporate condemnation in Adam** and **personal culpability for one's own sin** is exegetically defensible rather than merely logically conceivable.

## Current result

- Adam as mere example: **REJECTED**.
- Adam as merely causal ancestor of mortality: **INSUFFICIENT**.
- Adam as representative/corporate head whose trespass judicially constitutes the human order under condemnation, corruption, and death: **REQUIRED BY THE CURRENT READING**.
- Numerical/personal imputation of Adam's culpability to every descendant: **OPEN**.
- Personal culpability through actual voluntary sin: **STRONGLY SUPPORTED elsewhere in Romans 1-3 and compatible with Romans 5:12**.
