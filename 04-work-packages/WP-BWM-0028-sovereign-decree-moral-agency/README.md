## Controlling thesis

The Father sovereignly decrees and ordains the whole moral history of creation. The decree is certain, comprehensive, and unfailing. God is inherently logical. Logical coherence is not an autonomous law above God; it is inseparable from the divine nature. The Father's sovereign ordination therefore cannot coherently negate the genuine creaturely agency God ordains.

Within the decreed history:

- the Father decrees and ordains;
- the Son creates, sustains, judges, redeems, reconciles, and restores;
- the Holy Spirit of Truth reveals, testifies, convicts, regenerates, sanctifies, preserves, and seals;
- the works of the triune God toward creation are undivided while the divine persons fulfill distinct personal missions within the one divine purpose.

The infallibly decreed end is the manifestation of divine glory, particularly the glory of the Son as Creator, Lord, righteous Judge, and gracious Redeemer.

## Three kinds of necessity

### Logical necessity

God is inherently logical and cannot actualize contradiction. A genuine moral agent must genuinely own the voluntary act for which that agent is morally judged. God cannot coherently constitute a creature as culpable for an act while, in the same respect, making the sinful moral willing God's own act rather than the creature's.

Governing constraint:

> God's sovereignty cannot require the negation of the creaturely agency God sovereignly ordained.

The creature is not God. Creaturely will is derivative and sustained by God, yet the creature's voluntary act is genuinely the creature's act.

### Decretive necessity

The Father freely, sovereignly, and unfailingly decrees and ordains the history that will obtain, including its ends, means, boundaries, judgment, redemption, restoration, and consummation. Once decreed, the ordained history is certain and cannot fail.

This does not make creation metaphysically necessary to God. The necessity asserted here is the certainty of the freely decreed history.

### Dispositional necessity

After the Fall, Adam's descendants enter a cursed and disordered condition. Their sinful acts are not externally coerced. They proceed voluntarily from their own disordered loves, intentions, judgments, and desires. Universal personal sin is therefore proposed as morally and dispositionally necessary while remaining genuinely voluntary and personally culpable.

The glorified state is a limiting case: genuine agency does not require a continuing ability to choose evil. Perfected communion can entail genuinely voluntary righteousness without apostasy.

## Theological architecture

### Father: decree and ordination

The Father sovereignly decrees and ordains the whole moral history of creation. The Father's decree establishes the certainty, scope, limits, means, and final end of creation, history, judgment, redemption, restoration, and consummation.

Creation, Adam's stewardship, the Fall, curse, personal sin, judgment, election, redemption, restoration, renewal, and consummation occur within the ordained history.

Divine ordination establishes the certainty of the event without transferring the creature's sinful intention to God.

### Triune missions and inseparable operation

The Father, Son, and Holy Spirit are the one true God. The external works of God are undivided.

Within the ordered economy, the Father decrees, ordains, sends, gives, and elects. The Son creates and sustains, judges, accomplishes redemption, reconciles, and restores. The Holy Spirit reveals truth, glorifies the Son, convicts concerning sin, righteousness, and judgment, applies redemption, regenerates, indwells, sanctifies, preserves, and seals.

Personal distinction does not entail three competing wills or gods.

### Image-bearing personhood and moral agency

The image of God entails real individual personhood and includes rationality, selfhood, intentionality, deliberation, willing, action, love, and pursuit of perceived good.

A real moral agent's voluntary act is truly that agent's act. Praise, blame, judgment, forgiveness, and condemnation presuppose this ownership.

Freedom here means action according to the nature, knowledge, loves, intentions, and will of the genuine personal agent. Metaphysical indeterminacy between contrary possibilities is not treated as constitutive of genuine freedom.

### Ordered self-love and communion

Natural self-love is the orientation of a personal self toward perceived preservation, good, happiness, flourishing, security, fulfillment, and life. It is a created good when ordered under supreme love for God and genuine love for neighbor.

God is Creator, sustainer, rightful Lord, highest good, and final authority. The neighbor is a fellow image-bearer and is to be loved as oneself.

Rightly ordered creaturely life includes supreme love for God, rightly ordered self-love, genuine love for neighbor, and faithful stewardship of creation.

### Sin and the Fall

Sin is creaturely rebellion against God, expressed as lawlessness, unbelief, disordered love, and the breaking of rightful communion with God, neighbor, self, and creation.

Sin is not a positive substance created by God or an independent power equal to God. It is privation, corruption, disorder, and misdirection of created goods.

Adam was an image-bearing person, genuine moral agent, and delegated steward. He voluntarily preferred self-determination to God's command and sought creaturely good on terms independent of God's word. Adam's rebellion was Adam's own culpable act.

The sinful moral quality of the act belongs to the creature's intention, desire, and willing, and not to any defect, corruption, or sinful intention in God.

### Curse, inheritance, and personal guilt

This package preserves the proposed distinction:

- Adam bears the personal guilt of Adam's own sin.
- Adam's descendants inherit the curse and a disordered condition.
- Adam's descendants do not become personally guilty merely because Adam committed his particular sin.
- Each descendant becomes guilty through actual, voluntary, culpable rebellion.

The inherited condition includes mortality, alienation, disordered desire, fractured communion, and inability in the fallen condition to love God supremely apart from divine grace.

This requires dedicated exegesis, especially Romans 5:12-19, before canonical promotion.

### Universal personal sin

Every descendant of Adam is a genuine image-bearer and moral agent living under the curse. Each necessarily yet voluntarily repeats the rupture of communion.

The necessity asserted is dispositional rather than external coercion. The person acts according to his or her own disordered loves and perceived good. Every person therefore commits actual sin and becomes personally guilty through actual voluntary rebellion.

A major research question is to specify exactly what makes universal personal sin necessary before the first culpable act while maintaining the distinction between inherited corruption and personal guilt.

### Judgment

The Son judges real persons, acts, intentions, loves, harms, knowledge, freedom, deliberation, persistence, and violated relationships.

Just punishment is commensurate with the gravity of the offense. Gravity includes act, harm, knowledge, intention, freedom, deliberation, persistence, relationship violated, and the rightful claims of the offended party.

Sin against God concerns the creature's total moral relation to Creator, sustainer, rightful Lord, highest good, and giver of the creature's life and agency. Its gravity cannot be reduced to the temporal duration of an act or immediate visible harm.

The synthesis does not reduce judgment to a simple "infinite offended party therefore infinite punishment" formula.

### Mercy, election, redemption, and sealing

No guilty sinner can demand acquittal, forgiveness, redemption, mercy, or grace.

The Father freely elects a particular people from guilty humanity according to sovereign mercy and gives the elect to the Son. Election is not grounded in superior natural worth, lesser guilt, autonomous seeking, or self-established righteousness.

The Son effectually redeems all whom the Father gives him. The Holy Spirit applies the Son's accomplished redemption to every elect person, bringing the elect to faith and repentance, regenerating, indwelling, sanctifying, preserving, and sealing them for the day of redemption.

The Spirit's sealing does not create election. It applies, confirms, and secures the Father's elective purpose in the Son.

The condemned are judged for their own actual culpable sins. The redeemed are guilty persons who receive sovereign mercy. The synthesis therefore asserts: the condemned receive justice, the redeemed receive mercy, and no one receives injustice.

### Restoration and consummation

Redemption restores the communion sin shattered. The Son reconciles the elect to God and reforms disordered self-love. The Holy Spirit enables the redeemed to love God supremely and neighbor as self. Sanctification progressively restores rightly ordered creaturely love; glorification perfects it.

The Son will finally restore creation from the curse and gather the redeemed into consummated communion with God.

The Son is glorified as Judge because he exposes and judges genuine evil and real rebellion with righteousness. The Son is glorified as Redeemer because he saves actual guilty persons whom no obligation required God to save and restores the communion sin destroyed.

Evil is neither ultimate nor outside the Father's decree. Evil is creaturely voluntary rebellion, morally attributable to the creature as sinner, whose occurrence, limits, consequences, defeat, and final disposition are comprehended within the Father's sovereign ordination.
