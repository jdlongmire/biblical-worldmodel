# Agency, Noetic Effects, and Individual Culpability Model

Status: Research draft
Work package: WP-BWM-0028
Date: 2026-09-25

## Purpose

Formalize the distinction among the inherited Adamic judicial condition, the noetic and dispositional effects of the curse, actual creaturely willing, and individual culpability.

This distinction is load-bearing for H1. Inherited corruption must not be silently treated as identical to personal guilt, while personal responsibility must not be weakened into epistemic innocence.

## 1. Refined fallen-condition model

Replace the undifferentiated K with a structured condition:

K = {K_jud, K_mort, K_noetic, K_aff, K_rel, K_cre}

where:

- **K_jud** = the corporate judicial Adamic condition under condemnation/curse.
- **K_mort** = mortality and subjection to death.
- **K_noetic** = corruption affecting cognition, interpretation, judgment, valuation, and epistemic orientation.
- **K_aff** = affective/dispositional disorder, including disordered self-love and desire.
- **K_rel** = fractured communion with God and neighbor.
- **K_cre** = life within a created order subjected to frustration/corruption.

These dimensions are related but must not be collapsed.

## 2. Noetic effects

The noetic effects of the curse describe the fallen condition of human knowing. They include tendencies toward:

- suppression of known truth;
- futile reasoning;
- distorted valuation;
- rationalization of disordered desire;
- self-referential interpretation;
- exchange of truth for falsehood;
- resistance to God as final epistemic and moral authority.

Romans 1:18-32 is a primary canonical control because Paul does not describe fallen humanity as possessing mere innocent ignorance. Knowledge, suppression, darkening, exchange, desire, action, and approval form a moral-epistemic sequence.

## 3. Noetic condition is not personal culpability

The model shall preserve:

K_noetic(P) != Q(P)

Merely being born into the noetically damaged Adamic condition is not, under H1, identical to being personally culpable for Adam's intention and act.

Likewise:

K_aff(P) != Q(P)

Inherited affective disorder is not itself defined as personal guilt for Adam's act.

This distinction does not make the fallen person epistemically neutral. It distinguishes inherited condition from personally attributable moral action.

## 4. Culpable noetic action

Personal culpability arises where the genuine moral agent personally exercises agency in relation to truth and good.

Representative sequence:

K_noetic + K_aff
-> encounter with truth / moral knowledge
-> personal apprehension sufficient for responsibility
-> voluntary suppression, exchange, refusal, rationalization, or disordered willing
-> actual rebellion R(P)
-> personal guilt Q(P)

Thus the inherited condition supplies the corrupted epistemic and affective environment/disposition. The person supplies the personally attributable suppression, unbelief, intention, and act.

## 5. Recursive darkening

Romans 1 supports a recursive model rather than a static defect:

curse/noetic disorder
-> encounter with truth
-> culpable suppression and disordered valuation
-> further darkening/futility
-> reinforced disordered willing and practice

Later noetic darkness may therefore be both:

1. an effect of the inherited fallen condition; and
2. a judicial/consequential deepening associated with the person's own rebellion.

These must be distinguished case by case.

## 6. Agency ownership condition

For personal culpability Q(P) to attach to act A:

1. P must be the relevant personal agent.
2. A must genuinely proceed through P's own willing/intention.
3. The morally relevant truth or obligation must be sufficiently apprehensible for responsibility.
4. The act must express actual disordered willing rather than external compulsion contrary to P's will.
5. The judgment must account for knowledge, intention, deliberation, capacity, and other morally relevant conditions.

This model does not require libertarian indeterminacy. It requires genuine personal ownership of willing.

## 7. Dispositional necessity restated

The earlier expression:

□K(P sins voluntarily)

must not mean:

"K itself is P's personally culpable sin."

It means:

Given the fallen Adamic condition, including noetic and affective corruption, ordinary morally accountable descendants of Adam certainly act according to disordered loves and epistemic orientation and therefore personally commit voluntary sin.

The intended sequence is:

inherited K -> disordered disposition -> genuine personal willing -> actual sin -> personal guilt

not:

inherited K -> personal guilt simpliciter.

## 8. Corporate condemnation versus individual culpability

The strengthened Romans 5 model requires two judicial categories to remain analytically distinct unless exegesis proves they collapse:

### Corporate Adamic condemnation

Humanity is born into an objectively condemned Adamic order resulting from Adam's representative trespass. This includes mortality, corruption, alienation, and subjection to death.

### Individual culpability

The individual is personally blameworthy for personally attributable rebellion.

The open Romans 5 question remains whether Paul's corporate condemnation category necessarily includes imputation of Adam's personal culpability to descendants. H1 currently denies that entailment but has not yet established the denial exegetically.

## 9. Accountability boundary

This model creates an explicit question concerning persons who lack developed capacity for morally accountable personal action.

If Q(P) requires actual personally attributable culpable rebellion, then the model must specify how it treats:

- infancy;
- severe cognitive incapacity;
- other cases in which morally accountable apprehension and deliberation may be absent.

The inherited Adamic condition still applies. Personal culpability cannot simply be asserted without identifying its ground.

This issue requires a dedicated canonical analysis and must remain OPEN.

## 10. Judgment implications

Individualized righteous judgment should distinguish:

- inherited condition;
- available knowledge;
- apprehended truth;
- degree of noetic impairment;
- voluntary suppression;
- intention;
- deliberation;
- persistence;
- actual harms;
- relational obligations;
- subsequent hardening.

This coheres with scriptural patterns of differentiated accountability while retaining universal dependence upon grace.

## 11. Regeneration and noetic restoration

The Spirit's application of redemption addresses the person comprehensively. Regeneration does not merely change legal status. The Spirit renews the person, including epistemic and affective orientation.

The redemptive trajectory is therefore:

fallen noetic/affective disorder
-> Spirit-wrought regeneration
-> faith and repentance
-> progressive renewal of mind and loves
-> sanctification
-> glorified ordered communion.

Noetic renewal remains dependent upon divine grace and does not imply exhaustive epistemic correctness in the present life.

## 12. Formal constraints

**NC-1:** K_noetic(P) != Q(P).

**NC-2:** K_aff(P) != Q(P).

**NC-3:** inherited Adamic condition does not, by definition alone, establish personal culpability for Adam's intention.

**NC-4:** absence of inherited personal guilt does not imply epistemic neutrality, moral innocence after actual sin, or autonomous capacity to establish righteousness.

**NC-5:** personal guilt requires a personally attributable culpable relation to truth, good, command, intention, or action.

**NC-6:** noetic impairment can affect degree of accountability without automatically eliminating accountability.

**NC-7:** divine judgment must track moral truth about the actual person rather than treating inherited condition and personal action as interchangeable predicates.

## 13. First-sin contrast

Adam's first sin must not be explained through K_noetic or K_aff. The pre-Fall sequence is instead: good mutable agent -> temptation/presentation -> consideration -> valuation -> culpable disordered assent/willing -> rejection of communion -> enacted rebellion -> curse. See `temptation-first-sin-boundary.md`.

For fallen descendants, inherited noetic and affective disorder conditions valuation before personally attributable assent and rebellion. The agency categories remain distinguishable even though the starting dispositions differ.

## 15. Current synthesis

The model now distinguishes four stages:

**Adamic representative failure**
-> **inherited corporate judicial condition**
-> **noetic/affective/relational corruption**
-> **personally attributable voluntary rebellion**
-> **individual culpability**

The middle terms explain why fallen human agency is universally disordered without making inherited disorder itself identical to personal culpability.

The central proposition is:

> The noetic effects of the curse condition how fallen persons know, value, interpret, and desire; individual culpability concerns what the real moral agent personally does in and through that condition with the truth and good apprehensible to that agent.

This distinction shall govern subsequent Romans 5, agency, judgment, and accountability analysis.
