# Temptation, Self-Interest, and the First-Sin Boundary

Status: Research draft
Work package: WP-BWM-0028
Date: 2026-09-25

## Purpose

Formalize the category boundary between temptation and sin and apply it to Adam's first rebellion. The model must explain first sin without importing post-Fall noetic corruption into pre-Fall Adam and without treating mere consideration of disobedience as culpable rebellion.

## 1. Starting condition

Adam begins as a good, image-bearing moral agent in ordered communion.

His created capacities are goods:

- selfhood;
- natural self-love;
- rational apprehension;
- deliberation;
- valuation;
- willing;
- stewardship;
- relationship with God and neighbor;
- capacity to pursue perceived good.

No inherited K_noetic or K_aff explains Adam's first sin because those are consequences of the Fall.

## 2. Self-love and self-interest

### Natural self-love

Natural self-love is the creature's orientation toward his own perceived preservation, good, happiness, flourishing, security, fulfillment, and life.

It is not evil in itself.

### Ordinary self-interest

Self-interest is action or deliberation concerning a perceived good of the self. It may be morally ordered.

Therefore:

SelfInterest(P) does not entail Sin(P).

### Autonomous self-interest

Autonomous self-interest is the elevation of a perceived self-good into a governing criterion over God's word, rightful authority, and loving communion with God and neighbor.

This is the morally relevant transition.

## 3. Temptation is not sin

The model distinguishes:

### T0: Presentation

A possible course contrary to God's command is presented or becomes cognitively available.

No sin follows merely from presentation.

### T1: Consideration / temptation

The agent apprehends the alternative and considers its apparent benefit.

The proposition "I could disobey" or "this appears desirable" enters deliberation.

Temptation or consideration is not by itself sin.

Canonical control: Christ is tempted yet without sin (Hebrews 4:15).

### T2: Valuation

The agent evaluates the proposed alternative relative to perceived goods.

Valuation requires care. Recognition that an object or outcome has some attractive property is not yet equivalent to morally preferring it over God.

### T3: Disordered assent / dwelling

The agent begins to embrace, cultivate, or dwell upon the alternative as a good to be possessed contrary to God's ordering of the good.

Here the alternative is no longer merely apprehended. The agent's own valuation begins to reorder the good.

### T4: Volitional rejection of communion

The agent wills the perceived self-good over faithful loving communion with God.

This is the decisive moral rupture.

### T5: External enactment

The disordered willing is embodied in outward action.

External enactment is sin, but sin need not wait for bodily execution if the agent has already culpably assented and willed rebellion.

## 4. Proposed sin boundary

The working boundary is:

Presentation -> consideration -> morally neutral/ordered evaluation
[temptation / deliberation]

then, if the agent crosses the boundary:

disordered assent/dwelling -> volitional preference of autonomous self-good over communion -> enactment
[sin]

The James 1 pressure test refines this boundary: duration or mere recurrence is not the criterion. T3 is culpable conception/cultivation/embrace of the disordered good, and T4 is the will's preference for that good contrary to God's order. A persistent resisted temptation need not be sin, while a brief but fully willed evil intention may be.

Refined proposition:

> Temptation is the presentation or apprehension of a possible disobedient good and may include experienced attraction without itself constituting sin. Sin begins when the moral agent culpably conceives, embraces, or assents to the disordered good such that the agent's own will prefers that good contrary to God's rightful ordering, whether or not outward enactment has yet occurred.

See `james-1-temptation-pressure-test.md`.

## 5. Adamic first-sin model

Adam's first sin can now be represented without prior corruption:

ordered communion
-> presentation of alternative
-> consideration of perceived creaturely/self-good
-> valuation
-> autonomous self-interest becomes governing
-> disordered assent/willing
-> rejection of loving communion and God's word
-> external disobedience
-> guilt and curse.

The morally decisive event is not the existence of self-love, rational deliberation, or perceived attractiveness.

It is the creature's voluntary reordering of the good.

## 6. Definition of the Fall

> The Fall is Adam's voluntary reordering of the good, in which perceived self-good became determinative over God's word and loving communion, transforming rightly ordered self-love into autonomous self-rule.

This formulation makes rebellion Adam's own act without requiring a positive substance called evil to be inserted into him.

## 7. Privation/disorder account

The first sin uses good created capacities in a disordered relation:

selfhood: good
self-love: good
reason: good
deliberation: good
valuation: good
will: good
created object/good: good in its proper order
communion: good

Sin consists in the culpable misordering of those goods.

Therefore the model does not require:

God creates evil substance -> Adam receives evil -> Adam sins.

Instead:

God creates good agent and goods
-> alternative is apprehensible
-> agent evaluates
-> agent culpably reorders perceived good
-> rebellion obtains.

This is consistent with the privation/disorder account while preserving genuine agency.

## 8. Divine-authorship implication

The first-sin origination problem becomes more precise.

The Father decrees and ordains the history including Adam's Fall.

Adam's created faculties and their existence are good.

The morally sinful predicate enters at the level of Adam's own disordered assent and willing, where Adam elevates autonomous self-interest over loving communion and divine authority.

Therefore:

Divine creation of faculties != creation of sinful moral defect.

Divine ordination of the event != Adam's disordered intention.

Presentation of a possible alternative != divine production of sinful assent.

The remaining metaphysical question is how a good but mutable creature's genuine deliberation can terminate in disordered assent within a decretively certain history. The present model locates the transition without pretending to exhaust the metaphysics.

## 9. Canonical controls

### Genesis 3:1-7

Required analysis:
- presentation and reframing of God's command;
- perceived goods associated with the tree;
- movement from perception/valuation to taking/eating;
- Adam's participation;
- whether the narrative permits a distinction between consideration and culpable assent before enactment.

### Hebrews 4:15

Controls the category distinction: temptation does not entail sin.

### James 1:13-15

Provides a progression from temptation/enticement through desire, conception, sin, and death. It must be handled carefully because James also says God tempts no one with evil.

### Matthew 5:21-30

Controls against defining sin only as bodily execution. Inward hatred/lust can already be morally culpable.

### 1 John 2:15-17

Potential control for disordered desire and love of the world, to be used without anachronistically reading post-Fall categories directly into pre-Fall Adam.

## 10. Category discriminations

| Category | Moral status in model |
|---|---|
| Alternative is presented | Not sin |
| Agent recognizes possibility of disobedience | Not sin |
| Agent experiences temptation | Not sin |
| Agent recognizes apparent attractiveness | Not necessarily sin |
| Agent deliberates about alternative | Not necessarily sin |
| Agent culpably dwells on/embraces disordered good | Sin boundary begins |
| Agent wills autonomous self-good over communion | Sin |
| Agent externally performs rebellion | Sin enacted |
| Agent persists/rationalizes/hardens | Further culpable disorder |

## 11. Relation to noetic effects

Adam's sequence must not be explained by K_noetic.

Post-Fall descendants differ:

Adam:
good mutable agent -> temptation -> deliberation -> disordered assent -> rebellion -> curse.

Fallen descendant:
inherited K_noetic/K_aff -> temptation/encounter with perceived goods -> already disordered orientation conditions valuation -> personally attributable assent/willing -> rebellion -> guilt -> possible further darkening.

Thus the same agency categories operate in both cases while the starting dispositions differ.

## 12. Relation to dispositional necessity

For Adam:

Fall is not □L.
Fall is □D within the actual decree.
Fall is not explained by □K because K does not yet exist.

For fallen morally accountable descendants:

K conditions valuation and willing such that personal sin is proposed as □K, while the sinful act remains genuinely voluntary.

This sharpens the modal distinction between first sin and subsequent fallen sin.

## 13. No-smuggling constraints

1. Do not call natural self-love sinful.
2. Do not call every form of self-interest sinful.
3. Do not identify temptation with sin.
4. Do not define sin only as external bodily action.
5. Do not import post-Fall noetic corruption into Adam before the Fall.
6. Do not treat the attractiveness of a created good as itself evil.
7. Do not infer that considering a contrary possibility is culpable merely because the contrary act would be sinful.
8. Do not locate the sin boundary later than culpable inward assent/willing.
9. Do not claim that this taxonomy fully explains the metaphysics of first-sin origination.
10. Do not allow divine decree to erase Adam's personal valuation and willing.

## 14. Current disposition

The first-sin problem is narrowed from "Where did evil come from in a good Adam?" to:

> How did a good but mutable moral agent, within a decretively certain history, culpably reorder genuine goods by elevating autonomous self-interest over loving communion with God?

The model's answer at the moral-agency level is:

> Adam sinned when his own deliberation crossed from consideration of a possible alternative into culpable disordered assent and willing, preferring perceived self-good on autonomous terms over God's word and loving communion.

The deeper metaphysics of that transition remains OPEN.
