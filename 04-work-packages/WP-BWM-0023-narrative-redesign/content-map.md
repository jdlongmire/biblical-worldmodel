## Bridge-copy briefs

### The Model

Explain that the model asks how an initially created world and its subsequent history relate. Use the existing Adam, Cana, and game-world examples as orientation. Avoid compressing DFM, PFH, CHFM, cosmology, and biology into a list of acronyms on the front door.

### The Evidence

Explain that BWM begins with observations but does not call the entire historical reconstruction an observation. Use one concrete example, then route readers to the evidence checklist and domain pages.

### The Tests

Explain that a test matters when competing explanations make different expectations, and that an unresolved problem remains visible rather than being relabeled as support. Link to objections, open problems, falsifiers, and canonical research records.

## Editorial risks to resolve

- The phrase “The Tests” could imply a completed test suite. The page should say “predictions, falsifiers, and ongoing research” until the evidence register supports stronger wording.
- The calculated-age argument must not be presented as proving a young Earth or any specific chronology by itself.
- The video analogy must remain an illustration of an inference problem, not a substitute for domain-specific evidence.
- The three-path architecture must preserve the existing skeptic/reviewer and open-problems routes rather than hide them under a more promotional design.
