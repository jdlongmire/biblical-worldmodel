# WP-BWM-0010 — The Story Visual Narrative

**Status:** active. Renumbered from WP-BWM-0007 on 2026-09-12 -- that ID collided with WP-BWM-0007-ross-dfm-harvest (registered earlier the same day, with its own tracking issue). Scope unchanged.  
**Purpose:** design, generate, review, decompose, and integrate a coherent sequence of graphics that visually teaches the approved Tier 1 Biblical WorldModel story.

## Objective

Create a visual narrative for **The Story** in which each graphic communicates one major idea quickly, works as a standalone asset, and supports the webpage rather than attempting to reproduce the entire argument inside a text-heavy infographic.

The sequence should help a first-time reader move from **God's Word / God's world** through mature creation, calculated age versus actual history, real subsequent history, and finally into the deeper BWM methodology.

## Governing design principle

> **One graphic. One mental model. One step in the story.**

The webpage carries the argument. The graphic carries the picture the reader should remember.

Primary Story graphics should normally be understandable within roughly five seconds. Explanatory prose belongs primarily in the page around the graphic rather than inside the image.

## Primary visual sequence

### G1 — God's Word. God's World.

**Purpose:** establish the opening epistemic frame.

**Core visual:** Scripture paired with the created world, visually joined rather than presented as competing realities.

**Primary text:**

> **God's Word. God's World.**

Supporting ideas may include:

- one Creator;
- one reality;
- Scripture as revealed truth;
- nature as real evidence to be understood;
- Scripture interprets nature;
- nature informs/tests our understanding of how God's world works.

Avoid turning this graphic into a full methodology chart.

**Current concept:** first generated concept approved directionally by JD on 2026-09-12. The interpretation and composition are accepted as the baseline direction; final production asset remains subject to review and repository capture.

### G2 — Adam: Mature From the Beginning

**Purpose:** establish the concrete biblical idea that measurable maturity need not entail elapsed developmental history.

**Core visual:** contrast a created adult state with the normally expected infancy → childhood → adulthood pathway.

Because BWM visual policy avoids unnecessary human depiction in sensitive biblical contexts, prefer symbolic/anatomical/timeline treatment over a photorealistic depiction of Adam unless specifically dispositioned otherwise.

**Remembered idea:**

> Adult maturity can be real even when the normal years of development did not occur.

### G3 — The Wine at Cana

**Purpose:** provide a second biblical example with an especially clear process-history contrast.

**Core visual:**

```text
water → divine act → wine

versus

grapes → harvest → crushing → fermentation → wine
```

No human depiction of Christ.

**Remembered idea:**

> The wine was real. The normal production history had not happened.

### G4 — The Generated World Thought Experiment

**Purpose:** serve as the flagship modern analogy for the Story.

**Core visual:** a newly generated virtual valley already containing mountains, river, trees, and terrain; a scientist inside the world measures erosion and calculates a long virtual age.

**Key question:**

> **How long would it take?**

is not always the same question as:

> **How long has it been here?**

The graphic must make clear that BWM is using an analogy, not claiming the universe is literally a computer simulation.

### G5 — How Much Was There at the Beginning?

**Purpose:** introduce starting-state comparison fairly.

**Core visual:** two histories reaching the present from different degrees of initial development.

```text
Less-developed starting state ───── long development ─────► Today

Created functional state ── real subsequent history ──────► Today
```

Avoid prejudicial labels. Both sides receive a starting state. The difference is the degree and character of structure present at the beginning.

**Remembered idea:**

> Every account starts with something. How much is already there when the history begins?

### G6 — What Happened After Creation?

**Purpose:** prevent DFM from being mistaken for the claim that everything observed today was simply created in place.

**Core visual:**

```text
CREATION
   │
   ├── real history
   ├── Fall
   ├── generations
   ├── Flood
   ├── post-Flood change
   └────────────────────► WORLD TODAY
```

**Core questions:**

> **What was there at the beginning?**  
> **What happened afterward?**

This visual provides the narrative bridge from DFM concepts toward CHFM/Earth-history work without requiring those programme names in Tier 1.

### G7 — From What We See to What We Think Happened

**Purpose:** provide a plain-language bridge into the epistemic hierarchy.

**Core visual:**

```text
WHAT WE SEE
    ↓
WHAT WE MEASURE
    ↓
HOW WE THINK IT WORKS
    ↓
OUR MODEL OF THE PAST
    ↓
OUR LARGER STORY
```

The full L0–L6 epistemic hierarchy belongs in Tier 2/3 and WP-BWM-0009 (renumbered from WP-BWM-0006). This Story graphic should remain plain.

**Remembered idea:**

> Evidence constrains the story. Moving from what we see now to what happened before requires interpretation.

## Format strategy

### Mobile-first

Primary composition should be designed first for phone use.

Preferred starting formats:

- **4:5 portrait** for major explanatory cards;
- **1:1** where the concept is naturally compact;
- derive **16:9 / wide desktop variants** only when the composition benefits from additional horizontal space.

Do not design the primary concept as a wide poster and then merely shrink it for mobile.

### Responsive decomposition

Where a graphic contains separable components, retain/develop reusable assets rather than relying only on one flattened poster. Candidate components include:

- background/scene;
- iconography;
- labels/headlines;
- arrows/process markers;
- callout cards;
- mobile/desktop crops.

ThinxAI may integrate decomposed assets into responsive site components where that gives better readability than a single image.

## Visual language

Maintain continuity with the existing BWM site:

- restrained navy/blue/cream palette with natural earth accents;
- strong serif display typography where appropriate;
- clean sans-serif supporting text;
- natural-world imagery rather than generic technology stock imagery;
- high contrast and mobile readability;
- visual calm rather than argumentative or sensational presentation;
- avoid clutter, dense legends, and poster-like walls of text.

The graphics should feel like parts of one visual system even when their subject matter differs.

## Theological and representational constraints

- No human depiction of Christ.
- Prefer symbolic treatment for miracles involving Christ.
- Scripture should be represented respectfully and not as decorative pseudo-text where legibility matters.
- Avoid imagery that implies the universe is literally a simulation when illustrating the generated-world analogy.
- Avoid visual caricatures of conventional scientific or naturalistic models.
- Do not visually imply that an analogy itself constitutes proof.

## Per-graphic specification

Before generating each graphic, record at minimum:

1. Graphic ID and title.
2. Single teaching objective.
3. One remembered idea.
4. Exact text permitted in the graphic.
5. Visual composition.
6. What must not be implied.
7. Mobile composition.
8. Desktop adaptation, if needed.
9. Accessibility/alt-text intent.
10. Operator disposition after review.

This prevents image generation from deciding the conceptual architecture implicitly.

## Accessibility

Every public graphic requires:

- meaningful alt text when the image carries information;
- surrounding HTML text that carries the essential argument independently of the image;
- adequate contrast;
- text large enough to read on mobile without pinch zoom where practical;
- no critical information encoded only by color.

The Story must remain understandable if images fail to load.
