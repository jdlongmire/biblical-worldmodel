**hook**
on-screen: God gave us His Word. God also gave us His world.
narration: Christians believe the Bible tells us true things about the world God made. We can also study that world for ourselves — telescopes, rocks and fossils, living things, the rules by which nature normally works. So we have God's Word, and we have God's world. They come from the same God. The question is how to understand them together.

**tension**
on-screen: Most scientists say billions of years. The Bible seems to say something shorter.
narration: Most scientists today say the universe is about thirteen point eight billion years old, and Earth about four and a half billion. The Bible seems to give us a much shorter story of creation and early history. Many Christians wonder what to do with that difference. Do we change the way we read the Bible? Do we reject what scientists see? Maybe those aren't our only choices. There's another question worth asking first.
image: none — plain ground, springs the question in

**adam**
on-screen: Think about Adam.
narration: The Bible says God made the first man, Adam, as a grown man. Adam did not begin as a baby. Imagine a doctor meeting Adam the next day, with no idea God just made him. The doctor examines his bones, teeth, muscles, organs — everything says "grown man." That's a good observation. But if the doctor concludes this man spent years growing up, that would be wrong. Adam's body was mature. The years of growth had never happened. What the doctor saw that day was real. The past he expected was not.
image: v2-g02-adam-maturity.png (WP-BWM-0010, concepts-v2)

**cana**
on-screen: Think about the wine Jesus made.
narration: The Gospel of John tells us Jesus turned water into wine at a wedding. Normally wine has a history — grapes grow, ripen, get picked and crushed, the juice ferments. That didn't happen to the wine Jesus made. It was real wine. Someone studying it could have measured what it was like. But those features didn't come from the normal history of growing grapes and making wine. Again — what was there was real. The usual history behind it had not happened.
image: v2-g03-cana-wine.png (WP-BWM-0010, concepts-v2)

**game**
on-screen: Imagine a new video-game world.
narration: Imagine starting a large video game. The computer builds a world by following rules — mountains, forests, rivers, caves, valleys. You enter one second after the world appears. There's a river flowing through a deep valley. Now imagine you're a scientist inside the game. You study the river, learn how fast water wears away rock, measure the valley, and work backward — you calculate the river would need ten thousand years of game time to carve that valley. Your math could be right. But there's one question it can't answer: did the river actually carve the valley? Maybe it did. Or maybe the computer made the river and valley together when the game began. The valley could be one second old. *Working backward can tell us how long something would take to form. It cannot always tell us whether it actually formed that way.* That doesn't prove Earth is young. It does mean a calculated age and an actual history aren't automatically the same thing.
image: v2-g04-useful-analogy.png (WP-BWM-0010, concepts-v2)

**afterward**
on-screen: Then the game keeps going.
narration: After the game world begins, things start happening inside it. Rivers keep cutting into their banks, trees grow, storms come, rocks fall, volcanoes erupt. Those become part of the game's history. So if you enter much later, you have a problem: some things were there when the game began, other things happened afterward — how can you tell which is which? You can't simply assume everything you see was there from the start. You also can't assume everything was made by the processes happening now. You have to investigate. We think the same question is worth asking about the real world.
image: v2-g06-real-history-follows-creation.png (WP-BWM-0010, concepts-v2) — new, previously plain ground

**beginning**
on-screen: How much was already there when God made the world?
narration: Every story about the universe has to start with something. The usual scientific story begins with a universe that already has matter, energy, and dependable rules, then a very long history follows. Genesis begins differently — God creates a world already ready to work. Light. Land and water. Plants and animals that can reproduce. The sun, moon, and stars doing their work. Human beings beginning as living people. The two stories may not begin at the same place. That could explain part of the disagreement about time. If God made a world already ready to work, working backward may go too far — it may pass the real beginning. Again, Adam gives us the simple picture: a doctor working backward from his adult body would describe years of growth that never happened.
image: v2-g05-starting-conditions-matter.png (WP-BWM-0010, concepts-v2)

**flood**
on-screen: But the world did have a real history.
narration: This doesn't mean everything we see was made at the beginning. After God created the world, real things happened — people lived, animals reproduced, rivers flowed, rock wore away, mud and sand moved. The Bible also tells us about Noah — his family and the animals with him were saved through a great Flood that covered the earth. If the Flood happened on the scale the Bible describes, it would have changed the ground in major ways. That gives us two questions worth investigating: what did the Flood do to the earth, and how can we tell the difference between what was there from the beginning and what happened later?
image: v2-g07-flood-global-reset.png (WP-BWM-0010, concepts-v2)

**dating**
on-screen: What about scientific dating?
narration: Scientists have many ways of studying the past. One important method looks at atoms in rocks — some kinds slowly change into other kinds, and scientists measure how much has changed to calculate an age. The changes are real. The measurements are real. The calculation can be correct. But the same basic question remains: what was there when the rock began? If we know the starting point, working backward can tell us a great deal. If we assume the wrong starting point, the calculation may describe a history that didn't actually happen. That doesn't make scientific dating worthless — it tells us exactly what we need to investigate. What do we know from measurement? What are we assuming about the beginning? What happened afterward? And does the calculated history match the history that really happened?
image: v2-g08-scientific-dating.png (WP-BWM-0010, concepts-v2)

**humility**
on-screen: We do not have every answer.
narration: We don't claim to have solved every problem. There are hard questions about rocks, fossils, dating, heat, living things, and the stars. Some of our ideas may turn out to be wrong — if they do, we should say so. We shouldn't reject a real observation because it's difficult for us. We should also be careful not to confuse an explanation of an observation with the observation itself. The Bible is our authority. Our explanations of nature can improve. That's why this is an ongoing project.
image: v2-g09-epistemic-posture.png (WP-BWM-0010, concepts-v2) — new, previously plain ground

**invitation**
on-screen: How much was already there? What happened after that?
narration: The basic idea is simple. God made the world. God also gave us His Word. The Bible tells us things about the beginning that no human being was there to watch. The world around us gives us things we can observe and measure today. We want to understand both together. That brings us to two questions: how much was already there when God made the world, and what happened after that? Those questions are the doorway into this project. We invite you to explore them with us.

## Promo

**short (~20 words):** Adam was made grown. Jesus made wine with no vineyard behind it. What if a calculated age and an actual history aren't the same thing?

**medium (~50 words):** A doctor examining Adam the day after creation would see a grown man — and be wrong about how he got there. The Biblical WorldModel asks a simple question about starting points, working backward, and what scientific dating can and can't tell us about the past.

**long (~500 words):**

Most scientists today say the universe is about 13.8 billion years old, and Earth about 4.5 billion. The Bible seems to tell a much shorter story. Many Christians think their only options are to reinterpret Scripture or reject what scientists see. There's a question worth asking first.

Think about Adam. The Bible says God made him as a grown man — not a baby. A doctor examining Adam the next day would find every sign of a mature adult: bones, teeth, muscles, organs. If that doctor concluded Adam had spent decades growing up, the doctor would be wrong. What was there was real. The years of growth the doctor inferred had never happened.

The same pattern shows up when Jesus turns water into wine at Cana. Real wine, with none of the years a vineyard normally takes.

Now imagine a video game world that boots up already finished — mountains, rivers, valleys already carved. A scientist inside the game measures the river's erosion rate, calculates the valley needed ten thousand years to form, and the math is correct. But the math can't answer whether the valley actually took ten thousand years, or was there the instant the world loaded. Working backward tells you how long something *would take*. It doesn't always tell you whether it *actually took that long*.

That's the whole idea, applied to Genesis: if God made a world already ready to work, calculating backward from what we observe today may reach past the real beginning — the same way the doctor's calculation reached past Adam's real beginning.

This doesn't mean nothing has a real history. Real things happened after creation — people lived, rivers flowed, and the Bible tells us a real Flood reshaped the earth. The question becomes: what was there from the start, and what happened afterward? Scientific dating faces the same question radiometric methods measure real decay, but the calculated age still depends on assumptions about the starting point. Get the starting point right, and the method tells you a great deal. Assume the wrong one, and you get a real calculation describing a history that didn't happen.

We don't claim to have every answer. Some of our ideas may be wrong, and if they are, we'll say so. The Bible is our authority; our explanations of nature can improve. That's the whole invitation: how much was already there when God made the world, and what happened after that? Explore both questions with us at oddXian.com.
