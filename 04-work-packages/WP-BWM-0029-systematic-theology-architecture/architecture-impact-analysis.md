# Architecture Impact Analysis: WP-BWM-0013 to WP-BWM-0029

Status: Candidate analysis
Date: 2026-09-25

## Baseline

WP-BWM-0013 established the accepted four-category intellectual architecture:

Foundations | Historical Frameworks | World Domains | Integration.

WP-BWM-0029 does not reject those distinctions. It identifies a missing doctrinal layer.

## Finding

The proposed Systematic Theology category is **additive and clarifying**, not a replacement for the responsibilities assigned to the other four categories.

The principal architectural defect exposed by WP-BWM-0020 and WP-BWM-0028 is that doctrinal synthesis currently has no first-class owner. The Covenant and Redemptive History World Domain consequently risks carrying both doctrine and historical tracing, while Anthropology risks mixing theological anthropology with empirical anthropology.

## Changes required if accepted

1. Update `bwm-component-architecture.md` from four to five categories.
2. Add Systematic Theology jurisdiction and covenant-communion organizing motif.
3. Add the eight ST modules.
4. Narrow Covenant and Redemptive History World Domain to historical tracing.
5. Explicitly separate Theological Anthropology from Anthropology World Domain.
6. Update Integration rules to accept doctrinal constraints/status from ST and return tensions for explicit re-exegesis.
7. Add Systematic Theology to cross-domain invariants/dependency maps where appropriate.
8. Create canonical ST directory only after acceptance.
9. Map existing accepted artifacts before physical movement.
10. Preserve WP-BWM-0013 as provenance for the prior accepted architecture.

## What does not change

- Repository `00`-`06` lifecycle structure.
- Foundations ownership of epistemology/hermeneutics/ontology interfaces.
- DFM/PFH/CHFM classification as Historical Frameworks.
- Existing empirical World Domains.
- Integration as explicit cross-category consistency layer.
- External source authority of TRT/LRT and CHFM research repositories.
- Work-package promotion/provenance rules.

## Compatibility with WP-BWM-0013 rules

The proposal preserves the existing rules that Foundations are not World Domains, Historical Frameworks are not subject-matter domains, frameworks may span domains, domains may consume multiple frameworks, research remains in WPs until accepted, canonical architecture belongs under systems architecture, and cross-cutting relations use interfaces/invariants.

The necessary amendment is that those rules gain a fifth category and explicit ST ownership.

## Risk analysis

### Risk: Systematic Theology becomes a catch-all

Mitigation: primary ownership is question-based; historical, empirical, foundational, and integration questions retain their existing owners.

### Risk: Covenant/communion motif becomes a controlling hermeneutic over Scripture

Mitigation: Foundations retain Scripture-primary authority and canonical hermeneutics. Covenant/communion is a synthesis subject to canonical correction.

### Risk: doctrine becomes insulated from evidence

Mitigation: Integration explicitly routes tensions back to ST for re-exegesis/revision. Domain models cannot silently rewrite doctrine, but doctrine is not immune from falsification.

### Risk: duplicate doctrinal sources

Mitigation: canonical ST artifact is source of doctrinal synthesis; historical/domain artifacts consume it through interfaces.

### Risk: premature promotion of WP-BWM-0028 hypotheses

Mitigation: migration map marks claim status. H1 and other OPEN claims remain in WP-BWM-0028.

## Recommendation candidate

Architecturally, the five-category model resolves a real ownership gap with limited disruption to the accepted WP-BWM-0013 structure. Implementation should occur only after Principal Operator acceptance of the category, module taxonomy, and migration rules.
