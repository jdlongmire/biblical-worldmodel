# Covenant-Communion Taxonomy

Status: Candidate
Work package: WP-BWM-0029
Date: 2026-09-25

## Purpose

Define the organizing vocabulary, module relationships, conventional-loci crosswalk, and navigation rules for the proposed BWM Systematic Theology layer.

## Governing motif

**Covenant is structural. Communion is teleological.**

Covenant identifies God's established relational order: authority, obligations, promises, offices, signs, sanctions, administrations, and means.

Communion identifies the relational end: rightly ordered fellowship with God, neighbor, self, and creation under God's lordship.

Covenant and communion are not competing categories. Covenant orders communion; communion is the relational good toward which covenant order is directed.

## Core vocabulary

### Covenant

A divinely established relational order in which God sovereignly defines the terms, obligations, promises, offices, signs, sanctions, and means governing creaturely relationship to him and, derivatively, to neighbor and creation.

### Communion

Rightly ordered personal fellowship in which the creature receives God as God, self as creature, neighbor as fellow image-bearer, and creation as entrusted domain.

### Ordered love

The moral order in which God is loved supremely, self-love remains derivative and creaturely, neighbor is loved as self, and creation is stewarded under divine authority.

### Rupture

The culpable rejection or disordering of covenant communion through unbelief, lawlessness, or autonomous self-interest.

### Curse

The judicial consequence and condition following covenant rupture, including those effects established by Scripture and distinguished from later model-specific hypotheses.

### Redemption

The Son's effectual accomplishment of salvation and restoration for the people the Father gives him.

### Restoration

The Spirit-applied renewal by which the redeemed are regenerated, reconciled, sanctified, preserved, and increasingly reordered toward communion.

### Consummation

The final state in which judgment is complete, curse is removed, creation is renewed, and redeemed communion is perfected.

## Module relationships

### ST-1 God and Triune Purpose

Precedes every creaturely relation. Supplies doctrine of God, Trinity, decree, ordination, and providence.

Primary question: Who is the God who creates, covenants, judges, redeems, and consummates?

### ST-2 Creation and Covenant Communion

Establishes created persons, goods, relations, authority, agency, stewardship, and original ordered communion.

Primary question: What is the good created order of relationship?

### ST-3 Rupture, Sin, and Curse

Explains temptation, culpable disorder, Fall, curse, noetic/affective effects, and guilt questions.

Primary question: What is rebellion and what does it do to covenant communion?

### ST-4 Providence and Moral Agency

Cross-cuts ST-2 through ST-8.

Primary question: How do divine sovereignty, creaturely agency, intention, responsibility, judgment, and modal necessity cohere?

### ST-5 Covenant Redemption

Owns the objective redemptive work centered in Christ.

Primary question: What does the Father purpose and the Son accomplish for the elect?

### ST-6 Spirit-wrought Restoration

Owns application and progressive restoration.

Primary question: How is accomplished redemption applied to persons and communion restored?

### ST-7 Church, Bride, and Covenant People

Owns the corporate redeemed community.

Primary question: What people does redemption create and how do they live in covenant communion?

### ST-8 Judgment and Consummated Communion

Owns terminal judgment, resurrection, glorification, perfected love, New Creation, and final communion.

Primary question: What is the final state toward which the decreed history moves?

## Navigation rules

1. Navigate by the theological question first, not by a favorite conventional locus.
2. Assign one canonical primary owner to each doctrinal synthesis.
3. Use conventional-loci tags/cross-references for discovery.
4. Use interfaces rather than duplicate doctrinal text in Historical Frameworks or World Domains.
5. Keep open research in work packages.
6. Preserve scriptural warrant and confidence/status with promoted claims.
7. Keep covenant/communion subordinate to canonical warrant.
8. Do not force every biblical relationship into the same covenant administration.
9. Do not use "communion" to erase judgment, authority, holiness, or asymmetry between Creator and creature.
10. Do not use "covenant" to reduce personal fellowship to legal mechanism.

## Key relational distinctions

### Creator and creature

Communion never erases ontological distinction. God remains Creator and Lord; the creature remains derivative and dependent.

### Authority and love

Divine authority and loving communion are not opposites. Rightly ordered communion includes God's rightful lordship.

### Law and communion

Law articulates and governs moral order. It is not an arbitrary layer detached from relationship.

### Self and neighbor

Natural self-love is presupposed as a created good. Neighbor-love orders the self toward fellow image-bearers under supreme love for God.

### Justice and mercy

Justice truthfully judges rupture. Mercy is sovereign restoration granted to the guilty and is not owed.

### Individual and corporate

Systematic Theology must preserve both genuine individual personhood/culpability and corporate covenantal solidarity.

### Objective redemption and subjective application

ST-5 owns the Son's accomplished work. ST-6 owns the Spirit's application. The distinction does not divide the one divine purpose.

## Status vocabulary

Promoted ST claims should carry one of:

- **Explicit**: directly stated by Scripture.
- **Canonical synthesis**: derived from multiple scriptural claims.
- **Logical entailment**: follows from accepted premises by valid inference.
- **Theological inference**: plausible synthesis exceeding direct statement.
- **Active hypothesis**: under investigation.
- **Open problem**: unresolved tension/question.

These labels prevent architectural placement from being mistaken for epistemic certainty.

## Governing summary

Systematic Theology is organized as the coherent canonical account of the triune God's purpose to create and order covenant communion, the creature's culpable rupture of that communion, God's righteous judgment and sovereign redemption, the Spirit's restoration of the redeemed, and the final consummation of communion to the glory of God.
