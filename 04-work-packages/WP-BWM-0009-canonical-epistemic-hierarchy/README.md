## Proposed canonical levels

Number levels bottom-up so objections and tests can be classified compactly:

- **L0 — Observation**
- **L1 — Prediction / Test**
- **L2 — Auxiliary Hypothesis**
- **L3 — Model / Theory**
- **L4 — Research Programme**
- **L5 — WorldModel**
- **L6 — Worldview**

Working rule:

> **An observation, test result, or argument bears first upon the specific claim from which the tested expectation follows. Evidential consequences propagate to higher levels according to demonstrated dependency.**

A failed auxiliary hypothesis does not automatically falsify its parent model, research programme, WorldModel, or worldview. Repeated or indispensable lower-level failures may create cumulative evidential pressure on higher levels.

The same rule applies symmetrically to competing frameworks.

## Operator governance

### Operator

An **Operator** is an authorized human steward empowered to disposition BWM artifacts, interpretations, models, methodological changes, or canonical status within an explicitly assigned scope.

Operator authority is human governance authority. AI systems may research, draft, test, classify, critique, and recommend. AI systems do not independently confer canonical status.

### Principal Operator

**J. D. Longmire is the Principal Operator for the Biblical WorldModel.**

The Principal Operator holds BWM-wide disposition authority, including final disposition where authority has not been delegated and authority to resolve conflicts among scoped dispositions.

### Collaborator Operator

A **Collaborator Operator** is a human collaborator explicitly granted Operator authority over a defined scope, such as:

- a research programme;
- a repository;
- a work package;
- a scientific discipline;
- an artifact class;
- public/editorial content;
- another explicitly documented domain.

Collaborator Operator authority does not imply BWM-wide authority. Scope must be recorded.

Example: a collaborator may be authorized to disposition CHFM geological models without authority to alter BWM's theological or hermeneutical commitments.

### Contributor / Reviewer

A Contributor or Reviewer may propose, analyze, challenge, test, or recommend disposition. A Contributor/Reviewer does not change canonical status unless separately assigned Operator authority for that scope.

## Operator disposition states

Canonicalization and major status changes should use an explicit Operator disposition:

- **ACCEPT** — approve and promote within the Operator's scope.
- **ACCEPT-WITH-CONDITIONS** — approve subject to recorded conditions.
- **REVISE** — return for modification before disposition.
- **HOLD** — preserve without promotion pending additional work.
- **REJECT** — decline adoption.
- **RETIRE** — withdraw an existing canonical claim, model, or artifact from active use while preserving provenance.
- **SUPERSEDE** — replace an existing canonical item with an identified successor while preserving lineage.

A disposition record should identify:

1. artifact or claim;
2. Operator;
3. Operator role and authority scope;
4. disposition;
5. date;
6. conditions or rationale where applicable;
7. successor/predecessor where applicable;
8. commit or immutable provenance reference.

## Validation pass 1 — Dependency testing

Apply the hierarchy to actual BWM objections and open problems. Candidate cases include:

- radiogenic / Flood heat;
- pore-pressure and crustal-motion mechanisms;
- zircon lead and isotope starting states;
- fission tracks and alpha-recoil damage;
- Cardenas Basalt dating;
- Mt. St. Helens dating as a starting-condition-sensitivity case;
- starlight / cosmological maturity;
- other objections from the DFM and CHFM ledgers.

For each case identify the tested claim, level, parent dependencies, severity, and upward consequence.

If the ontology repeatedly cannot identify the actual target level, revise the hierarchy.

## Validation pass 2 — Symmetry testing

Apply the same hierarchy to competing UTE / conventional examples, including where appropriate:

- inflationary models;
- dark-matter candidates;
- abiogenesis pathways;
- geological auxiliary mechanisms;
- evolutionary mechanisms;
- cosmological initial/boundary conditions.

The hierarchy must not give BWM escape routes that would be rejected when used by a competing framework. It must also avoid imposing restrictions on BWM that are not imposed on competing models without an independent reason for the asymmetry.

## Validation pass 3 — Adversarial testing

Invite strong criticism of the hierarchy itself.

Primary challenge to test:

> **Can BWM always protect itself by demoting a failed claim to an auxiliary hypothesis?**

The methodology must answer through dependency and precommitment rather than assertion.

Second challenge, recorded 2026-09-12 on review of the working source (thinx): the hierarchy states that "a major direct contradiction or *sustained cumulative* conflict can challenge a WorldModel interpretation," but names no criterion for how much cumulative conflict crosses that threshold. As written, that is an infinitely adjustable buffer at the WorldModel level -- structurally the same rescue-assumption pattern the auxiliary-hypothesis section warns against, just relocated one level up. Parallel framing:

> **Can BWM always protect itself by inflating the cumulative-conflict threshold required to challenge the WorldModel, the same way an auxiliary hypothesis can be inflated to protect a model?**

This should get the same dependency-and-precommitment treatment as the primary challenge -- a stated, precommitted threshold (even a qualitative one) before the fact, not an assessment made only after a challenge arrives.

### Dependency rule

> **A claim cannot be treated after failure as dispensable if the parent model or programme was previously committed to, or materially dependent upon, that claim.**

### Precommitment rule

Before a severe test where practical, record:

- the claim being tested;
- its hierarchy level;
- the expected result;
- the relevant parent dependencies;
- what failure would mean for the hypothesis/model/programme;
- what competing frameworks predict or permit.

This reduces after-the-fact movement of claims between hierarchy levels.

## Objections-ledger integration

Every objections-ledger entry should ultimately support:

- **Target level:** L0–L6;
- **Target claim:** exact proposition;
- **Evidence:** observation, argument, calculation, or source;
- **Dependency:** parent claims materially dependent on it;
- **Severity:** local, material, major, programme-threatening, or WorldModel-threatening;
- **Status/disposition:** unanswered, partial, answered, retired, revised, rejected, escalated, etc.;
- **Upward consequence:** evidential pressure propagated to parent levels;
- **Precommitment:** where one existed before the test;
- **Operator disposition:** where canonical or programme status changes are made.
