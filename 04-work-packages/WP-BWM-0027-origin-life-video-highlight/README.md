## Governing question
Origin-of-life research has demonstrated chemistry relevant to life. The stronger historical question is whether research has demonstrated a causally continuous, geochemically plausible, unguided trajectory from plausible prebiotic starting conditions to replication, heredity, functional information, translation, and open-ended evolvability.

## The Arrow Problem
```text
prebiotic chemistry → building blocks → functional polymers → replication → heredity → translation → integrated living system
```
The explanatory burden lies in the arrows. Demonstrating phenomena on both sides of an arrow does not explain the historical transition.

The animation's critical transition is the appearance, "one day, by accident," of a replicator. That imports molecular recognition, suitable substrates, templating, copying fidelity, product separation, persistence, repeated replication, heritable variation, and usable free energy. Once replication with heritable variation exists, selection has a substrate on which to operate. Selection does not itself explain the origin of that substrate.

## Model discipline
Stability does not entail replication, heredity, or functional integration. Time supplies opportunities for mechanisms but is not itself the mechanism. Laboratory capability and historical occurrence are distinct propositions.

Experimental intervention must be audited rather than automatically converted into evidence for design. Ask what the investigator supplied: selection/purification, concentration, activation, sequence, catalysts, cycling, replenishment, separation, and transfer.

## Local versus global feasibility
Separate experiments may establish that different conditions permit different transitions. That does not establish one historical environment capable of traversing the whole pathway while preserving products and satisfying each subsequent boundary condition.

> Local chemical feasibility does not establish global historical plausibility.

## Information
BWM analysis must distinguish Shannon/statistical information, sequence specificity, functional information, and semantic/representational information. Design requires a positive inference; naturalistic generation of functional organization also carries a positive burden.

## Revised assessment
The examiners' strongest criticisms concern narrative compression of unresolved transitions, the origin of a capable replicator, polymerization/degradation/interfering-reaction constraints, misuse of large timescales, complementarity versus sequence specificity, investigator-supplied experimental conditions, translation, and the absence of a demonstrated end-to-end pathway.

Examiner claims also require scrutiny. Avoid categorical aqueous-polymerization claims, generalized sequence-space probabilities beyond their experiments, and treating laboratory intervention itself as proof of design.
