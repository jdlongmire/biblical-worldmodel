# Tour Tranche A Audit — TOUR-001 through TOUR-010

Status: primary-literature comparison pass. Dispositions are provisional research states.

## Audit rule

A later experiment counts against a Tour objection only to the extent that it demonstrates the chemistry Tour says is missing under materially relevant conditions. A proposed environmental scenario, model, or possibility argument remains an auxiliary and retains explanatory debt under WP-BWM-0009.

## TOUR-001 — Mixtures, byproducts, purification

**Tour claim:** multi-step prebiotic synthesis commonly produces complex mixtures; purification/separation is often required before subsequent chemistry.

**Primary literature comparison:** Patel et al. (2015) is important because it explicitly attempts a shared reaction network for precursors of ribonucleotides, amino acids and lipids, reducing some incompatibility concerns. Cafferty et al. (2016) is stronger against an absolute purification objection for its specific proto-nucleotide system: melamine/barbituric-acid products form and assemble in crude reaction mixtures.

**Disposition:** RETAIN, NARROW.

The universal claim "prebiotic chemistry always requires purification" is false. The systems burden survives: for each proposed end-to-end pathway, identify which intermediates can proceed in crude mixtures and which require isolation, selective removal, phase separation or environmental sorting.

**Naturalistic auxiliary credit:** Patel's common chemistry is E2 for demonstrated reaction steps, not E3/E4 for historical pathway integration.

## TOUR-002 — Reagent ordering

**Tour claim:** order/timing of reagent addition is often decisive in synthetic chemistry and may be externally imposed in experiments.

**Primary comparison:** Powner/Sutherland-type pathways and Patel's network use staged conditions/reagents. The literature proposes geochemical sequencing, spatially separated environments and environmental cycling as possible natural ordering mechanisms.

**Disposition:** RETAIN.

A laboratory sequence is demonstrated chemistry. Historical ordering remains an additional proposition unless the proposed environment independently supplies the sequence with adequate flux and timing.

**State of proposed environmental sequencing:** generally E0-E2 depending on specific mechanism; do not credit as historical solution without case-specific evidence.

## TOUR-003 — Controlled boundary conditions

**Tour claim:** selected temperature, pH, UV exposure, dehydration/rehydration and atmosphere can carry substantial causal work.

**Primary comparison:** Patel et al. requires UV photochemistry, H2S reductant and Cu photoredox chemistry for key steps. Other nucleotide pathways likewise depend on specified conditions.

**Disposition:** RETAIN.

This is not an objection merely because conditions are controlled. The research question is whether the required condition sequence is independently geochemically plausible and mutually compatible.

## TOUR-004 — Intermediate instability

**Tour claim:** useful intermediates/products may degrade under the conditions or times required for subsequent chemistry.

**Primary comparison:** origin-of-life literature recognizes hydrolysis, degradation and stability as central constraints. Sutherland's own ribonucleotide review describes classical ribose/nucleobase routes as chemically problematic; modern routes bypass some unstable/classically difficult intermediates.

**Disposition:** RETAIN, PATHWAY-SPECIFIC.

A new route can bypass an old instability objection. It does not thereby establish persistence and transport for the new route's intermediates.

## TOUR-005 — Low yield and material throughput

**Tour claim:** sequential low yields can exhaust material and make a pathway implausible.

**Primary comparison:** later work demonstrates high yields for some local steps. Becker et al. (2016) reports ~60% yield for natural N9 purine nucleosides in its pathway; Cafferty et al. reports high crude yields for its noncanonical proto-nucleotides.

**Disposition:** QUALIFY.

"Prebiotic yields are inherently tiny" is rejected. The pathway-wide mass-balance objection survives. Yield must be evaluated across the actual connected network including recycling, side reactions, concentration, transport and losses.

## TOUR-006 — Homochirality

**Tour claim:** ordinary abiotic synthesis tends toward racemic products while biology uses strong homochirality; wrong chirality can interfere with informational polymerization.

**Primary comparison:** the origin of biological homochirality remains an active research problem. Experimental mechanisms for enantioenrichment and chiral amplification exist; reviews explicitly distinguish proof-of-concept chemistry from a complete prebiotic account.

**Disposition:** RETAIN.

Reject categorical language that no natural enantioenrichment mechanism exists. Do not credit existence of enrichment mechanisms as an E4 historical explanation. Required audit: starting ee, amplification, achievable ee, compatibility with monomer synthesis/polymerization, racemization rate and geochemical source.

## TOUR-007 — Ribose/carbohydrate selectivity

**Tour claim:** formose-type carbohydrate chemistry generates complex mixtures and ribose is a difficult/stability-limited target.

**Primary comparison:** Sutherland explicitly agrees that the classical route of making ribose then condensing it with bases is fraught with difficulty. Powner et al. (2009) bypasses free-ribose-plus-base assembly by building activated pyrimidine ribonucleotides through a different route. Later RAO/cyanosulfidic chemistry further changes the problem.

**Disposition:** RETAIN WITH ROUTE DISTINCTION.

Tour's critique remains strong against naive formose -> purified ribose -> canonical nucleoside narratives. It cannot be generalized to show that every modern ribonucleotide route requires free ribose or fails for the same reason.

## TOUR-008 — Nucleotide activation, regioselectivity and stereochemistry

**Tour claim:** biologically relevant nucleotide chemistry requires substantial regio/stereochemical control and activated species.

**Primary comparison:** Powner et al. demonstrates activated pyrimidine ribonucleotide synthesis under proposed prebiotic conditions. Becker et al. demonstrates a high-yielding, highly regioselective purine nucleoside pathway.

**Disposition:** PARTIALLY ANSWERED LOCALLY; RETAIN SYSTEM BURDEN.

These papers materially answer older claims that such selectivity cannot be obtained chemically. They do not establish an integrated historical supply of all canonical nucleotides, activation chemistry, compatible concentrations and downstream polymerization.

Evidential state: E2 for demonstrated local chemistry; historical integration below E4.

## TOUR-009 — RNA linkage selectivity, copying and product inhibition

**Tour claim:** nonenzymatic RNA formation/copying faces 2'-5'/3'-5' linkage heterogeneity, activation, fidelity, strand separation and related burdens.

**Primary comparison:** Szostak-group work explicitly reports mixed 2'-5'/3'-5' linkages in known nonenzymatic primer-extension reconstructions, while also showing conditions/intermediates that strongly improve 3'-5' regioselectivity. Clay-catalyzed RNA work likewise produces mixed linkage types.

**Disposition:** RETAIN, UPDATED.

The strong claim that spontaneous RNA is overwhelmingly unusable 2'-5' material is not portable across modern systems. Linkage heterogeneity remains a demonstrated issue whose severity depends on chemistry. Copying length, fidelity, strand separation and repeated autonomous cycling remain separate arrows.

## TOUR-010 — Peptide formation in water

**Tour claim:** peptide-bond formation is unfavorable/problematic in bulk aqueous settings and side reactions matter.

**Primary comparison:** multiple activation, mineral/interface and wet-dry/cycling systems demonstrate peptide formation under candidate prebiotic conditions.

**Disposition:** REJECT CATEGORICAL IMPOSSIBILITY; RETAIN INTEGRATION BURDEN.

The valid question is whether a proposed environment supplies activation, concentration and cycling while remaining compatible with the rest of the pathway, and whether produced peptides have relevant sequence/function.

## Tranche-A result

Tour's strongest surviving objections are not categorical "chemistry cannot happen" propositions. They are systems constraints:

```text
mixture management
+ temporal ordering
+ boundary-condition continuity
+ intermediate persistence
+ network mass balance
+ stereochemical selection
+ route compatibility
+ activation supply
+ polymer linkage/copying quality
+ downstream integration
```

Modern experiments close portions of this space at E2. They do not, merely by accumulation, establish E3/E4 historical continuity from geochemistry to an autonomous evolvable system.
