# WP-BWM-0030 — Methodological Designism and Research Programme Governance

**Status:** active / framework institutionalization authorized 2026-09-25  
**Purpose:** formalize the ontological, epistemological, hermeneutical, and methodological rules governing BWM research, and institutionalize them as reusable constraints for BWM Historical Frameworks, World Domains, Integration work, and interfaces to associated research programmes.

## Objective

BWM already distinguishes Scripture, observation, model inference, retrodiction, and actual history, and WP-BWM-0009 supplies the canonical claim-level epistemic hierarchy. This package extends that foundation into an explicit research-method charter.

The governing architecture is:

```text
Christian Ontology
        ↓
Revelational Epistemology
        ↓
Methodological Designism
        ↓
BWM Research-Programme Governance
        ↓
Historical Frameworks / World Domains / Integration
        ↓
Models, hypotheses, predictions, tests, and dispositions
```

Methodological Designism is a cross-cutting methodological governance layer. It is not another Historical Framework or World Domain.

## Core formulation

BWM proceeds from the Christian claim that reality is created by God through the Logos and is therefore objectively intelligible, logically ordered, informationally determinate, causally structured, and purposive.

Scripture is the uniquely God-breathed authoritative propositional norm for the programme. Creation supplies genuine evidence. Human interpretation of Scripture and creation is fallible and therefore requires disciplined testing and correction.

The Holy Spirit is the infallible giver and steward of God's Word and guides believers into truth. A human attribution of an inward impression to the Spirit is fallible. A perceived counsel that conflicts with Scripture is rejected as a failed attribution, not treated as a conflict between Word and Spirit.

Science is treated as disciplined knowledge-seeking through observation, inference, testing, explanation, and correction. Methodological naturalism is a philosophical restriction on admissible explanation and is not built into BWM's definition of scientific method.

## Three-layer distinction

### 1. Ontology
Reality is created and designed. Logical order and intelligibility are features of reality rather than products of an investigator's methodological convention.

### 2. Epistemology
Humans are genuine but finite knowers. Scripture provides authoritative propositional revelation; creation provides real evidence; testimony, history, observation, and instruments may provide genuine information. Post-Fall cognition is noetically impaired without being destroyed.

### 3. Methodology
BWM uses exegesis, observation, measurement, historical inference, logic, model comparison, Bayesian reasoning where appropriate, prediction, testing, adversarial review, and correction under the governing ontology and epistemology.

## Hard core

For BWM inquiry, the following function as governing commitments:

- God is the creator and reality is intelligible because creation is ordered by divine rationality.
- Scripture is God-breathed and is the primary epistemic authority for biblical-historical claims.
- Scripture interprets Scripture first; canonical warrant constrains lower-level interpretation.
- Creation supplies genuine evidence and must be investigated rather than dismissed.
- The Holy Spirit does not contradict the Word He gave; human claims of inward counsel remain fallible and testable.
- Logical contradiction is not an acceptable terminal state. Apparent contradiction requires reevaluation of discernment, interpretation, auxiliary assumptions, models, or dependency claims.
- Human interpretations, scientific reconstructions, and BWM models are corrigible.
- Methodological naturalism is not treated as neutral ground or as a definitional requirement for science.

These commitments define the programme under inquiry. Their presence does not exempt subordinate claims from evidence or criticism.

## Protective belt

Revisable content includes:

- particular models and auxiliary hypotheses;
- scientific and historical reconstructions;
- empirical mechanisms;
- model parameters and boundary-condition proposals;
- attributions of inner impressions to divine counsel;
- particular interpretations where the text admits a legitimate hermeneutical range;
- domain-specific implementations of broader BWM commitments.

An anomaly should first bear on the claim that generated the failed expectation, consistent with WP-BWM-0009. Upward consequences follow demonstrated dependency.

## Hermeneutical constraint protocol

BWM distinguishes:

```text
TEXT → INTERPRETATION → MODEL
```

The text is not revised by the research programme. Human interpretation is corrigible, but reinterpretation is constrained.

A proposed reinterpretation must be independently defensible from the grammatical, historical, literary, canonical, and theological properties of the text. Empirical inconvenience alone does not create an admissible reading.

A model may not use hermeneutical elasticity as an unlimited rescue mechanism. Where a model depends materially on an interpretation, that dependency must be recorded.

## Noetic effects and culpability

BWM distinguishes the noetic consequences of the Fall from individual moral culpability.

Finite cognition, noetic impairment, presuppositional commitments, incomplete evidence, and ordinary interpretive error can produce mistaken conclusions. Disagreement with a BWM conclusion does not by itself establish intellectual dishonesty, rebellion, or culpable suppression of truth.

Moral culpability is a distinct theological category and shall not be inferred merely from scientific or interpretive disagreement.

## Research-programme heuristics

### Negative heuristic
Do not abandon governing commitments merely because a subordinate model encounters an anomaly. Locate the failed expectation and trace demonstrated dependencies before escalating the consequence.

### Positive heuristic
Use the governing commitments to generate constrained questions, models, predictions, integrations, and tests. Prefer proposals that increase explanatory integration, novel constraint, empirical exposure, and error-correcting capacity.

The protective belt is not permission for unlimited rescue assumptions.

## Model states and loss conditions

BWM models and belt hypotheses should use the following research states where applicable:

- **SUPPORTED** — presently accounts for relevant evidence and has survived applicable tests.
- **LIVE** — admissible and sufficiently motivated for continued investigation, but not adequately established.
- **DEGENERATING** — increasingly dependent on auxiliary repair without corresponding explanatory, predictive, or integrative gain.
- **REJECTED** — contradicted by sufficiently strong evidence, internally incoherent, incompatible with an established higher-level constraint, or defeated by a superior model under precommitted criteria.

A failed claim cannot be relabeled after the fact as dispensable when the parent model was previously materially dependent on it. WP-BWM-0009 dependency and precommitment rules govern escalation.

## Progressive-programme criterion

BWM should evaluate progress using at least four dimensions:

1. novel constraint;
2. successful prediction or risky retrodictive discrimination;
3. explanatory integration across relevant domains;
4. demonstrated error correction.

Ad hoc repair counts against progress when it primarily protects a preferred conclusion without independent evidential or explanatory gain.

These dimensions are governance criteria, not a claim that a single cardinal score currently exists.

## Bayesian use

### Intra-programme
For a governing frame C, competing belt models may be compared as:

```text
P(M_i | E, C) ∝ P(E | M_i, C) P(M_i | C)
```

Priors shall not be used to make rival admissible belt models impossible by definition, and likelihood assignments must remain exposed to correction.

### Inter-programme
Whole-worldview or whole-programme comparison must not manufacture victory by assigning the governing core probability 1 and rival cores probability 0.

Competing programmes may be compared through internal coherence, explanatory scope, empirical performance, risky predictions, historical explanation, parsimony where relevant, dependency behavior, correction behavior, and transcendental/presuppositional critique.

There is no requirement that all parties accept a worldview-neutral probability space before meaningful comparison can occur.

## Framework-disagreement rule

> **Framework disagreement is not model falsification. Model failure must be demonstrated against observations, logical consequences, internal constraints, demonstrated dependencies, or mutually applicable evaluative criteria rather than manufactured by importing a rival framework's hard-core commitments.**

This rule is symmetric.

Methodological naturalism cannot falsify a BWM model merely by defining design or divine action out of bounds. BWM cannot falsify a naturalistic model merely because that model does not affirm design.

## Symmetry and rival evaluation

A rival need not accept BWM's hard core to identify:

- internal contradiction;
- failure of a prediction;
- evidential mismatch;
- ad hoc auxiliary repair;
- dependency failure;
- a stronger competing explanation within BWM's own admissible model space.

BWM shall apply materially equivalent standards to competing programmes. Where an asymmetry is claimed, its independent justification must be stated.

## No free lunch for initial conditions

All models employ some set of initial conditions, boundary conditions, regularities, or initialized states. BWM shall not treat initialized functional maturity as uniquely illegitimate merely because a competing programme prefers a reconstructed prior history. Conversely, design shall not function as an unconstrained explanation for any observation.

Model + interpretation generates expectations. Tests evaluate those expectations and the dependencies that generated them.

## Methodological exemplars

The programme uses exemplars as a layered toolkit rather than treating them as one identical procedure:

- **Jesus and the apostles** — normative pattern of revelation, Word, reason, testing, and faithful knowledge.
- **Van Til** — presuppositional and transcendental critique; methodological naturalism is not treated as neutral ground.
- **Aristotle** — categorical precision, logic, causality, and teleological intelligibility.
- **Reid** — common-sense realism and the ordinary reliability of human cognitive faculties.
- **Plantinga** — warranted belief, properly basic theistic belief, and critique of restrictive evidentialism.
- **Aquinas** — disciplined metaphysical reasoning and public argument.
- **Socrates** — dialectical testing and exposure of hidden assumptions.
- **Gödel** — formal limits, modal rigor, explicit axiomatization, and metaphysical demonstration. His incompleteness results constrain claims that sufficiently expressive formal systems can provide an exhaustive internally derivable account of truth. His modal ontological argument is retained as an exemplar of exposing metaphysical premises and inferential dependencies to formal logical analysis. Neither is treated as independently establishing Christianity.
- **Bayes** — disciplined comparative updating and evidential calibration within stated assumptions.

These exemplars have different jurisdictions. Their inclusion does not imply agreement among them on theology or metaphysics.

### Gödel research interface

Gödel warrants a specific comparative research action rather than only an exemplar citation:

```text
Gödel modal ontological argument
            ↕
Logic Realism Theory (LRT)
            ↕
Logos Transcendental Argument (LTA)
```

The comparison shall identify primitive commitments, modal assumptions, definitions of necessity and actuality, inferential dependencies, explanatory burden, and the point at which each argument moves from formal validity to metaphysical truth claims.

The analysis shall preserve a strict distinction between:

1. what Gödel's incompleteness theorems formally establish;
2. philosophical conclusions sometimes drawn from incompleteness;
3. the axioms and formal consequences of Gödel's ontological argument;
4. the truth or independent warrant of those axioms;
5. claims distinctive to LRT, LTA, and Christian revelation.
