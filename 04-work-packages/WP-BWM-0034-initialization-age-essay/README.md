# WP-BWM-0034 — Initialization, Functional Maturity, and the Age Question

Status: implemented on feature branch; review pending  
Owner: Principal Operator / BWM  
Date opened: 2026-10-05

## Intent

Develop a public-facing BWM essay reframing the Young Creation / Old Creation age dispute around initialization, functional maturity, and the distinction between retrodictive age and actual historical age. The work also makes explicit that naturalistic origins accounts require some functionally adequate initial state. This is not offered as a proof of design by itself. It identifies a shared explanatory burden: every developmental account must specify a state capable of supporting the dynamics it assigns to subsequent history.

## Claim boundaries

1. Every origins model with a developmental history requires an initialization boundary or starting state adequate for the subsequent dynamics it invokes.
2. Functional maturity is used broadly for that adequacy in the comparative argument; it does not imply that naturalistic cosmology assumes mature macroscopic structures.
3. The claim that YEC and OEC differ by degree is restricted to the extent of initialized functional structure versus subsequent developmental history. It does not collapse their exegetical or theological differences.
4. Genesis 1 is interpreted as presenting ordinary terrestrial days based on the light/dark and evening/morning pattern.
5. The Day One light mechanism is left unspecified. Revelation 21:23 and 22:5 establish the theological possibility of divine illumination independent of the sun; they are not asserted to identify the physical mechanism of Genesis 1.
6. Day Four is described as appointment of ordinary astronomical governance of an already-established terrestrial cycle.
7. DFM does not license arbitrary apparent-history explanations. Initialized state depth must be coherent with created function and textual warrant.
8. Cana is an analogy for the state/history distinction, not a physical model of creation.
