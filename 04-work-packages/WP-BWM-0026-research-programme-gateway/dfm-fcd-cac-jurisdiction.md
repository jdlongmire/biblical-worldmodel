# DFM / FCD / CAC Jurisdiction Analysis

**Status:** WP-BWM-0026 Tranche A disposition proposal  
**Date:** 2026-09-24

## 1. Question

Determine whether Designed Functional Maturity (DFM), Functional Cosmological Deployment (FCD), and Creation Actualization Cosmology (CAC) are duplicate programmes, nested programmes, or programmes with distinct jurisdictions that BWM should consume separately.

## 2. Authoritative-source findings

### DFM

DFM is the general research programme for designed functional maturity, initialized boundary conditions, and the epistemology of retrodiction versus actual history. Its scope crosses domains. Current work includes radiogenic inference and information-rich astronomical states, but the programme is not limited to cosmology.

### FCD

FCD is a cosmological research programme. Its core is deliberately chronology-general. It develops functionally specified cosmological deployment, Cauchy-data constraints, observable ledgers, strong functional-necessity rules, and cosmology-facing discriminators without requiring a Creation Week chronology.

The authoritative FCD/CAC nesting artifact states that FCD is the more general cosmological programme and retains branches that CAC closes by chronology.

### CAC

CAC is a cosmological research programme that adds Creation Week and Day Four chronology commitments to the general functional-deployment architecture. The shared repository states that CAC's hard core entails FCD's and not conversely. CAC is therefore a chronology-specific specialization of FCD.

## 3. Jurisdiction decision

The three programmes are not peers covering identical scope.

DFM is **cross-domain**. FCD and CAC are **domain-specialized cosmology programmes**.

This means FCD should not be collapsed into DFM merely because both use functional maturity. FCD contains cosmology-specific formal and empirical machinery that properly belongs in a specialized programme.

## 4. CAC overlap problem

CAC overlaps DFM more strongly than FCD does because CAC imports chronology and functional-maturity commitments that are now being developed canonically in DFM.

That overlap does not justify silently deleting or absorbing CAC. Its authoritative repository contains independent research, traceability, accommodations, predictions, and open problems. However, its future role should be narrower:

- DFM owns the general doctrine and methodology of functional maturity, initialization, retrodictive age, and historical provenance.
- FCD owns chronology-general cosmological deployment machinery.
- CAC owns only the additional cosmological consequences of adding its Creation Week / Day Four commitments to FCD, and must consume rather than restate DFM's general framework where those commitments overlap.
- BWM owns the integration of those outputs into the Biblical WorldModel.

## 5. Recommended authority relationships

| Source | Owns | Consumes |
|---|---|---|
| DFM | general functional maturity; constrained initialization; retrodiction/history rules; cross-domain DFM research | BWM canonical boundary commitments as applicable |
| FCD | chronology-general cosmological deployment model; cosmological formalism and discriminators | general maturity principles where explicitly imported |
| CAC | chronology-specific specialization of FCD; additional Day Four/Creation Week cosmological consequences | FCD cosmological machinery + DFM general maturity/retrodiction rules + relevant BWM canonical commitments |
| BWM | world-model integration, canonical biblical boundaries, interfaces, comparative synthesis, publication | governed outputs from DFM/FCD/CAC |

## 6. Anti-duplication rules

1. DFM shall not reproduce FCD's cosmological formalism merely because cosmology is a DFM application domain.
2. FCD shall not acquire chronology commitments from CAC or BWM by implication.
3. CAC shall not maintain an independent competing definition of functional maturity or retrodiction when DFM owns the canonical programme-level formulation.
4. BWM shall not copy any of the three programmes' research into its own canonical baseline when an interface is sufficient.
5. Shared claims shall have one authoritative owner and typed consumers.
6. Cross-programme disagreement remains visible until the owning programmes disposition it.

## 7. BWM gateway disposition

BWM should recognize all three as associated programmes, but expose them differently:

- **DFM interface:** cross-domain research-programme interface.
- **FCD interface:** Cosmology World Domain interface.
- **CAC interface:** specialized Cosmology interface, explicitly marked as `specializes: FCD` and as consuming relevant DFM/BWM commitments.

CAC should not appear as a peer alternative to DFM in BWM navigation. They answer different architectural questions.

## 8. DTE / UTE consequence

This disposition does not alter the DFM/DTE/UTE comparison taxonomy. DTE and UTE remain BWM comparative interpretive/model families, not research programmes.

FCD's chronology-general scope also does not make it DTE. FCD is an authored research programme with its own hard core and tests; DTE is a BWM comparison family.

## 9. Migration implications

No repository merge is recommended.

The required cleanup is interface and ownership work:

1. pin DFM as owner of general functional-maturity and retrodiction definitions;
2. pin FCD as owner of chronology-general cosmological deployment machinery;
3. review CAC for duplicated DFM definitions and replace future duplication with governed imports;
4. add typed BWM interfaces for all three;
5. record any incompatible propositions rather than harmonizing them editorially.
