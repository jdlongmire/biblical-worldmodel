# Quantitative Audit 02 — Fossil Molecular Preservation

Status: initial evidence framing. No chronological conclusion authorized.

## Observation

Original/endogenous molecular and tissue-like structures have been reported from dinosaur fossils. Boatman et al. (2019) investigated T. rex vascular structures and reported evidence consistent with protein crosslinking and iron-associated preservation mechanisms.

**Disposition of the observation: RETAIN.**

## Source claim requiring correction

The intake text treats surviving soft tissues/proteins as intrinsically incompatible with Mesozoic conventional ages.

**Disposition: UNRESOLVED as a chronological discriminator.**

The observation is real enough to impose a preservation burden. The chronological inference requires quantitative kinetics under the actual burial/diagenetic environment.

## Competing hypotheses

### H-C
Long-duration preservation is possible through early chemical stabilization, mineral association, crosslinking, iron-mediated reactions, encapsulation, or other diagenetic mechanisms.

### H-K
The surviving molecular state is substantially more probable under a shorter elapsed burial history than under conventional chronology, even after known preservation mechanisms are modeled.

## Required discriminator

For a specified biomolecule/tissue and depositional environment:

```text
P(observed preservation state | H-C, environment)
vs
P(observed preservation state | H-K, environment)
```

The research programme therefore needs:
- molecular identity confidence;
- endogenous versus contamination assessment;
- initial concentration/state;
- temperature history;
- water activity;
- pH/redox history;
- mineral association;
- crosslink density;
- iron chemistry;
- hydrolysis/oxidation kinetics;
- microbial exposure;
- burial pressure and porosity;
- measured residual molecular distribution.

## Corpus recommendation

- **Earth History/DFM:** retain as a preservation-state constraint.
- **Public argument:** do not state "soft tissue proves young fossils."
- **Research target:** preservation half-life/kinetics under experimentally constrained diagenetic states.
- **Potential DFM discriminator:** whether initialized/preserved states expected by competing histories produce measurably different molecular signatures.

## Primary source

Boatman, E.M. et al. (2019), *Mechanisms of soft tissue and protein preservation in Tyrannosaurus rex*, Scientific Reports 9, 15678.

Confidence: HIGH that preservation is a legitimate empirical research burden; UNCERTAIN that it presently discriminates chronology.
