# WP-BWM-0031 — God's Word, God's World

## Working title

**God's Word, God's World**  
*Making Sense of All the Noise*

## Purpose

Develop a public-facing book from the Biblical World Model corpus that equips an intelligent general Christian reader to adjudicate competing claims about Scripture, nature, science, philosophy, culture, and AI without requiring mastery of the underlying BWM-associated research programmes.

## Governing editorial rule

> Introduce the insight. Demonstrate its usefulness. Leave the machinery in the corpus.

This is not a book about the author's models. It is a book about making sense of reality. BWM, DFM, Methodological Designism, LRT, TRT, PFH, CHFM, and related programmes appear only where their concepts illuminate the reader's problem.

A reader should be able to finish the book without mastering the programme acronyms while nevertheless absorbing their strongest epistemic and explanatory insights.

## Core proposition

There is one reality because there is one Creator. God's Word and God's world cannot ultimately contradict one another. Human interpretation remains fallible. The practical problem is therefore adjudication among revelation, observation, measurement, inference, models, historical reconstruction, philosophical commitments, cultural narratives, institutional claims, and machine-generated synthesis.

The recurring question is:

> What do we actually know, how do we know it, and what authority does each kind of claim possess?

## Intended reader

The primary reader is an intelligent Christian who is unwilling to surrender either the authority of Scripture or serious engagement with the created world, but who is confronted by a dense mixture of scientific, theological, philosophical, cultural, and technological claims.

The book should remain accessible to skeptical and non-Christian readers wherever possible by defining terms, exposing assumptions, and distinguishing evidence from interpretation rather than relying on insider vocabulary.

## Narrative architecture

**Noise → authority → interpretation → evidence → models → worldview → coherence → Logos**

### Provisional chapter sequence

1. **The Noise**  
   Information abundance, expert disagreement, institutional authority, algorithms, AI synthesis, and the modern problem of adjudication.

2. **God's Word**  
   Revelation, Scripture, Logos, truth, hermeneutics, and the distinction between infallible revelation and fallible human interpretation.

3. **God's World**  
   Creation as genuinely intelligible and investigable. Observation matters. Evidence constrains models.

4. **Models Aren't Reality**  
   Separate observation, measurement, interpretation, model, extrapolation, and historical reconstruction. Introduce the principle that an inferred or retrodictive age is model-dependent and is not identical by definition to historical age.

5. **Nobody Comes Without a Worldview**  
   Presuppositions, methodological naturalism, materialism, theism, designism, empiricism, scientism, and the question of which commitments are smuggled into interpretation.

6. **How Explanations Compete**  
   Accessible introduction to explanatory burden, auxiliary hypotheses, Bayesian updating, Lakatosian research programmes, prediction/accommodation, falsification and model loss conditions.

7. **Origins and Deep Time**  
   Use BWM/DFM as a worked application of the epistemology. Genesis, cosmology, geological history, radiometric interpretation, the Flood, functional maturity, and competing historical reconstructions. Clearly distinguish canonical constraints from defeasible models.

8. **Life, Information, and Design**  
   Biological information, origin-of-life chemistry, codes, function, regulation, evolution/adaptation, and design inference. Harvest the corpus selectively rather than reproduce programme-level technical treatment.

9. **Mind, Meaning, and the Machine**  
   Mind/brain distinction, consciousness, intentionality, semantic content, rational inference, computation, and AI. Use contemporary AI to clarify the difference between representation, computation, meaning, and agency.

10. **Why Does Anything Make Sense?**  
    Accessible treatment of logic, information, action, intelligibility, contingency, necessity, and grounding. LRT/TRT supply conceptual infrastructure without requiring formal programme mastery.

11. **Christ the Logos**  
    Bring creation, intelligibility, revelation, rationality, meaning, and redemption together in the biblical doctrine of the Logos, with particular attention to John 1 and Colossians 1.

12. **Making Sense of the Noise**  
    Give the reader a reusable epistemic discipline for evaluating claims.

## Reader-facing epistemic discipline

Develop a compact recurring method:

1. What exactly is being claimed?
2. What terms require definition?
3. What was actually observed or revealed?
4. What has been inferred?
5. What assumptions connect evidence to conclusion?
6. Which auxiliary hypotheses are carrying explanatory load?
7. What authority properly governs the question?
8. What would count against the claim?
9. What competing explanation addresses the same evidence?
10. How certain should the conclusion actually be?

## Epistemic-status device

Evaluate a recurring visual or marginal notation that identifies the status of important claims:

- **Revelation**
- **Observation**
- **Inference**
- **Model**
- **Hypothesis**
- **Speculation**

The device should reinforce disciplined distinctions without cluttering the prose.

## Programme integration policy

### BWM
Use as the principal worked case for integrating Scripture and investigation of nature. Do not require wholesale adoption of every BWM component hypothesis.

### DFM
Introduce functional maturity, retrodictive age, initialization conditions, and model dependence through concrete examples before naming the formal programme.

### Methodological Designism
Use its epistemic discipline, symmetry requirements, burden accounting, and research-programme governance. Avoid turning the book into a methodological charter.

### LRT
Introduce logical constraint through intuitive questions about whether logical truths depend upon human recognition. Reserve formal development for the underlying corpus.

### TRT
Introduce the distinction among logical constraint, informational specification, and actualization/action through concrete examples. Formal notation is optional and should appear only where it materially improves understanding.

### PFH / CHFM and other BWM components
Harvest only where required by a worked example. Clearly label conjectural historical reconstruction.

## Corpus-harvesting workstream

Perform a structured sweep of BWM and associated repositories to build a book-source ledger. For each candidate artifact record:

- source repository and path
- concept or argument
- candidate chapter
- epistemic status
- accessibility level
- whether direct quotation, adaptation, or conceptual rewrite is appropriate
- primary sources requiring verification
- conflicting material or superseded formulations
- disposition: include / sidebar / notes / omit / technical appendix / underlying corpus only

Priority harvesting areas:

- canonical epistemic hierarchy
- origins epistemology
- retrodictive age terminology and burden ledger
- Methodological Designism charter
- DFM foundations and reconstruction argument
- Genesis systems architecture
- creation/deep-time discussions
- abiogenesis and information discussions
- mind/brain and AI material
- LRT/TRT explanatory concepts
- Logos and intelligibility material
- relevant public debate exchanges that contain unusually clear lay explanations

## Source discipline

The book must preserve the corpus's source standards:

- primary sources before secondary sources
- never fabricate citations
- never present secondary material as primary
- use “as cited in” when primary material is accessed only through a secondary source
- state when only secondary evidence is available
- report substantive divergence among sources
- distinguish documented evidence from the author's inference
- maintain proportionate confidence

Scripture citations should use the ESV within applicable quotation limits.

## Tone and presentation

Narrative and concrete before abstract. Define terms before arguments. Use technical vocabulary where it earns explanatory value. Avoid making readers learn programme nomenclature merely to follow the argument.

The book should model the epistemic conduct it advocates. Where the author advances a defeasible hypothesis, identify it as such. Where evidence creates pressure on a preferred interpretation, show that pressure rather than hiding it.

## Success criteria

The WP succeeds when:

- a reader can understand the central epistemic method without prior knowledge of BWM or associated programmes;
- programme concepts are introduced only when they solve a reader-facing problem;
- speculative BWM components are clearly distinguished from canonical/theological claims;
- scientific observations are distinguished from model-dependent historical inference;
- competing explanations receive symmetrical methodological scrutiny;
- the manuscript remains recognizably Christian and culminates coherently in Christ the Logos;
- technical readers can trace important claims back into the deeper research corpus;
- the resulting book functions as a coherent work rather than a compilation of existing papers.
