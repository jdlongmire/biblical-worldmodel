## Evidence distinctions

1. Sequenced bases, reads, mapped variants and skeletal measurements are observations subject to technical error.
2. Population labels, inferred admixture fractions and coalescent times are model-conditioned reconstructions.
3. A first-generation Neanderthal–Denisovan offspring is direct evidence of successful reproduction; it does not alone determine a taxonomic rank or biblical epoch.
4. The existence of archaic ancestry in present humans does not by itself identify whether a source individual lived before or after a proposed Flood.
5. Genome-wide patterns and geographic variation must be tested jointly, rather than fitting one ancestry percentage.

## Source confidence

HIGH: existence and content of the inspected publisher-hosted primary studies. MEDIUM: broad reported genomic reconstructions conditional on their statistical models. UNCERTAIN: Flood-relative placement and compressed chronology. These levels refer to provenance and inferential dependency, not to theological truth.
