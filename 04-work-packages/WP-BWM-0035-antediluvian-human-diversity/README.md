# WP-BWM-0035: Antediluvian Human Diversity

**Status:** Proposed / research initiated; not accepted or canonicalized  
**Owner:** Biblical WorldModel integration programme  
**Date:** 2026-10-10  
**Principal:** James D. Longmire  
**Scope:** Comparative human-origins models spanning Fall, Flood, and post-Flood history

## Objective

Compare young-earth creationist interpretations of Neanderthals, Denisovans, and anatomically modern humans, then evaluate a bounded antediluvian-diversification alternative against primary fossil, stratigraphic, genetic, and textual evidence. Keep the Pre-Fall Hypothesis (WP-BWM-0012) separate; use only its interface that human death does not precede the Fall.

## Epistemic classification

- **Scriptural premises:** Genesis 1:26–27; 2:17; 3:19; 5; 6–9; 10–11; Romans 5:12. Scripture affirms a common human ancestry and Flood survivors. Distinct archaic fossil categories are not named in Scripture.
- **Observed data:** Morphological trait distributions, ancient genomic sequences, introgression, stratigraphic context, taphonomy, and measurement results.
- **Model hypotheses:** Placement of fossils relative to the Flood, initial genetic variation, bottleneck inheritance, population divergence, and elapsed chronology.
- **Unresolved:** Identification of a globally correlatable Flood boundary, reconciliation of ancient genomic divergence and diversity with a small founder population, and whether the antediluvian interval can accommodate the required demographic history.

## Competing models

| ID | Model | Distinguishing claim | Primary exposure |
|---|---|---|---|
| M1 | Post-Flood archaic diversification | Neanderthal/Denisovan differentiation primarily follows the Flood and Babel | Short chronology, lineage divergence, geographic dispersal |
| M2 | Antediluvian diversification | Differentiated populations exist after the Fall but before the Flood; archaic ancestry survives in Noah's family | Fossil placement, bottleneck transmission, dating |
| M3 | Mixed interval | Some lineage divergence predates the Flood; later populations continue to differentiate | Multiple rates and phase boundaries, identifiability |
| M4 | Conventional paleoanthropological reconstruction | Long-timescale hominin population divergence and admixture | Comparative empirical benchmark, distinct theological commitments |

Do not assume fossil morphology alone identifies an antediluvian individual. A post-Flood individual can inherit archaic ancestry. Likewise, the extinction of a distinct population at the Flood does not imply loss of every allele associated with it.

## Workstreams and deliverables

1. **Text and chronology:** Derive constraints from Genesis and Romans; distinguish Adam-to-Flood from Fall-to-Flood. Compare Masoretic, Septuagint, and Samaritan chronological variants; do not assert an Eden duration.
2. **Creationist model register:** Extract explicit claims from Answers in Genesis, Creation Ministries International, Institute for Creation Research, Creation Research Society, Jordan, Habermehl, Oard, Clarey, and dissenting models. Record original source, date, page/section, chronology, fossil boundary, genetic mechanism, and uncertainty.
3. **Morphological evidence ledger:** Document population-level trait distributions and multivariate diagnostics, including robusticity, cranial geometry, developmental variation, and limits of sparse Denisovan morphology.
4. **Ancient DNA ledger:** Evaluate Neanderthal/Denisovan/modern-human relationships, introgression, mitochondrial and Y-chromosome genealogies, allele sharing, coalescent-time assumptions, and ascertainment/contamination.
5. **Flood boundary and taphonomy:** Compare proposed geological boundaries against site-specific stratigraphy, burial environment, reworking, and fossil chronology.
6. **Population-genetic feasibility:** Parameterize founder genotypes, effective population size, pre-Flood diversity, generation length, mutation/recombination, drift, admixture, and selection. Explicitly model transmission through Noah's family, including kinship and sex-specific ancestry. Avoid equating eight passengers with eight unrelated founders.
7. **Discriminators and severe tests:** Register predictions before model fitting; report contrary results and non-identifiable parameters.
8. **Synthesis:** Produce a comparative appraisal, open-problems register, and recommendation for whether an antediluvian human-diversification submodel merits canonical promotion.

## Initial test register

| Test | Question | Failure condition |
|---|---|---|
| T1 Founder bottleneck | Can surviving genomes transmit the observed shared archaic alleles and lineage structure? | No viable founder genotype configuration under stated bounds |
| T2 Divergence | Can population models reproduce ancient DNA differences and admixture proportions? | Required rates or populations exceed explicitly justified limits |
| T3 Stratigraphic boundary | Can a single Flood-boundary hypothesis consistently classify relevant fossil sites? | Repeated contradictory site classifications or circular boundary assignment |
| T4 Morphology | Do modeled within-human distributions account for diagnostic trait combinations? | Model cannot reproduce multivariate fossil morphology |
| T5 Chronology | Are population sizes and generation counts feasible in each biblical textual scenario? | Necessary events cannot fit the permitted interval |
| T6 Comparative fit | Does M2 or M3 explain more independent observations than M1 without ad hoc parameters? | Complexity increases without measurable improvement |
