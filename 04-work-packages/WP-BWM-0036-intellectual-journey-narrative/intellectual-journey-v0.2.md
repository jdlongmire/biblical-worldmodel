# Coming Back Differently
## An intellectual journey toward a Biblical World Model, v0.2

I did not begin this journey trying to construct a new creation model. I began with a Bible I believed was true.

The journey described here spans roughly thirty-five years. The early and middle portions are remembered history, not a reconstruction from a complete documentary archive. The later portions are increasingly documented in articles, papers, repositories, work packages, and model revisions. I do not want the abundance of recent records to create the false impression that the journey began when the records became dense.

Nor was the path straight.

## Formation

My upbringing was very much fundamentalist. I do not say that as a criticism. It gave me a durable confidence that Scripture speaks truthfully about the world God made and the history He governs. Genesis was not merely devotional literature or theological symbolism. God created. Adam and Eve were real. The Fall was real. The Flood was real history. The creation account described divine action in the real world.

Young creation was therefore the basic framework I inherited.

What that formation did not give me, at least in the form I received it, was a mature method for interrogating sophisticated challenges to those conclusions. I knew what I believed. I was less prepared to ask what assumptions governed a competing account, how historical models were constructed, where observation ended and reconstruction began, or whether the rules of inquiry themselves carried philosophical commitments.

That limitation should not be confused with a rejection of the formation itself. The confidence in Scripture remained. What changed over the following decades was the architecture by which I tried to relate Scripture, reason, observation, scientific models, and historical reconstruction.

## Learning to Think in Systems

My professional life was teaching me a different set of intellectual habits.

I came up through information technology from the ground level, beginning as a network technician and maturing through architecture into enterprise architecture and systems engineering. Exposure to defense-system modeling and simulation added another layer.

In those disciplines, abstractions eventually have to survive contact with systems.

A model is not the system.

Models have boundaries. They have assumptions. They have inputs, initial states, interfaces, constraints, abstractions, and permitted behaviors. A simulation can execute perfectly and still misrepresent the system because the error lies in the model rather than the computation. Verification asks whether we built the model correctly. Validation asks whether we built the right model for the real thing we are trying to understand.

Those habits did not immediately become an origins methodology. For much of the journey they were simply part of how I learned to solve technical problems. Their relevance to creation, deep time, and historical science became explicit much later.

That distinction matters. I do not want to write my present methodology backward into my younger self.

## Permission to Reconsider

A significant theological milestone came through the Presbyterian Church in America's creation-study process around the turn of the century.

My memory of its significance to me is stronger than the denomination's actual conclusion. The PCA did not adopt old-earth creationism. Its Creation Study Committee documented substantial disagreement over the nature and duration of the creation days while unanimously affirming Genesis 1–3 as the inerrant Word of God, true history, creation ex nihilo, the special creation of Adam and Eve, the historical Fall, and biblical supernaturalism. The General Assembly affirmed that the diversity covered by the report could exist within the church so long as the full historicity of the creation account was maintained.

What the PCA formally said and what I heard at that point in my journey are therefore related but not identical.

What I heard was permission.

Faithful, confessionally Reformed Christians with a high doctrine of Scripture could disagree about the creation days. Asking whether my inherited interpretation was correct was not itself unbelief. Considering an old earth did not automatically mean surrendering biblical authority.

That opened a door.

## The Long Middle

The next stage should not be compressed into a paragraph simply because I have fewer dated artifacts from it.

Over a considerable period I engaged old-earth interpretations, conventional cosmology and geology, radiometric dating, literary approaches to Genesis, and Ancient Near Eastern context. I tried to understand the strongest versions of those arguments rather than dismissing them from outside.

The project became one of harmonization.

Could I retain Scripture as materially authoritative while understanding Genesis in a way compatible with an old cosmos and old earth? Could the days bear something other than the ordinary chronological sense I had inherited? Could literary structure or ANE context explain features of the text that a straightforward chronology seemed not to capture? Could conventional natural history and a faithful Christian systematic coexist?

For a substantial period, I believed some form of that synthesis was possible.

This was not a season in which I ceased caring about Scripture. Quite the opposite. I was trying to preserve both what I understood Scripture to teach and what appeared to be an overwhelming reconstruction of natural history.

That distinction is essential to the story. If I portray the harmonization period as simple compromise, I erase the reason it was persuasive to me in the first place.

## A Problem of Direction

Over time, however, something began to trouble me.

No single accommodation seemed decisive. Many were individually plausible. The problem became visible cumulatively.

When the conventional reconstruction of natural history and my reading of Scripture appeared to conflict, the interpretive pressure seemed to run consistently in one direction.

Scripture moved.

The external reconstruction rarely did.

That observation forced a deeper question. What was actually regulating my interpretation?

I eventually came to call what I saw happening in myself **presuppositional capitulation**:

> Presuppositional capitulation is the progressive, often individually plausible, accommodation of one's systematic commitments to the governing assumptions of another worldview until those assumptions acquire functional authority over the original system.

The phrase is intentionally stronger than influence. It describes a migration of functional authority that can occur while someone sincerely continues to affirm inspiration, inerrancy, creation, miracles, resurrection, and divine sovereignty.

It was first a criticism of my own reasoning.

It is not a synonym for old-earth creationism. An old-earth interpretation might be reached through independent exegesis while genuinely maintaining Scriptural authority. The diagnostic question concerns the direction of revision:

**When Scripture and a historical reconstruction appear to conflict, which framework is permitted to challenge the assumptions of the other?**

I increasingly realized that I had spent considerable energy asking how Scripture might accommodate the reconstruction while spending less energy asking how the reconstruction itself had been constructed.

That asymmetry became difficult to ignore.

## Questioning the Axis

As I reassessed the creation question, two highly visible public poles often framed the debate for me, though they never exhausted the possible positions.

Ken Ham represented a familiar recent-creation package: ordinary creation days, recent creation, a global Flood, and resistance to deep-time reconstruction.

Hugh Ross represented a serious old-earth alternative: an ancient cosmos and earth combined with special creation and an explicit claim to biblical fidelity.

I found things worth retaining in both conversations. I shared major biblical conclusions associated with recent creation, but I was not willing simply to inherit every scientific auxiliary, apologetic argument, or proposed mechanism associated with conventional YEC. At the same time, I wanted the empirical seriousness emphasized by old-earth advocates without granting the conventional deep-time reconstruction the status of neutral observation.

So I began asking a question that became a hinge:

**Is there a third way outside the Ken Ham and Hugh Ross camps?**

In retrospect, "third way" was not quite the right geometry.

I was not looking for a midpoint.

I was beginning to question the axis.

Could biblical history be taken with full material seriousness and empirical evidence with full intellectual seriousness? Could observation be distinguished from historical inference? Could assumptions and initial conditions be exposed rather than hidden inside a reconstruction? Could supernatural divine action remain an admissible historical cause where Scripture independently warrants it? Could all of that be done without turning "God did it" into an escape from explanatory accountability?

Those questions began reconnecting my theological journey with the systems habits accumulated through my professional life.

## From Systems Thinking to Origins Methodology

The questions I routinely asked of technical systems now became relevant to historical reconstruction.

What is the observed state?

What is inferred from it?

What model connects the two?

What initial and boundary conditions have been specified?

Which variables are measured and which are derived?

Which causal mechanisms are admitted?

Which have been excluded before the analysis begins?

How sensitive is the conclusion to those choices?

Are apparently independent lines of confirmation genuinely independent, or do they inherit common assumptions?

Are we confusing the output of the model with the history of the system itself?

This did not prove a recent creation.

It changed what I thought had to be demonstrated before an inferred history could be treated as actual history.

Creation also began to look like an initialization problem.

The laws governing an operating system do not necessarily specify how the system was initialized. A running network does not contain its deployment history. The behavior of software after startup does not tell us how its initial data was populated. A simulation beginning from a specified state does not entail that the real system traversed the simulated trajectory to arrive there.

The analogy has limits, but the methodological point is important: **operation does not by itself establish provenance**.

## The Recent Documentary Phase

By late 2024 and 2025, the documentary resolution of the journey becomes much higher.

My oddXian writing from December 2024 already records the importance of questioning, evidence, mind, Logos, and worldview comparison. By January 2025 I was explicitly distinguishing science from naturalistic assumptions and using the language of methodological designism.

A January 2025 essay, "Whether You're Young Earth, Old Earth... Four Non-Negotiables," is especially revealing in retrospect. I was still deliberately charitable across origins timelines. I identified divine fiat, genuine divine intervention, Scriptural interpretive priority, and the imago Dei as more fundamental than the age question. Yet the essay also asked whether a framework merely permitted those commitments or actually required them, and warned against allowing interpreted nature to acquire priority over special revelation.

The later terminology was not yet fully developed, but the methodological concern was already visible.

During 2025 the work became increasingly experimental and technical. Literal Programmatic Intervention, or LPI, attempted to use systems concepts to construct an explicit recent-creation framework. Hydrotectonic models, time architecture, physical mechanisms, radiometric questions, heat budgets, and other auxiliaries were proposed and tested.

Some of those ideas did not survive intact.

That is important.

The record includes gap analyses that concluded the framework was not ready, objections that exposed insufficiently rigorous mechanisms, revisions prompted by technical criticism, and the removal or downgrading of arguments that could not carry the load assigned to them. Systems metaphors were explicitly distinguished from physical mechanisms: the metaphor could clarify the claim, but the mechanism had to bear the empirical burden.

This period taught me something that now governs the larger programme: a biblical commitment is not rescued by a weak scientific auxiliary. If a mechanism fails, the mechanism fails.

The proper response is not to hide the anomaly.

## Research Programmes Rather Than Isolated Arguments

My exposure to Imre Lakatos gave increasingly precise language to what I was trying to do.

A research programme has a hard core, a protective belt of auxiliary hypotheses, positive and negative heuristics, and a developmental history. An anomaly need not instantly destroy the programme. Neither may the programme be protected indefinitely through ad hoc auxiliaries whose only purpose is to prevent failure.

That distinction helped me separate theological commitments from scientific models proposed under them.

A particular Flood mechanism is not Scripture.

A particular cosmology is not Scripture.

A proposed explanation for radiometric observations is not Scripture.

A model may fail without biblical authority failing with it.

By late 2025 this research-programme structure, together with increasingly symmetric scrutiny of naturalistic and design-oriented explanations, was becoming explicit in Scientific Designism and Christian Designism work. The goal was no longer to collect arguments for a preferred conclusion. It was to build a framework in which competing explanations could be compared, burdens recorded, auxiliaries revised or abandoned, and unresolved anomalies retained rather than concealed.

## Initialization, Operation, and Designed Functional Maturity

Another distinction was becoming clearer.

If Scripture describes actual supernatural creation, then creation cannot be modeled merely as an unusually early interval of ordinary operation. Creation is the deployment of the system.

That does not mean every feature of the present world may be assigned arbitrarily to initialization. Such a move would make the proposal immune to evidence and therefore explanatorily empty.

The developing insight was narrower.

If God created functioning systems, then their initialized states matter to historical reconstruction. Extrapolating ordinary post-initialization dynamics backward through a genuine initialization boundary may yield a coherent retrodictive history without demonstrating that the retrodicted history actually occurred.

This became one of the roots of **Designed Functional Maturity**.

DFM is therefore not simply "apparent age" with a new label. It attempts to distinguish operational dynamics from deployment, observed state from reconstructed trajectory, and retrodictive age from warranted historical provenance.

And because initialization can itself become an evasion, the model requires explanatory discipline. Ordinary post-creation processes should be investigated first where adequate. Scripturally warranted catastrophes should be tested where they possess relevant causal capacity. Creation-process events should be considered where warranted. Macro-state initialization comes within an ordered explanatory allocation rather than functioning as a universal escape clause.

## Regulative Supernaturalism

This developing methodology required a clearer statement about supernatural causation.

I now express it as **regulative supernaturalism**:

> **If Scripture warrants supernatural divine action in the history of creation, explanations of the resulting world must permit that action to function as a genuine historical cause.**

More formally:

> Where special revelation independently warrants divine action as an event in created history, that action is an admissible historical cause and must not be excluded by methodological rule. Its explanatory use remains constrained by the content of revelation, observed evidence, causal adequacy, and comparative model performance.

The word *regulative* matters.

Supernaturalism establishes the admissible causal space under revelation. It does not tell us in advance which particular scientific explanation wins.

That is the distinction I had been missing when the choice appeared to be between accepting conventional historical reconstruction or invoking supernaturalism without analytical discipline.

## The Exemplars

A parallel intellectual stream involved thinkers whose work helped me name, sharpen, or discipline questions already emerging.

They did not arrive as a curriculum, and I did not adopt any of them as a complete system.

Christ the Logos is not one exemplar among many. He is the governing center. The apostolic witness supplies the authoritative Scriptural revelation by which everything else is tested.

Under that authority, Cornelius Van Til sharpened my recognition that there is no neutral intellectual ground and helped me interrogate the presuppositions beneath methodological naturalism.

Aristotle reinforced categorical precision, causality, teleology, and the need to identify what kind of thing is under discussion before arguing about it.

Socrates reinforced the discipline of asking the next question, exposing assumptions, and following an argument through its implications.

Thomas Reid helped preserve a robust realism about human cognition and the external world without pretending human interpretation is infallible.

Alvin Plantinga clarified warrant and challenged the assumption that Christian belief must first receive permission from a naturalistic epistemic tribunal.

Thomas Aquinas, engaged critically from within my Reformed commitments, demonstrated the seriousness of disciplined reasoning about metaphysics, causality, analogy, natural theology, and revelation.

Kurt Gödel reinforced the limits of formal systems and the danger of confusing rigorous representation with exhaustive reality.

Imre Lakatos supplied the architecture for research programmes, hard cores, protective belts, heuristics, progress, degeneration, and disciplined theory change.

Thomas Bayes supplied a language for comparative reasoning under uncertainty. The question became not merely whether an observation was compatible with my model, but whether it discriminated between competing models and how strongly.

My professional systems disciplines supplied another kind of exemplar: requirements, architecture, interfaces, traceability, verification, validation, configuration control, boundary conditions, model fidelity, and explicit assumptions.

I did not adopt these voices as a composite magisterium.

I tested them.

I retained what proved useful, qualified what required qualification, and rejected what conflicted with the governing authority of Scripture.

"Test everything; hold fast what is good" became an apt description of the method.

## Coming Back Differently

There is an irony in where roughly thirty-five years of questioning have brought me.

I now hold conclusions about creation that resemble important convictions with which I began.

Yet I do not hold them in quite the same way.

The recent creation of my childhood was substantially inherited. The position I hold now has passed through decades of theological reflection, serious attempted harmonization, engagement with old-earth and ANE interpretations, revision of my own systematic theology, presuppositional critique, professional systems thinking, model construction, failed and revised auxiliaries, philosophy of science, comparative model analysis, and continuing exposure to evidence that can place pressure on my preferred conclusions.

I have not returned by pretending the intervening questions never happened.

I came back differently.

That means I reserve the right to be wrong about my models.

If my exegesis fails, it should be corrected.

If DFM accumulates unresolved explanatory burdens, those burdens should be recorded rather than hidden.

If a Flood model fails against geological evidence, biblical authority does not rescue that particular geological model.

If an auxiliary hypothesis exists mainly to protect a preferred conclusion, it should be recognized as ad hoc.

If conventional reconstruction explains an observation better, that fact belongs in the ledger even when I dispute the larger historical inference attached to it.

Christian confidence should be able to survive scrutiny.

## Toward a Biblical World Model

The question that once sounded like "Is there a third way?" eventually became a different class of question.

Who establishes the rules of inquiry?

What counts as an observation?

What has been inferred?

Which assumptions connect observation to reconstruction?

What causal history is admissible?

Where are the initialization boundaries?

What is measured, and what is modeled?

What is revelation, what is observation, what is inference, what is model, what is hypothesis, and what remains speculation?

What would count against my preferred explanation?

Those questions eventually led toward a Biblical World Model.

BWM is not intended to replace God's world with another human system. It is an attempt to make the architecture of interpretation explicit: Scripture first, nature genuinely observed, assumptions exposed, models testable, burdens retained, competing explanations compared, and supernatural divine action neither excluded by rule nor invoked as an undisciplined escape.

The journey did not begin with BWM.

BWM is one present result of the journey.

And the journey continues.
