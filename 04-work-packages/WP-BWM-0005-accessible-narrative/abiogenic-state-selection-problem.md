# Abiogenic State-Selection Problem

**Status:** captured for Tier 2/3 development and later canonical cross-check.  
**Purpose:** preserve a structural analogy between quantum decoherence/measurement and a pre-Darwinian stabilization problem in naturalistic abiogenesis, without asserting that abiogenesis is itself a quantum-measurement process.

## Core distinction

The analogy is **structural**, not ontological.

Quantum decoherence provides a known case in which interaction with an environment suppresses interference among alternatives and stabilizes certain effectively classical states. Decoherence helps explain why some states become dynamically robust. It does not by itself explain semantic meaning, biological function, or teleology.

Naturalistic abiogenesis faces a different physical problem, but with a potentially illuminating structural similarity: prebiotic chemistry permits a vast space of possible molecular states, while life requires a far narrower class of coordinated states capable of persistence, replication, heredity, regulation, compartmentalization, metabolism, and eventually coding and error correction.

The relevant pattern is:

> **possibility space → constraint/selection → stabilized state**

In quantum decoherence, environmental interaction supplies a defined physical mechanism for the stabilization of classical alternatives.

In abiogenesis, the unresolved explanatory question is what physical processes select and stabilize the very small subset of chemical organizations that can participate in a self-maintaining, information-bearing biological system.

## Working formulation

> **Natural law can delimit what chemical states are possible. Abiogenesis must also explain why a tiny subset of those states became persistently selected, stabilized, and integrated into a self-maintaining informational system before biological selection itself existed.**

This should be treated as an explanatory problem rather than as a claim that current origin-of-life research has no candidate mechanisms. Candidate mechanisms must be evaluated on their demonstrated capacity to bridge the relevant transition.

## Pre-Darwinian stabilization problem

Darwinian evolution presupposes, at minimum, some form of:

- persistence;
- replication or reproduction;
- heritable variation;
- differential propagation or success.

A naturalistic origin-of-life account therefore bears an antecedent burden: it must explain how a chemical system first acquired enough organized causal structure for those Darwinian dynamics to become applicable.

Working question:

> **Before biological selection can preserve adaptive function, what mechanism establishes the first system capable of preserving, reproducing, and differentially propagating functionally relevant states?**

This may be called the **pre-Darwinian stabilization problem** or the **abiogenic state-selection problem**.

## Relation to information and actualization

For BWM/TRT/LRT cross-reference, the problem can be framed in terms of lawful possibility versus functional actualization.

Physical law constrains admissible state transitions. Biological organization requires coordinated causal relationships in which informational states are preserved, interpreted, acted upon, and reproduced in ways that contribute to continued system function.

Using the existing LIA formulation:

`χ ≡ 𝒜(I | L)`

where:

- `L` = lawful/logical constraint;
- `I` = informational content;
- `𝒜` = action or causal agency/process;
- `χ` = actualized state or effect.

The abiogenesis question is therefore not merely how chemically complex molecules can form. It includes the origin of the first stable coupling among information-bearing states, lawful constraints, and causal processes sufficient to generate and preserve system-level function.

## Guardrails

Do **not** state:

> `Abiogenesis has a quantum measurement problem.`

That formulation overstates the analogy and risks conflating distinct physical domains.

Prefer:

> **Abiogenesis has a selection-and-stabilization problem that is structurally analogous to the role decoherence plays in selecting dynamically robust states from a broader possibility space.**

Likewise, do not infer design merely from the existence of a large chemical state space. The design argument, if developed, must depend on demonstrated properties such as functional integration, informational dependence, causal closure, coordinated control, or failure of candidate unguided mechanisms to generate the required organization under realistic conditions.

## Research questions

1. What minimum organizational state must exist before Darwinian selection becomes applicable?
2. Which prebiotic mechanisms can demonstrably stabilize such states under plausible early-Earth conditions?
3. Can known chemical selection processes account for integrated replication, compartmentalization, metabolism, and informational control within the same system?
4. What is the distinction between thermodynamic/kinetic stability and biological functional persistence?
5. At what point, if any, does a chemical system instantiate information in a biologically operative sense rather than merely exhibit statistical correlations?
6. Does the transition require a threshold form of causal closure or functional integration that is not captured by sequence production alone?
7. Which features of the problem are genuine empirical gaps, and which are artifacts of how the state space is represented?

## Public-language candidate

> **Chemistry can produce many kinds of molecules. Life requires much more: a system that keeps the right things together, preserves useful states, reproduces them, and uses them to keep the system going. The origin-of-life question is therefore not only how molecules formed, but how chemistry first became an organized system capable of preserving and acting on functionally relevant information.**
