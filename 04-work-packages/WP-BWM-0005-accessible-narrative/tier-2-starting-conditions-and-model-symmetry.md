# Tier 2 Notes: Starting Conditions, Calculated Age, and Model Symmetry

**Status:** captured for Tier 2 development and later canonical cross-check.  
**Purpose:** preserve conclusions from adversarial/reviewer discussion without overloading the Tier 1 story.

## 1. The two-question architecture

The public story is organized around two questions:

1. **How much was already there when God made the world?**
2. **What happened after that?**

The first concerns the created starting state. The second concerns real history after creation: Fall, Flood, later Earth history, and ordinary lawful processes.

This provides an accessible bridge from the Tier 1 story into DFM and CHFM without requiring the reader to begin with programme terminology.

## 2. Process behavior does not by itself establish object history

A central Tier 2 distinction is:

> **We can measure how a process works without thereby knowing that the process produced everything we are measuring.**

Erosion rates tell us how erosion behaves. Radioactive decay rates tell us how decay behaves. These measurements are real and important. Working backward can then calculate how much time a process would require to produce a measured state under the model being used.

The remaining historical question is whether that process, beginning from the assumed starting state, actually produced the full state being measured.

Plain-language formulation:

> **Working backward can tell us how long something would take if that process made what we see. It does not always tell us that the process actually made it.**

This is the conceptual heart of the video-game valley thought experiment.

## 3. Calculated age and actual elapsed history

Do not collapse a calculated age into actual elapsed history by definition.

A calculated age is evidence that must be taken seriously. It can strongly constrain possible histories. It does not become direct observation of the unobserved past merely because the calculation is precise.

Preferred Tier 2 formulation:

> **A dating system can yield a calculated history under its model. BWM asks whether that calculated history corresponds to actual elapsed history.**

Avoid using `process age` as a blanket replacement for conventional dates unless the term is explicitly defined and warranted in the specific analysis.

## 4. Data and narrative

The phrase `data does not come with a narrative` is useful but can be overstated.

Preferred formulation:

> **Data does not interpret itself. What we observe constrains the story, sometimes very strongly, but moving from what exists now to what happened before also requires a model of the past.**

Some observations contain strong historical information. BWM should never imply that historical reconstruction is arbitrary or that all stories fit the evidence equally well.

## 5. Scripture and nature

BWM's interpretive order should be explicit:

> **Scripture provides revealed information about history that nature alone cannot supply. Nature provides real evidence that our interpretation must account for. Scripture interprets nature, while nature tests our scientific explanations of how the revealed history relates to the physical world.**

This keeps biblical authority fixed while leaving DFM, CHFM, and auxiliary physical explanations exposed to observational and quantitative testing.

The public shorthand remains:

> **God's Word. God's world.**

## 6. Every origins reconstruction has a starting state

Every origins model begins its physical reconstruction with something rather than nothing.

The question is not whether a model has a starting state. The question is:

> **From nothing to something, how far along the line of something is the world when the history being reconstructed begins?**

Conventional physical reconstructions begin with a state that already includes substantial specification: physical regularities/laws, fields or matter-energy content, constants/parameters, boundary conditions, and an unusually ordered early universe. Different cosmological models specify this boundary differently.

BWM permits a substantially richer created starting state because Scripture describes God creating an ordered world ready to fulfill appointed purposes.

The disagreement therefore includes the degree and character of structure present at the starting boundary.

## 7. Do not overconstrain Designed Functional Maturity

BWM must not use `God made it that way` as an unrestricted answer to difficult evidence. At the same time, DFM should not be forced to prove that every feature present at creation was narrowly necessary for one identifiable function before it may belong to the created state.

That would impose a stronger starting-state rule on BWM than is normally imposed on competing origins models.

Adopt this working standard:

> **The created starting state must be coherent with Scripture, sufficient for the world God intended, consistent with the world's lawful operation, and explanatorily defensible.**

Functional necessity is one possible warrant. It is not the only possible warrant.

Candidate categories for features of the created state include:

- directly required for an appointed function;
- required by coherent system-level initialization;
- part of the specified created state even where a narrow functional necessity is not yet known;
- uncertain and requiring further investigation.

Do not rule out a category merely to satisfy a methodological constraint that competing models do not bear.

## 8. Symmetry in model comparison

The governing methodological principle is **symmetry**.

BWM does not receive a free pass for difficult evidence. Naturalistic or conventional historical reconstructions do not receive a free pass for their own starting conditions, auxiliary assumptions, or unobserved mechanisms.

Avoid the asymmetric standard:

> Conventional model: `Here is the initial state required by the model. Now let the laws run.`  
> BWM: `Every feature in your initial state must first be independently derived from a separate functional necessity.`

Use the same comparative questions for each model:

1. **What starting state does the model posit?**
2. **What explains or warrants that state?**
3. **What laws, mechanisms, and auxiliary assumptions connect it to later states?**
4. **What history follows from it?**
5. **How well does the whole package account for the evidence it is intended to explain?**
6. **What observations create unresolved problems or count against it?**

For BWM, Scripture is additionally an authoritative source of historical information and therefore part of the evidence/constraint set the model is expressly intended to reconcile with nature.

## 9. Mature creation does not automatically predict every old-looking feature

Avoid the unrestricted statement:

> `If God made a mature world, old-looking data is expected.`

That is too broad at the present stage.

A mature Adam follows directly from the biblical account. Mature wine at Cana follows directly from the miracle. A created world must possess enough system-level maturity to operate as the world God intended.

Whether specific isotope distributions, fission tracks, impact records, stellar states, or other apparently historical features belong to the created starting state is a research question. They may have different explanations and should not all be placed into initialization by default.

The appropriate posture is investigation rather than prior exclusion.

## 10. Radiometric dating case-study ladder

Tier 2/3 should consider using an easy-case / hard-case teaching sequence.

### Mt. St. Helens dacite

The young dacite commonly discussed in creationist literature is useful as a demonstration of **starting-condition sensitivity**, especially inherited/excess argon. It should not be presented as a general disproof of radiometric dating. Conventional geochronology does not treat K-Ar dating of extremely young material as an ordinary high-confidence application where tiny radiogenic accumulation can be cleanly separated from inherited argon.

Teaching point:

> **A calculated date can be badly wrong when the starting assumptions required by the calculation do not hold.**

### Cardenas Basalt

The Cardenas Basalt is a harder test because multiple dating approaches have been used to support an ancient history. BWM should not dismiss this evidence or simply rename the result.

Teaching/research question:

> **The dating systems yield an ancient calculated history. Does that history correspond to actual elapsed history under the BWM starting state and subsequent Earth history?**

Candidate investigations include inherited components, mixing, system history, disturbance, and whether any relevant features plausibly belong to creation's starting state. If these do not provide an adequate account, the result remains an evidential problem for the model.

This easy-case / hard-case sequence is preferable to presenting only anomalies favorable to BWM.

## 11. Implications for the evidence ledger

The BWM comparative ledger should not count `has assumptions` or `has a starting state` as a debit unique to either side.

Instead evaluate:

- specification of the starting state;
- explanatory grounding of that state;
- generative sufficiency of the proposed history;
- dependence on auxiliary mechanisms;
- fit to observations;
- unresolved anomalies;
- ad hoc rescue behavior;
- successful discriminating predictions where available.

Operational science itself receives no partisan credit merely because a historical model uses it. Repeatable physical regularities are common evidence available to competing reconstructions.
