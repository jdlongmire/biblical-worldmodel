# Biblical WorldModel Narrative Tiers

This document defines the progressive-disclosure language architecture for WP-BWM-0005.

The public site should not ask every reader to understand the research programme before understanding the basic idea. Each tier has a different job.

## Tier 1 — The Story

**Audience:** first-time visitor, seeker, mixed biblical literacy, general reader.  
**Target:** very plain language; approximately grade 5–6 ceiling where practical.  
**Purpose:** comprehension and invitation.

### Rules

- Use everyday words first.
- Prefer one idea per sentence.
- Put concrete pictures before abstract explanation.
- Assume no training in theology, philosophy, geology, cosmology, or philosophy of science.
- Explain biblical scenes briefly rather than assuming familiarity.
- Do not use programme acronyms or formal model names in the narrative body.
- Do not use specialist terms such as retrodiction, epistemology, falsification, hydrotectonic, Bayesian, or initialization.
- Avoid `special revelation` and `natural revelation`; use `God's Word` and `God's world`.
- Avoid `procedural generation`; say that the computer builds the world by following rules.
- Explain scientific dating in ordinary terms before any technical label is introduced elsewhere.
- Keep the Bible's authority explicit rather than hiding the project's Christian starting point.
- State uncertainty plainly: `We do not have every answer.`

### Core pictures

1. Adam created as a grown man.
2. Water made into wine at Cana.
3. Scientist inside a newly generated video-game world.
4. World continues to develop after its beginning.
5. Noah's Flood as a later event that could alter the ground.

### Core questions

- **How much was already there when God made the world?**
- **What happened after that?**

## Tier 2 — How the Idea Works

**Audience:** interested general reader / high-school graduate ready to go deeper.  
**Purpose:** introduce the conceptual machinery after Tier 1 is understood.

Tier 2 may introduce, with immediate plain-English explanation:

- created ready to work / Designed Functional Maturity (DFM);
- starting conditions;
- working backward from present evidence;
- procedural generation as the technical name for the video-game analogy;
- observation versus historical reconstruction;
- Flood reconstruction and CHFM;
- the difference between calculated age and actual elapsed history.

Do not assume that a technical label explains the idea. Explain first, name second.

## Tier 3 — The Research Programmes

**Audience:** deep thinker, skeptic, reviewer, technically literate reader.  
**Purpose:** expose the actual programme architecture and evidential accounting.

Tier 3 may use normal educated-adult and technical vocabulary, including:

- DFM;
- CHFM;
- TRT/LRT;
- cosmology programmes;
- radiometric work;
- objections and responses ledgers;
- open problems;
- falsification controls;
- Bayesian/evidential accounting;
- canonical status and confidence labels.

Claims must route to canonical programme sources.

## Tier 4 — Canonical Research

**Audience:** researcher, specialist reviewer, formal critic.  
**Purpose:** full technical inspection.

No artificial reading-level constraint applies. Tier 4 includes equations, formal arguments, primary literature, calculations, code, datasets, severe tests, detailed objections, model revisions, and research provenance.

Tier 4 normally resides in or links directly to the Atlas-designated canonical research repositories rather than duplicating them in the public narrative layer.

## Promotion Rule

Material should enter the lowest tier that can communicate it truthfully without losing the needed meaning.

Do not promote technical vocabulary upward merely because it is familiar to the project team.

The reader should understand the idea before being asked to learn its project name.

## Drift Check

Before publishing or revising Tier 1, ask:

1. Can a general reader retell the main idea in their own words?
2. Does the piece use a technical term that can be replaced with an everyday phrase?
3. Does an abstract claim appear before the picture that makes it understandable?
4. Has a programme name or acronym entered the piece unnecessarily?
5. Does the reader need outside knowledge of a Bible story to follow the argument?
6. Did the revision add research-programme material that belongs in Tier 2 or Tier 3?

If yes to 2–6, revise before promotion.
