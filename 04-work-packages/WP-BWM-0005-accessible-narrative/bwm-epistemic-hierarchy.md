# BWM Epistemic Hierarchy

**Status:** working architecture for BWM narrative, objections, and evidential accounting. Canonicalization governance transferred to WP-BWM-0006.  
**Purpose:** distinguish levels of commitment so objections and failed hypotheses are applied to the level they actually address.

## Hierarchy

```text
BIBLICAL WORLDVIEW
        │
        ▼
BIBLICAL WORLDMODEL
        │
        ├── Interpretive commitments
        ├── Historical claims
        └── Boundary / starting conditions
        │
        ▼
RESEARCH PROGRAMMES
        │
        ├── DFM
        ├── CHFM
        ├── TRT / LRT
        └── companion programmes
        │
        ▼
MODELS / THEORIES
        │
        ├── specific mechanisms
        ├── quantitative relationships
        └── testable reconstructions
        │
        ▼
AUXILIARY HYPOTHESES
        │
        ├── supporting mechanisms
        ├── parameter assumptions
        └── local explanations
        │
        ▼
PREDICTIONS / TESTS
        │
        ▼
OBSERVATIONS
```

Observations are not merely the lowest ideological layer. They are the empirical interface against which predictions, hypotheses, models, and programmes are tested.

For compact classification, the canonicalization candidate uses bottom-up levels: **L0 Observation, L1 Prediction/Test, L2 Auxiliary Hypothesis, L3 Model/Theory, L4 Research Programme, L5 WorldModel, L6 Worldview.**

## 1. Biblical worldview

The worldview level contains the foundational Christian commitments from which BWM proceeds, including the Triune God, Christ the Logos, the authority of Scripture, creation as divine action, and the intelligibility and lawful character of God's world.

A failed physical auxiliary hypothesis does not directly falsify this level. A challenge to the worldview must address the relevant theological, philosophical, historical, or revelational claim at that level.

## 2. Biblical WorldModel

The WorldModel is the high-level interpretation of reality and history under the biblical worldview. It includes claims such as creation, Fall, Flood, subsequent history, and the relationship between God's Word and God's world.

The WorldModel constrains lower-level research by specifying historical and interpretive commitments. It does not itself specify every physical mechanism by which those historical events occurred.

A major direct contradiction or sustained cumulative conflict can challenge a WorldModel interpretation. Failure of one proposed mechanism does not automatically do so.

## 3. Research programmes

Research programmes organize families of models and hypotheses around a major explanatory problem.

Examples include:

- **DFM:** how a created world may begin sufficiently mature and coherent to perform its appointed purposes;
- **CHFM:** how the biblical Flood and its aftermath may relate to the physical Earth record;
- **TRT / LRT:** foundational work concerning logic, information, action, actualization, and ultimate grounding.

A programme is judged cumulatively. It may survive failure of an auxiliary hypothesis or particular model if viable alternatives remain and the programme continues to generate explanatory and discriminating work.

A programme becomes weaker if it repeatedly protects itself through after-the-fact rescue assumptions without increasing explanatory power, constraint, or successful prediction.

## 4. Models and theories

A model or theory specifies a more particular mechanism or reconstruction within a programme.

Examples may include a particular Flood tectonic sequence, a particular initialization model for an isotope system, or a quantitative cosmological realization of a broader DFM claim.

Models and theories should expose enough structure to permit comparison with evidence. A sufficiently clear contradiction can displace or falsify the specific model without automatically falsifying the programme containing it.

## 5. Auxiliary hypotheses

Auxiliary hypotheses support a model with local mechanisms, parameters, boundary assumptions, or explanatory details.

Examples might include:

- a proposed heat-removal mechanism;
- a particular pore-pressure condition;
- an assumed isotope starting state;
- a transport rate;
- a specific post-Flood cooling mechanism.

These are intentionally replaceable. Failure should normally be registered first against the auxiliary hypothesis actually tested.

Replacing an auxiliary is legitimate when independently warranted. Repeatedly inventing unconstrained auxiliaries solely to avoid contrary evidence is evidence of a degenerating research programme.

## 6. Predictions and tests

Predictions connect hypotheses and models to observations.

Tests should state, where practical:

- what is expected;
- under which model/hypothesis;
- what observation would count against the expectation;
- how strongly the result bears on the parent claim;
- what competing model predicts or permits.

A failed prediction updates the hypothesis or model that generated it. Its implications propagate upward only to the extent that higher levels depend on that claim.

## 7. Observations

Observations include measured isotope ratios, decay rates, mineral structures, stratigraphic contacts, fossil locations, spectra, temperatures, pressures, field relationships, and other empirical findings.

BWM should distinguish observations from the historical models used to explain them.

This distinction does not make observations weak. Some observations constrain historical explanations very strongly.

## Falsification propagation

The governing principle is:

> **Failure propagates upward according to dependency, not by automatic leap.**

An irregularity or failed prediction does not automatically falsify the highest-level claim in the hierarchy.

Example:

```text
Observation contradicts predicted heat removal
        ↓
Heat-removal auxiliary fails
        ↓
Can the physical model survive without it?
        │
        ├── YES → revise/replace auxiliary and retest
        │
        └── NO  → model is threatened or falsified
                    ↓
             Are viable models left in CHFM?
                    │
                    ├── YES → programme continues
                    │
                    └── NO  → CHFM faces major evidential failure
                                ↓
                         WorldModel interpretation receives
                         corresponding evidential pressure
```

Logical falsification and evidential pressure must be distinguished. Failure of CHFM would not logically entail that no Flood occurred. But repeated inability to produce a coherent physical account of a claimed global Flood would create genuine evidential pressure on the BWM interpretation of the Flood.

## Dependency and precommitment

A hierarchy must not become a device for demoting failed claims after the fact.

Working dependency rule:

> **A claim cannot be treated after failure as dispensable if the parent model or programme was previously committed to, or materially dependent upon, that claim.**

Before a severe test where practical, record the claim, its hierarchy level, expected result, parent dependencies, consequences of failure, and what competing frameworks predict or permit. This precommitment limits retrospective movement of claims between levels.

## Example: Flood hierarchy

### WorldModel historical claim

> A global Flood occurred substantially as Scripture describes.

### Research programme

CHFM asks what physical history could connect the pre-Flood world to the post-Flood world we observe.

### Model/theory

A particular reconstruction may propose crustal motion, sediment transport, water movement, thermal history, and post-Flood normalization.

### Auxiliary hypothesis

> Near-lithostatic pore pressure reduced effective friction sufficiently for a proposed motion.

### Test

Quantitatively evaluate whether the proposed pressure state, rheology, velocity, energy, and thermal consequences are mutually consistent.

### Possible result

If the calculation fails, the pore-pressure auxiliary or the model depending on it fails first. If all physically viable models capable of satisfying the programme's requirements fail, the evidential consequence rises through the hierarchy.

## Example: DFM hierarchy

### WorldModel claim

> God created the world in the manner Scripture describes.

### Interpretive consequence

> The created world began sufficiently mature to fulfill God's appointed purposes.

### Research programme

DFM investigates the implications of a functionally mature created starting state.

### Model or auxiliary hypothesis

> A particular isotope distribution was present in the created starting state.

That lower-level claim can fail without DFM automatically failing.

However, DFM becomes methodologically weaker if every difficult observation is moved into the created state only after it is encountered, without independent constraint or explanatory gain.

The created starting state is permitted to be rich. It must remain coherent with Scripture, sufficient for the world God intended, consistent with lawful operation, and explanatorily defensible.

## Symmetry with competing worldmodels

Apply the same hierarchy to UTE / conventional naturalistic reconstruction:

```text
NATURALISTIC / UTE WORLDVIEW COMMITMENTS
        ↓
UTE / CONVENTIONAL ORIGINS WORLDMODEL
        ↓
RESEARCH PROGRAMMES
        ↓
COSMOLOGICAL / GEOLOGICAL / BIOLOGICAL MODELS
        ↓
AUXILIARY HYPOTHESES
        ↓
PREDICTIONS / TESTS
        ↓
OBSERVATIONS
```

Failure of an inflationary model does not by itself falsify naturalism. Failure of one abiogenesis pathway does not falsify universal natural history. Failure of a dark-matter candidate does not falsify cosmology as a whole.

Researchers correctly allow revision at the level actually challenged.

BWM receives the same treatment.

This is the symmetry rule:

> **The same level of failure should have the same level of consequence across competing frameworks unless a specific dependency justifies a different result.**

## Objections-ledger rule

Every objection entered into a BWM objections/responses ledger should identify:

1. **Target level:** L0–L6 / observation, prediction/test, auxiliary hypothesis, model/theory, research programme, WorldModel, or worldview.
2. **Target claim:** the exact proposition under challenge.
3. **Evidence:** the observation, argument, calculation, or source supporting the objection.
4. **Dependency:** which higher-level claims actually depend on the challenged claim.
5. **Severity:** local, material, major, or potentially programme-threatening.
6. **Current disposition:** unanswered, partially answered, answered, auxiliary retired, model revised, model rejected, or escalated.
7. **Upward consequence:** what evidential pressure, if any, propagates to higher levels.
8. **Precommitment:** where one existed before the test.
9. **Operator disposition:** where canonical or programme status is changed.

An objection must not be represented as falsifying a higher level unless the dependency is shown.

Likewise, BWM must not quarantine a lower-level failure if the higher-level programme genuinely depends upon it.

## Two-direction flow

The hierarchy operates in both directions.

### Downward: constraint

Worldview and WorldModel commitments constrain which lower-level explanations are admissible within BWM.

```text
Worldview / revelation
        ↓
WorldModel interpretation
        ↓
Programme constraints
        ↓
Models and hypotheses
```

### Upward: evidential pressure

Observations test predictions and exert pressure upward according to dependency.

```text
Observations
        ↑
Predictions / tests
        ↑
Hypotheses and models
        ↑
Research programmes
        ↑
WorldModel interpretation
```

This prevents two opposite errors: allowing lower-level models to silently rewrite the project's revealed starting commitments, and insulating those models from evidence merely because they were built under a biblical WorldModel.

An **Operator** is an authorized human steward empowered to disposition BWM artifacts, interpretations, models, methodological changes, or canonical status within an explicitly assigned scope.

**J. D. Longmire is the Principal Operator for the Biblical WorldModel**, holding BWM-wide disposition authority. Other human collaborators may be designated **Collaborator Operators** with explicitly recorded scopes. Contribution or review alone does not imply Operator authority.
