# Candidate: Initialization Burden Taxonomy

Status: candidate under WP-BWM-0028  
Target owners: Integration and DFM BWM interface

## Purpose

Refine the source working paper's functional-maturity ladder so that initialized operational state is not conflated with initialized evidence of particular untraversed events.

This taxonomy extends the existing BWM distinction between functional maturity and chronicle-maturity.

## I0: Logical and nomic structure

The system is intelligible under determinate logical and lawful constraints.

Examples include logical consistency, physical law, constants, permitted state relations, and other nomic structure presupposed by subsequent dynamics.

This category concerns the rules and constraints under which a system operates, not an archive of prior events.

## I1: Physical and systemic boundary state

The system begins with physically instantiated values and structures required for the modeled system to exist and evolve.

Examples may include fields, matter/energy distributions, thermodynamic state, spatial relations, or other boundary conditions.

The explanatory burden is to specify and physically integrate the state.

## I2: Relational and operational initialization

Multiple components begin in coordinated relationships required for immediate system-level operation.

Examples may include mutually consistent system relationships, environmental coupling, or observationally relevant propagation states.

The burden increases where relational state maps strongly onto a unique antecedent history under ordinary dynamics.

## I3: Organismal and ecological functional maturity

Living systems or ecological systems begin capable of performing commissioned functions without traversing the ordinary developmental history one would infer from their mature state.

BWM's scriptural rationale for this category is evaluated through the DFM framework and Genesis interpretation. The category itself does not determine every physical implementation.

## I4: Chronicle-maturity / historical-record initialization

An initialized feature appears specifically to record one or more antecedent events that, under the proposed reconstruction, did not occur.

Examples in the source paper include tree-ring-like, depositional, clock-like, or other archive-shaped structures. Whether any concrete observation belongs here must be established case by case.

This is the highest-burden category because functional sufficiency alone does not explain why a determinate untraversed chronicle is encoded.

## Burden principle

> The stronger the initialized state resembles a specific record of untraversed events rather than a requirement of system function or integration, the stronger the independent justification required.

No category automatically licenses a proposed instance.

## Functional requirement test

For any DFM initialization claim, record:

1. What function or system relationship requires the proposed initialized state?
2. Could a materially different initialized state perform the same function?
3. Does the observation encode a determinate historical sequence beyond what function requires?
4. What expectation follows specifically from the proposed initialization?
5. What observation would count materially against it?
6. Is the ancillary independently motivated, or introduced only after contrary evidence?
7. Does it preserve cross-domain consistency with other initialized states?

## Starlight and record-like structure

The taxonomy does not pre-dispose distant-light questions. "Light already in transit" may involve relational/operational initialization, chronicle-like information, or both depending on the physical proposal. A DFM model must specify the information content and discriminating burden rather than resolving the issue by label.

Likewise, strata, isotope systems, tree rings, supernova remnants, and other candidate archives are not assigned wholesale to I4. Their status depends on what is observed, what is inferred, and what historical specificity the initialized-state hypothesis requires.

## Relationship to retrodictive age

An initialized state may map under a model to a retrodictive age `t_R`. This does not by itself establish actual elapsed age `t_H`.

Conversely, `t_R != necessarily t_H` does not establish initialization. DFM retains the burden to explain the observed state and cross-domain consilience.
