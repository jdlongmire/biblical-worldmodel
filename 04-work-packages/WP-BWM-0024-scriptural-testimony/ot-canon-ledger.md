# WP-BWM-0024 — Old Testament Canon Ledger

Status: active research
Date opened: 2026-09-21

## Purpose

Trace the recognition and boundaries of Jewish Scripture and the later Christian Old Testament using primary Jewish evidence first, then early Christian canonical evidence.

This stream is distinct from textual transmission. A manuscript can witness a text without proving that the community regarded the book as canonical.

## Guardrails

- Manuscript abundance at Qumran does not by itself define canon.
- “Septuagint” must not be treated as one first-century bound Bible with a fixed table of contents.
- Josephus's 22-book count is strong boundary evidence but does not name every book individually.
- 4 Ezra's 24 public books are important boundary evidence, but the work is pseudonymous and its versional textual history must be represented.
- Later rabbinic evidence must not be silently projected backward.
- Protestant, Catholic, and Orthodox OT boundaries must be described historically before theological evaluation.

## OT-CAN-001 — Qumran preserves biblical and non-biblical Jewish literature together

The Judean Desert/Qumran corpus includes approximately 200 biblical manuscripts encompassing almost all books of the later Hebrew Bible, alongside Tobit, Jubilees, 1 Enoch, sectarian works, and other Jewish literature.

**Relation:** manuscript/use evidence.  
**Confidence:** HIGH.

**What it establishes:** books later included in the Hebrew Bible were extensively copied and used in Second Temple Judaism alongside other religious literature.

**What it does not establish:** that every copied religious work had identical scriptural/canonical status.

Primary/institutional anchor:
- Israel Museum Digital Dead Sea Scrolls.

## OT-CAN-002 — Qumran demonstrates that canon and library contents are distinct questions

Because biblical, apocryphal/pseudepigraphal, commentary, and sectarian works occur in the same archaeological manuscript corpus, physical presence in a religious library cannot itself function as a canon criterion.

**Relation:** methodological inference from primary manuscript corpus.  
**Confidence:** HIGH.

**STP significance:** the same rule applied to Sinaiticus/Alexandrinus must be applied consistently to Qumran.

## OT-CAN-003 — Josephus reports a bounded collection of 22 divine books

In *Against Apion* 1.37–43, Josephus contrasts Jewish Scripture with an indefinite multitude of competing Greek books and states that Jews possess only twenty-two books “justly believed to be divine.”

He divides these as:
- five books of Moses;
- thirteen books by prophets covering the period from Moses to Artaxerxes;
- four books containing hymns to God and precepts for human conduct.

**Relation:** direct late-first-century Jewish boundary testimony.  
**Confidence:** HIGH.

Primary anchor:
- Josephus, *Against Apion* 1.37–43.

## OT-CAN-004 — Josephus distinguishes later historical writing from the authoritative collection

Josephus states that Jewish history continued to be written after Artaxerxes but that later writings were not regarded with the same authority because the exact succession of prophets had ceased.

**Relation:** direct evidence for a perceived canonical/prophetic boundary.  
**Confidence:** HIGH.

**Significance:** Josephus's criterion is not merely “old books”; he connects the authoritative corpus with prophetic succession.

## OT-CAN-005 — Josephus testifies to strong Jewish commitment to the bounded books

Josephus claims Jews neither add to, subtract from, nor alter the authoritative books and describes willingness to suffer for them.

**Relation:** direct evidence for Josephus's account of Jewish scriptural commitment.  
**Confidence:** HIGH as testimony to Josephus's claim; MEDIUM-HIGH for universalizing the behavior to every Jewish group.

**Qualification:** Josephus is writing apologetically against Greek critics. His rhetoric and generalizations require historical context.

## OT-CAN-006 — Josephus's 22-book count does not by itself identify every modern book mapping

Josephus does not enumerate the individual titles in *Against Apion*. Reconstructions mapping his 22 books to the later 24-book Hebrew collection generally depend on combining books such as Ruth/Judges and Lamentations/Jeremiah, but the precise mapping is an inference.

**Relation:** source-discipline qualification.  
**Confidence:** HIGH.

**BWM rule:** say “22-book bounded collection” unless the mapping is independently supported.

## OT-CAN-007 — 4 Ezra distinguishes 24 public books from 70 esoteric books

4 Ezra 14 describes Ezra's scribes producing ninety-four books in the Syriac/Ethiopic and related textual tradition: twenty-four are to be published for general reading, while seventy are reserved for the wise.

**Relation:** Jewish apocalyptic evidence for a numbered public scriptural collection.  
**Confidence:** HIGH for the 24/70 distinction in the relevant versional tradition; MEDIUM-HIGH for exact reconstruction across all versions.

**Qualification:** the Latin textual tradition contains numerical corruption. The versional evidence must be cited explicitly.

## OT-CAN-008 — Josephus and 4 Ezra independently witness a bounded Jewish collection around the turn of the era

Josephus gives 22 authoritative books; 4 Ezra gives 24 public books. Different counting conventions can explain a 22/24 difference, but neither source individually supplies the full title mapping needed to prove identity.

**Relation:** cumulative boundary evidence.  
**Confidence:** HIGH that both sources envision a bounded collection; MEDIUM-HIGH that the collections are extensionally identical in every title.

**STP significance:** the evidence supports a substantially bounded Jewish scriptural corpus by the late first/early second century without requiring us to overstate exact-title certainty.

## Initial assessment

The first OT canon pass supports a careful model:

```text
Second Temple period:
    extensive scriptural use
    + broader religious literary ecosystem
        ↓
late 1st / early 2nd century:
    explicit numbered Jewish collections
    (Josephus 22; 4 Ezra 24)
        ↓
later Jewish and Christian witnesses:
    title-level lists and divergent reception histories
```

The appropriate provisional claim is:

> **By the late first century, Jewish sources provide explicit evidence for a bounded body of authoritative Scripture, while the Qumran library shows that authoritative Scripture existed within a much larger religious literary environment. The precise title-level mapping and later Christian Old Testament boundaries require further evidence.**
