# WP-BWM-0024 — Major New Testament Variant Ledger

Status: active research
Date opened: 2026-09-21

## Purpose

Make the strongest and most familiar New Testament textual problems explicit before BWM makes any public claim about textual confidence.

The governing rule is:

> **Textual confidence is strengthened by identifying uncertainty accurately, not by pretending uncertainty does not exist.**

This ledger asks four questions for each variant:

1. What is the documentary problem?
2. How substantial is the uncertainty?
3. Can the disputed wording be identified transparently in modern critical texts/translations?
4. What doctrine or historical claim, if any, materially depends upon the disputed wording?

The final question is theological and must be answered by canonical cross-reference, not by assuming in advance that every variant is doctrinally inconsequential.

## Variant classes

- **Class A — Extended passage:** multiple verses / substantial narrative unit.
- **Class B — Significant sentence or clause:** meaningful unit with substantial external/internal evidence on both sides.
- **Class C — Local wording:** smaller reading that materially affects translation or interpretation.
- **Class D — Late secondary expansion:** historically influential wording with weak early documentary support.

## Initial major variants

### STP-VAR-001 — Mark 16:9–20, the Longer Ending

**Class:** A — extended passage.

The ending after Mark 16:8 is one of the two largest textual problems in the New Testament. Early major Greek witnesses omit the Longer Ending, while it became widespread in the later manuscript tradition. Paratextual evidence is also relevant: the early Eusebian canon system did not originally provide sections for material after Mark 16:8.

**Working judgment:** the evidence against Mark 16:9–20 belonging to the earliest recoverable form of Mark is substantial.

**Confidence in textual judgment:** HIGH.

**Required qualification:** the transmission history is more complicated than “all early manuscripts omit it.” Alternative endings and scribal awareness of multiple endings must be represented in the specialist pass.

**Doctrinal dependency audit:** required. No doctrine may be declared unaffected until the propositions in the Longer Ending are mapped against undisputed canonical loci.

Primary/specialist anchors:
- early Greek manuscript witnesses via INTF/NTVMR;
- Eusebian canon evidence documented by CSNTM;
- current critical editions and specialist commentaries.

### STP-VAR-002 — John 7:53–8:11, the Pericope Adulterae

**Class:** A — extended passage.

The story of the woman caught in adultery is absent from the earliest Greek manuscript evidence and appears at multiple locations in later transmission, including locations in Luke and elsewhere in John. This “floating” location is significant evidence concerning its secondary insertion into the Gospel tradition.

CSNTM notes that no known New Testament manuscript through the fourth century contains the passage and identifies Codex Bezae, fifth century, as the oldest known Greek manuscript containing it.

**Working judgment:** John 7:53–8:11 was almost certainly not part of the earliest recoverable text of John's Gospel at this location.

**Confidence in textual judgment:** HIGH.

**Historical-status qualification:** a judgment that the passage is textually secondary to John does not by itself determine whether the underlying episode preserves an authentic Jesus tradition. Those are separate historical questions.

**Doctrinal dependency audit:** required.

Primary/specialist anchors:
- P66 and other early Johannine witnesses;
- Codex Bezae;
- CSNTM manuscript studies;
- critical editions.

### STP-VAR-003 — Luke 22:43–44, angel and sweat like drops of blood

**Class:** B — significant two-verse unit.

The verses describing an angel strengthening Jesus and his sweat becoming like drops of blood have a complex manuscript history. They are absent from significant ancient witnesses, present in others, marked with critical signs in some, and transposed in portions of the tradition.

Published scholarship explicitly treats the external evidence as complex, with arguments both for omission and for inclusion in the earliest text.

**Working judgment:** genuine textual uncertainty remains substantial.

**Confidence in textual judgment:** MEDIUM.

**STP significance:** this is precisely the sort of passage that should prevent overclaiming a uniformly certain reconstructed text at every verse.

**Doctrinal dependency audit:** required, particularly Christological claims sometimes invoked in proposed explanations for addition or omission.

Specialist anchor:
- Claire Clivaz, “The Angel and the Sweat Like ‘Drops of Blood’ (Lk 22:43–44),” *Harvard Theological Review* 98.4 (2005), 419–440.

### STP-VAR-004 — Luke 23:34a, “Father, forgive them”

**Class:** B — significant clause.

The prayer “Father, forgive them, for they know not what they do” has divided early documentary support and is bracketed or otherwise marked in critical treatments.

**Working judgment:** meaningful uncertainty remains concerning whether the saying stood in the earliest recoverable text of Luke.

**Confidence in textual judgment:** MEDIUM.

**Required research:** collate the principal Greek witnesses, early versions, patristic citations, and proposed transcriptional/exegetical motivations before assigning a stronger judgment.

**Doctrinal dependency audit:** forgiveness of enemies and Jesus's teaching on forgiveness must be mapped against undisputed passages separately from the textual judgment here.

### STP-VAR-005 — 1 John 5:7–8, the Comma Johanneum

**Class:** D — late secondary expansion.

The expanded Trinitarian wording familiar from later forms of the Textus Receptus has extremely weak Greek manuscript support and entered the Greek printed tradition late, despite a substantial history in the Latin tradition.

**Working judgment:** the Comma Johanneum is not part of the earliest recoverable Greek text of 1 John.

**Confidence in textual judgment:** HIGH.

**STP significance:** BWM should state this plainly. The doctrine of the Trinity must be grounded canonically rather than defended by retaining a textually secondary expansion.

**Required research:** trace the Latin transmission, Erasmus/printed Greek history, and the small number of late Greek witnesses.

### STP-VAR-006 — Acts 8:37, the Ethiopian eunuch's confession

**Class:** B/D — substantial sentence with secondary textual history.

The explicit confession before baptism is absent from important early Greek witnesses and appears in portions of the later tradition, with evidence for an early underlying tradition outside the principal Greek manuscript line.

**Working judgment:** probably secondary to the earliest recoverable text of Acts.

**Confidence in textual judgment:** MEDIUM-HIGH pending full patristic/versional audit.

**Doctrinal dependency audit:** baptismal confession and faith/baptism relations must be evaluated from undisputed canonical passages independently.

### STP-VAR-007 — John 1:18, monogenēs theos / monogenēs huios

**Class:** C — local wording with Christological significance.

Ancient witnesses divide over readings commonly represented as “only/unique God” and “only/unique Son.” The variation affects translation and the precise form of John's closing statement in the prologue.

**Working judgment:** significant local variant; modern critical editions generally favor theos, but the evidence and translation issues warrant explicit treatment.

**Confidence in textual judgment:** MEDIUM-HIGH.

**Doctrinal dependency audit:** Johannine Christology must not be made dependent upon resolution of this single reading.

### STP-VAR-008 — Romans 5:1, echomen / echōmen

**Class:** C — local wording.

A one-letter difference produces either an indicative sense (“we have peace”) or hortatory subjunctive (“let us have peace”). This is an excellent example of a very small scribal/orthographic difference with a real translation effect.

**Working judgment:** local uncertainty with limited scope.

**Confidence in textual judgment:** MEDIUM-HIGH.

**STP significance:** “small variant” does not necessarily mean “no semantic effect,” while semantic effect does not necessarily imply major doctrinal instability.

### STP-VAR-009 — Revelation 13:18, 666 / 616

**Class:** C — local numerical reading.

The number of the beast is transmitted principally as 666, while ancient evidence also attests 616. Early papyrus evidence makes 616 historically significant rather than a purely medieval anomaly.

**Working judgment:** 666 has the stronger claim to the initial text, while 616 is an ancient variant requiring interpretation.

**Confidence in textual judgment:** MEDIUM-HIGH.

**Required research:** collate P115, P47, Codex Alexandrinus, Irenaeus, and other early evidence.

### STP-VAR-010 — Matthew 6:13, doxology of the Lord's Prayer

**Class:** B/D — liturgical expansion.

The familiar doxology “For yours is the kingdom and the power and the glory forever” is absent from important early witnesses and appears in varying forms in later manuscript and liturgical traditions.

**Working judgment:** the doxology is probably secondary to the earliest recoverable text of Matthew.

**Confidence in textual judgment:** HIGH.

**Qualification:** its liturgical antiquity and theological orthodoxy are distinct from its textual originality in Matthew.

## Initial assessment

These variants already establish several important propositions.

### 1. Some New Testament passages remain genuinely uncertain

The responsible confidence case cannot claim that every word of the earliest recoverable text is equally certain.

### 2. The largest uncertainties are identifiable

The two largest extended Gospel passages, Mark 16:9–20 and John 7:53–8:11, are not hidden discoveries. They are prominent, extensively studied textual problems and are normally marked in modern translations.

### 3. Textual and historical authenticity are distinct

A narrative may be secondary to a particular Gospel's earliest text while still raising a separate question about whether it preserves an early or authentic historical tradition.

### 4. Doctrinal stability must be demonstrated canonically

BWM should not repeat the apologetic slogan “no doctrine is affected by any textual variant” without an explicit audit. A better test is:

```text
For each disputed reading:
    proposition(s) uniquely supplied by variant
        ↓
    compare with undisputed canonical loci
        ↓
    determine whether any doctrine depends uniquely upon variant
```

That makes the claim falsifiable and auditable.

### 5. Transparency is part of the confidence argument

The manuscript tradition preserves enough evidence to identify major secondary additions and genuine uncertainty. The fact that Mark 16, John 8, the Comma Johanneum, and other cases can be isolated is evidence of textual-critical resolution, even though it also demonstrates that transmission was not mechanically perfect.
