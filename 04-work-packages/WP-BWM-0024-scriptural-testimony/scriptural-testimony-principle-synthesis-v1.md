## 1. Principle

The **Scriptural Testimony Principle (STP)** is a BWM hard-core commitment:

> **Scripture is truthful divine revelation and therefore provides reliable testimony concerning reality wherever it speaks, including matters of history, human nature, moral order, creation, and divine action. Because the same God is the author of Scripture and Creator of the natural world, genuine revelation and genuine observation cannot ultimately conflict. Apparent conflicts call for examination of our interpretation of Scripture, our interpretation of nature, or both.**

This is the confessional starting point of BWM.

WP-BWM-0024 asks a second question for readers who do not begin by granting that commitment:

> **Is confidence in Scripture as an explanatory source rationally warranted by its observable contact with reality and by ordinary principles of knowledge and historical inference?**

The cumulative case is intended to answer that question positively without pretending to derive divine inspiration from archaeology, psychology, or textual criticism.

## 4. Textual recoverability

Tranche B distinguishes:

```text
Recoverability of the text
        ≠
Historical truth of the text
        ≠
Divine inspiration of the text
```

The Hebrew Bible manuscript record shows:
- substantial pre-medieval manuscript evidence;
- long-range continuity;
- genuine textual plurality;
- comparative recoverability.

The New Testament record shows:
- thousands of catalogued Greek witnesses;

- extensive visible variation;
- critical reconstruction through manuscript comparison;
- explicit identification of disputed readings.

The audit found:

> **No core Christian doctrine in the audited set depends uniquely upon accepting a disputed reading.**

This does not mean variants are unimportant. They can materially affect exegesis, translation, liturgy, rhetoric, history, and particular practices.

### Quantitative discipline

BWM rejects underdefined slogans such as:
- “99.5% accurate”;
- exact unqualified manuscript counts;
- “400,000 variants” without definition;
- “all variants are spelling errors”;
- simplistic manuscript-number comparisons with classical works.

### Bounded conclusion

> **The biblical manuscript traditions contain real variation, but enough evidence survives to expose that variation, compare witnesses, reconstruct the text in detail, and identify remaining uncertainty transparently.**

## 5. Canon and reception

The canon research distinguishes:

```text
Composition → Circulation → Use → Recognition → Collection → Canon Lists
```

### New Testament

The evidence supports:
- a visible late-second-century fourfold Gospel and apostolic core;
- continuing disagreement over a smaller set of books;
- progressive convergence;
- exact 27-book lists in the fourth century;
- regional differences, including Syriac reception.

The best historical model is:

> **progressive recognition and boundary consolidation around an earlier stable core.**

The evidence does not support a single late council creating the New Testament from scratch.

### Old Testament

Jewish evidence near the turn of the era provides:
- Josephus's bounded 22 divine books;
- 4 Ezra's 24 public books;
- Qumran evidence of scriptural books within a broader religious library.

Christian witnesses subsequently show:
- repeated Hebrew-sized or Hebrew-oriented collections;
- broad Greek Christian use of additional Jewish books;
- differing late-fourth-century criteria in Jerome and Augustine;
- later Protestant, Catholic, and Orthodox divergence.

### Bounded conclusion

> **Both Testaments show histories of transmission and recognition that contain real boundary questions while also displaying substantial inherited cores.**

## 7. Testimony as knowledge

Tranche D establishes that testimony is an ordinary epistemological category.

Reductionist, non-reductionist, and hybrid theories disagree about the source of testimonial warrant, but they do not treat all testimony as intrinsically epistemically worthless.

Much ordinary, scientific, and historical knowledge depends upon reliance on others.

Therefore:

> **Biblical testimony should be assessed for credibility, provenance, interpretation, evidence, and defeaters rather than excluded merely because it is testimony.**

## 8. Historical inference

The past is reconstructed from surviving evidence.

```text
present evidence
+
background knowledge
+
initial-condition assumptions
+
causal models
+
testimony / records
        ↓

```

Historical inference is constrained rather than arbitrary.

This matters especially for origins because present observations are not identical to the histories proposed to explain them.

## 9. Naturalism

BWM distinguishes:

```text
methodological naturalism
        ≠
metaphysical naturalism
```

A method restricted to natural causal explanations can be highly successful without logically demonstrating that natural causes exhaust reality.

Sophisticated metaphysical naturalism has additional arguments, including causal closure, explanatory success, parsimony, and dependence relations. Those must be engaged on their merits.

BWM therefore rejects both:
- naturalistic ontology smuggled in as mere scientific method;
- supernatural causation inserted merely because a natural explanation is incomplete.

## 10. Admissibility and acceptance

A testimonial claim can be **admissible** without being **accepted**.

Minimal admissibility requires that the proposition be intelligible, relevant, sufficiently attributable to a source, and not excluded merely by assuming the negation of the worldview claim under dispute.

Acceptance requires evaluation of:
- source credibility;
- independent evidence;
- background coherence;
- explanatory contribution;
- defeaters;
- competing explanations.

This permits a non-Christian reader to consider biblical testimony rationally without first granting inspiration.

## 11. Genesis and initial conditions

The bridge to BWM is now precise.

Let (S_t) be a present observed state and (A) the assumptions used to reconstruct its history:

```text
R(S_t, A) → H
```

A retrodictive age can be represented:

```text
A_r = f(S_t | I, L, P, C)
```

where:
- (I) = initial conditions;
- (L) = governing process relations;
- (P) = historical process regime;
- (C) = continuity assumptions.

A correct conditional calculation does not by itself establish that the inferred history occurred.

If credible testimony (T) supplies information concerning initial conditions or historical interventions:

```text
R(S_t, A, T) → H_2
```

The testimony becomes another constraint to reconcile with observation.

## 12. Designed Functional Maturity

If a system begins at (t_0) in a mature functional state (S_0), and later develops under ordinary process (F):

```text
S_t = F^(t-t_0)(S_0)
```

then extrapolating (F) backward beyond (S_0) can generate a hypothetical prehistory that never occurred.

Adam and Cana provide canonical examples if interpreted as BWM does:
- genuine mature state;
- genuine observable properties;
- absent ordinary developmental history.

They establish the conceptual possibility within the Christian worldview.

They do **not** prove that stars, rocks, isotopic systems, or biological structures were initialized analogously.

## 13. BWM's burden

STP establishes neither a young Earth nor DFM automatically.

BWM bears two burdens:

### Epistemic burden

Show that present state does not uniquely entail ordinary-process prehistory and that Scriptural testimony is rationally admissible.

### Model burden

Construct specific models constrained by Scripture that account for actual observations competitively and without unconstrained auxiliary assumptions.


## 14. Hard core and protective belt

### Hard core

- God is the Creator of reality.
- Scripture is truthful divine revelation.
- God's Word and God's world cannot ultimately conflict.
- Scripture is a legitimate source of testimony concerning reality and history.

### Protective belt

## 15. Providential Non-Preservation Hypothesis

A related theological hypothesis proposes:

> God may have providentially preserved the truth of His Word through a distributed manuscript tradition while permitting the physical autographs to disappear, thereby keeping authority attached to the communicated Word rather than a unique sacred artifact.

Biblical patterns such as the bronze serpent and Jeremiah 36 make the artifact/revelation distinction coherent.

The proposed divine purpose concerning the autographs remains a hypothesis, not explicit biblical teaching.

## 16. Confidence statement

The cumulative case can now be stated:

> **Confidence in Scripture does not rest on a single archaeological artifact, manuscript statistic, psychological correlation, or philosophical argument. The Bible demonstrates repeated historical contact with independently recoverable reality; its text survives in rich and auditable manuscript traditions; its canonical history shows inherited cores alongside visible boundary disputes; its account of human beings continues to correspond substantially with independently observed human behavior; and ordinary epistemology recognizes testimony as a legitimate source of knowledge. Taken together, these strands provide rational cumulative warrant for treating Scripture as a serious explanatory source concerning reality.**
>
> **When Scripture speaks about origins, it therefore should not be excluded merely because no human observer was present or because its account includes divine action. Its testimony may rationally enter the historical reconstruction as evidence concerning initial conditions and early history. BWM then bears the burden of showing that models constrained by that testimony can account for the world we actually observe.**

## 17. Public-facing concise form

### Why trust the Bible about the beginning?

We trust sources partly because of how reliably they describe reality where we can check them.

The Bible speaks about history, human nature, wisdom, relationships, suffering, morality, and the world we inhabit. Across those domains it repeatedly demonstrates serious contact with reality.

Its text has also survived through an unusually rich manuscript tradition. The manuscripts contain real differences, and those differences can be examined rather than hidden.

Genesis then speaks about something none of us directly observed: the beginning.

If Scripture proves worthy of confidence where its claims can be investigated, it is reasonable to take seriously its testimony concerning history we cannot directly observe.

That does not settle every question about origins.

It gives us another source of evidence.

**That is where the Biblical WorldModel begins.**
