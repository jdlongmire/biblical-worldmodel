# WP-BWM-0024 — New Testament Quantitative Claims Audit

Status: active research
Date opened: 2026-09-21

## Purpose

Audit common numerical claims used in popular defenses or critiques of New Testament textual transmission before BWM repeats them.

The governing rule is:

> **A number without a defined numerator, denominator, counting method, date, and evidentiary significance is rhetoric, not evidence.**

## Q-001 — “There are 5,800+ Greek New Testament manuscripts”

**Verdict:** usable only with qualification.

INTF operates the international official recording list of Greek New Testament manuscripts and continually evaluates new finds and catalogue entries. INTF's current public methodological material speaks of roughly 5,700 extant manuscripts; other recent counts based on Liste identifiers can exceed 6,000 because identifiers are not equivalent to extant independent manuscript objects.

Complications include:
- destroyed or lost manuscripts that retain catalogue numbers;
- duplicate catalogue entries later shown to belong to one manuscript;
- one physical codex carrying more than one Gregory-Aland number;
- manuscripts containing multiple textual layers;
- lectionaries and continuous-text manuscripts;
- newly catalogued and de-registered witnesses.

**BWM rule:** never present a precise manuscript count without date and counting definition.

**Preferred public language:** “roughly 5,700 extant catalogued Greek New Testament manuscripts, with the exact count depending on how catalogue entries and physical manuscripts are counted.”

**Confidence:** HIGH.

Primary/specialist anchors:
- INTF official Liste / institute documentation;
- INTF CBGM overview;
- CSNTM “Ask the Prof: How many Greek manuscripts…?”

## Q-002 — “There are 400,000 textual variants”

**Verdict:** outdated/underspecified as a precise claim.

Peter J. Gurry's peer-reviewed estimate, based on extensive collation data and excluding spelling differences, is approximately **half a million** Greek manuscript variants. His definition counts a word or sequence differing from another manuscript in a comparable segment, excluding spelling and nomen-sacrum abbreviation differences.

The estimate is not a count of 500,000 places where the New Testament is uncertain.

Gurry reports that a very large share of variants occur in only one manuscript and many have little or no claim to originality.

**BWM rule:** do not use “400,000 variants” without defining “variant.” Prefer the current scholarly estimate and explain what it measures.

**Preferred public language:** “One modern estimate identifies roughly half a million non-spelling textual variants across the surviving Greek manuscript tradition. The number grows with the amount of manuscript evidence and does not represent half a million uncertain passages.”

**Confidence:** HIGH.

Primary specialist anchor:
- Peter J. Gurry, “The Number of Variants in the Greek New Testament: A Proposed Estimate,” *New Testament Studies* 62 (2016), 97–121.

## Q-003 — “There are more variants than words in the New Testament”

**Verdict:** mathematically true under common counting conventions but rhetorically misleading without explanation.

The comparison places two unlike quantities side by side:
- total words in one reconstructed Greek New Testament text;
- accumulated differences across thousands of manuscript witnesses copied over many centuries.

A single copying error repeated independently or across descendants contributes to the variant evidence while the base text length does not grow.

**BWM rule:** if the comparison is mentioned, immediately explain the category difference.

**Confidence:** HIGH.

## Q-004 — “The New Testament is 99.5% accurate / pure”

**Verdict:** DO NOT USE as a BWM evidentiary statistic unless a reproducible method and denominator are supplied.

The percentage circulates widely in apologetic literature, but “accuracy” or “purity” can mean several different things:
- percentage of words whose initial reading is certain;
- percentage of variation units judged secure;
- percentage of text unaffected by “meaningful and viable” variants;
- agreement between a printed edition and a hypothetical autograph.

Those are not interchangeable.

A percentage also depends on tokenization, edition, variant definition, weighting, and what counts as uncertainty.

**BWM rule:** retire the 99.5% formulation from the public case unless future research produces a clearly reproducible metric.

**Preferred language:** “Most textual differences are readily resolved, while a smaller set remains genuinely disputed. BWM identifies the important disputed readings rather than converting textual confidence into a spurious precision percentage.”

**Confidence:** HIGH that the popular percentage is methodologically underdefined.

## Q-005 — “Most variants are insignificant”

**Verdict:** broadly supportable if “insignificant” is defined.

Gurry's research and subsequent discussion indicate that many variants:
- occur only in one witness;
- are nonsensical copying errors;
- have no plausible claim to the initial text;
- make little or no difference to translation.

But some variants clearly affect meaning, interpretation, translation, theology, or practice. The major-variant ledger already documents examples.

**BWM rule:** say “most variants create little or no serious uncertainty about the initial wording,” then explicitly acknowledge the important minority.

**Confidence:** HIGH.

## Q-006 — “No doctrine is affected by textual variants”

**Verdict:** reject as stated; replace with audited dependency language.

Variants can affect the wording and exegetical support used in doctrinal argument. The relevant question is whether a core doctrine **depends uniquely** on a disputed reading.

The WP-BWM-0024 doctrine-dependency audit currently samples twenty substantial variants and has found no core doctrine uniquely dependent upon accepting the disputed reading.

**Preferred public language:** “Textual variants can affect interpretation and individual proof texts. In the major variants audited so far, no core Christian doctrine depends uniquely on the disputed wording.”

**Confidence:** MEDIUM-HIGH, bounded to the current audit.

## Q-007 — “The more manuscripts, the more variants”

**Verdict:** directionally correct and important.

More manuscript witnesses create more opportunities to observe copying differences. Gurry's estimate is therefore partly a consequence of the frequency and scale of copying. Variant totals must be interpreted alongside manuscript abundance and relationships.

This does not mean more manuscripts automatically produce greater certainty at every passage. It means raw variant count cannot function independently as a corruption metric.

**Confidence:** HIGH.

## Q-008 — “No two manuscripts agree exactly”

**Verdict:** supportable at the broad tradition level but should not be turned into an absolute slogan without scope.

INTF states that each manuscript has its own variant profile and that no single copy agrees precisely with another one in the manuscript comparisons underlying its CBGM discussion.

**BWM rule:** use only with an INTF-defined scope, and explain that unique variant profiles coexist with broad textual coherence.

**Confidence:** HIGH within INTF's methodological framing.

## Q-009 — “Only about 10% of variation units show a significant split”

**Verdict:** useful specialist statistic with defined scope, not a universal percentage of the New Testament.

INTF's CBGM overview states that a significant split of the tradition occurs in about ten percent of variation units in the relevant comprehensive apparatus context.

**BWM rule:** do not convert this into “90% of the New Testament is certain.” Variation units and total text are different denominators.

**Confidence:** HIGH for the INTF statement; scope must accompany use.

## Q-010 — “The New Testament has far more manuscripts than any other ancient work”

**Verdict:** likely broadly true for manuscript abundance, but comparisons are routinely oversimplified.

A responsible comparison must control for:
- language;
- date range;
- fragments versus complete manuscripts;
- continuous texts versus quotations/versions;
- manuscript survival conditions;
- work length;
- whether medieval witnesses are included;
- what counts as an independent copy.

**BWM rule:** do not use simplistic tables comparing “5,800 NT manuscripts” against tiny single-number counts for Homer, Caesar, Tacitus, Plato, etc. until each comparator is independently sourced using the same counting rules.

## Quantitative baseline

The audit supports this public formulation:

> **The New Testament is preserved in thousands of Greek manuscript witnesses. Their abundance exposes a very large number of copying differences, probably around half a million non-spelling variants across the Greek tradition by one modern scholarly estimate. Most do not create serious uncertainty about the initial wording, while a smaller set does require substantive textual judgment. The important cases can be identified and examined directly.**

This is more informative than either:
- “99.5% pure,” or
- “there are more variants than words.”

Both slogans compress a multidimensional textual tradition into a rhetorically convenient number.

## Claims prohibited pending further evidence

BWM public materials should not currently state:

- “The New Testament is 99.5% accurate.”
- “The New Testament is 99.9% accurate.”
- “There are exactly 5,800 Greek manuscripts.”
- “There are exactly 400,000 variants.”
- “No doctrine is affected by any variant.”
- “All variants are spelling errors.”
- “The Bible has X times more manuscripts than the next-best ancient work.”
- “The original text is 100% certain.”

Any future quantitative claim must identify source, date, counting method, and denominator.
