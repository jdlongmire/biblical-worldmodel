# WP-BWM-0024 — Tranche D: Testimony, Naturalism, and Historical Inference

Status: active research
Date opened: 2026-09-21

## Purpose

Build the philosophical bridge from the cumulative Scriptural Testimony Principle (STP) evidence to BWM's use of Scripture in historical reconstruction.

The target conclusion is deliberately bounded:

> **Scriptural testimony cannot be excluded from historical consideration merely because some of its claims involve divine action. Admissibility as evidence is distinct from automatic acceptance as true.**

## Core distinctions

### Testimony versus proof

```text
testimony = a source of information offered by another knower
credibility = warrant for relying on that testimony
truth = whether the testified proposition corresponds to reality
```

Treating testimony as epistemically admissible does not entail accepting every testimony.

### Methodological versus metaphysical naturalism

```text
Methodological naturalism:
    a rule/constraint governing the kinds of explanations
    admitted within a scientific method

Metaphysical naturalism:
    an ontological claim that reality contains no supernatural
    beings, causes, or domains
```

Success under the first does not deductively entail the second.

### Observation versus historical reconstruction

```text
present evidence
    +
background knowledge
    +
initial-condition assumptions
    +
causal model
    +
testimony / records where available
        ↓
historical reconstruction
```

The reconstruction is an inference from evidence, not direct observation of the past.

## D1 — Testimony is an ordinary source of knowledge

Contemporary epistemology treats testimony as a central source of human knowledge. Much that any individual knows about history, geography, science, institutions, other persons, and events outside direct experience depends upon testimony.

The philosophical dispute is primarily over **how testimonial warrant works**, not whether all testimony is epistemically worthless.

### STP-EPI-001 — Reductionism

Reductionist theories hold, roughly, that testimonial justification depends on non-testimonial positive reasons such as evidence for the speaker's reliability or testimony in general.

**Relation to STP:** this model makes the cumulative reliability case in Tranches A–C especially relevant: demonstrated historical contact, textual recoverability, and explanatory correspondence can function as positive reasons for trusting testimony.

### STP-EPI-002 — Non-reductionism

Non-reductionist theories hold, roughly, that hearers can possess prima facie entitlement to accept testimony absent relevant defeaters, rather than independently proving speaker reliability for every testimonial belief.

**Relation to STP:** Scripture need not uniquely satisfy a burden that ordinary testimonial knowledge never bears.

### STP-EPI-003 — Hybrid and interpersonal accounts

Contemporary testimony literature contains hybrid, assurance, interpersonal, and social-epistemological models that complicate the reductionist/non-reductionist binary.

**BWM rule:** do not claim “epistemology proves testimony is basic.” Present the live positions and show that all take testimonial knowledge seriously.

Primary scholarly orientation:
- Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy, “Epistemological Problems of Testimony”;
- C.A.J. Coady, *Testimony: A Philosophical Study*;
- Elizabeth Fricker;
- Tyler Burge;
- Jennifer Lackey.

## D2 — Human knowledge is testimonial at scale

### STP-EPI-004 — Epistemic dependence is unavoidable

No individual independently verifies most propositions used in ordinary or specialist reasoning. Scientific knowledge itself depends on reports, instruments operated by others, published methods/results, calibration chains, institutional trust, and accumulated prior research.

**Relation:** philosophical/social-epistemic support for testimony as a normal knowledge channel.  
**Confidence:** HIGH as a general epistemic proposition.

**Qualification:** scientific testimony is structured by reproducibility, methodological norms, peer criticism, and institutional practices. It is not equivalent in every respect to historical or religious testimony.

### STP-EPI-005 — Testimony therefore cannot be dismissed merely because it is testimony

If testimony were inadmissible in principle, enormous portions of ordinary, historical, and scientific knowledge would become inaccessible.

**Relation:** categorical argument.  
**Confidence:** HIGH.

**STP significance:** objections to Scripture must concern its credibility, interpretation, provenance, content, defeaters, or competing evidence rather than the bare fact that it is testimonial.

## D3 — Historical knowledge is inferential

### STP-EPI-006 — The past is not directly repeatable

Historical events are not reproduced experimentally in the sense of rerunning the same event. Historical inquiry reconstructs past states from surviving traces, documents, testimony, material remains, and causal knowledge.

**Relation:** philosophy/methodology of history.  
**Confidence:** HIGH.

**Qualification:** this does not make historical knowledge arbitrary. Independent evidence, source criticism, causal constraints, chronology, convergence, and explanatory comparison can strongly constrain reconstruction.

### STP-EPI-007 — Historical evidence is heterogeneous

Historical reconstruction can incorporate:
- material artifacts;
- inscriptions;
- manuscripts;
- environmental traces;
- biological/geological records;
- contemporaneous documents;
- later testimony;
- institutional records;
- causal/process knowledge.

No single evidence class has automatic priority independent of the question and evidentiary quality.

**STP significance:** textual testimony is one evidence class among others and should be evaluated rather than prohibited categorically.

## D4 — Methodological naturalism does not equal metaphysical naturalism

### STP-EPI-008 — Philosophical naturalism is not simply a laboratory observation

Standard philosophical treatments distinguish methodological commitments from ontological naturalism. A scientific method may restrict itself to natural causal explanations for practical and disciplinary reasons without thereby demonstrating that supernatural reality does not exist.

**Relation:** philosophical category distinction.  
**Confidence:** HIGH.

Primary scholarly orientation:
- Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy, “Naturalism”;
- Peter Harrison on naturalism and the success/history of science;
- philosophy-of-science literature representing both naturalist and theist positions.

### STP-EPI-009 — Methodological success under a restricted model does not establish ontological exhaustiveness

Formal structure:

```text
Method M admits causal class N
M successfully explains many observations using N
        ⇏
Only N exists
```

The invalid inference is from explanatory success within a constrained search space to the metaphysical claim that the search space exhausts reality.

**Confidence:** HIGH as a logical distinction.

**Qualification:** a naturalist may offer additional abductive or philosophical arguments for metaphysical naturalism. BWM must engage those arguments rather than implying naturalism has no positive case.

## D5 — Supernatural claims and historical admissibility

### STP-EPI-010 — “Supernatural” cannot function simultaneously as the disputed conclusion and an exclusion rule

If an inquiry asks whether divine action occurred, a rule stating “divine action may not be considered as an explanation” settles the question procedurally rather than evidentially.

```text
Question:
    Did divine action occur?

Rule:
    Divine action is inadmissible.

Result:
    No admissible reconstruction contains divine action.
```

The result follows from the rule, not from historical evidence alone.

**STP significance:** BWM may challenge the ontological reach of such a rule without challenging its pragmatic usefulness inside ordinary natural science.

### STP-EPI-011 — Admissibility does not entail credulity

Removing an a priori exclusion does not imply:

```text
miracle claim → accept
```

Instead:

```text
claim
+ source credibility
+ independent evidence
+ background knowledge
+ explanatory fit
+ defeaters
        ↓
historical judgment
```

Extraordinary or low-prior claims may rationally require correspondingly strong evidence under many Bayesian or abductive frameworks.

**BWM rule:** do not replace naturalistic exclusion with supernaturalistic automatic acceptance.

## No-free-lunch rules

1. **No authority smuggling:** BWM's hard core may confess Scripture as divine revelation, but the public cumulative case must identify when an inference depends on that confessional premise.
2. **No naturalism smuggling:** natural causes cannot be assumed ontologically exhaustive merely because scientific method searches for them.
3. **No miracle smuggling:** supernatural causation cannot be inserted merely because a natural explanation is incomplete.
4. **No testimonial privilege:** biblical testimony must be interpreted carefully and competing evidence addressed.
5. **No evidential symmetry fiction:** admitting multiple explanatory categories does not imply they have equal evidence.
6. **No gap inference:** “current natural explanation incomplete” does not entail “therefore God directly acted here.”
7. **No reconstruction-to-observation collapse:** inferred history must remain distinguished from measured present state.
