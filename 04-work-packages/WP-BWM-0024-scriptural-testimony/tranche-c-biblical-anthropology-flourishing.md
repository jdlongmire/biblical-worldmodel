# WP-BWM-0024 — Tranche C: Biblical Anthropology, Wisdom, and Flourishing

Status: active research
Date opened: 2026-09-21

## Purpose

Test whether Scripture demonstrates durable explanatory correspondence with independently observable human behavior, moral psychology, relationships, wisdom, and flourishing.

The method begins with bounded biblical propositions. External research is then used to test correspondence. Modern psychology, sociology, behavioral economics, epidemiology, or flourishing research does not define what Scripture is permitted to say.

## Method

```text
Biblical proposition
        ↓
define observable implication
        ↓
identify relevant external literature
        ↓
compare correspondence / tension / underdetermination
        ↓
record evidentiary force and limitations
```

This tranche is cumulative. No single psychological or epidemiological finding can establish divine revelation.

## Guardrails

- Do not retrofit vague biblical language to any favorable modern result.
- Distinguish descriptive anthropology from moral prescription.
- Distinguish correlation, longitudinal association, intervention evidence, and causal inference.
- Do not infer biblical truth from health benefits.
- Negative or null findings remain in the ledger.
- Population, culture, age, sex, and study design must accompany empirical claims.
- “Religion” as measured in a study is not automatically equivalent to biblical Christianity.
- Flourishing is multidimensional; comfort, health, virtue, meaning, holiness, and faithfulness are not interchangeable.

## Claim families

### C1 — Human moral inconsistency

**Biblical loci:** Genesis 3–4; Jeremiah 17:9; Romans 1–3; Romans 7; James 1:14–15.

**Bounded proposition:** humans display recurring conflict between moral knowledge, desire, self-interest, rationalization, and conduct.

**Observable domains:** moral licensing, motivated reasoning, self-deception, temptation, akrasia, cognitive dissonance, behavioral ethics.

### C2 — Pride, status, rivalry, and partiality

**Biblical loci:** Genesis 4; Proverbs 16:18; Mark 9:33–37; Luke 14:7–11; James 2:1–9; 3:13–18.

**Bounded proposition:** status competition, pride, envy, and partiality predict conflict and distorted judgment.

**Observable domains:** social comparison, status competition, envy, narcissism, in-group bias, prestige/dominance research.

### C3 — Social belonging and communal formation

**Biblical loci:** Genesis 2:18; Ecclesiastes 4:9–12; Acts 2:42–47; Hebrews 10:24–25; 1 Corinthians 12.

**Bounded proposition:** humans are relational creatures whose practices, identity, resilience, and conduct are shaped by durable community.

**Observable domains:** loneliness, social integration, communal participation, social support, health, prosocial behavior.

### C4 — Forgiveness and reconciliation

**Biblical loci:** Matthew 5:23–24, 43–48; 6:12–15; 18:21–35; Ephesians 4:31–32; Colossians 3:12–13.

**Bounded proposition:** forgiveness and reconciliation are central to healthy interpersonal moral order, while bitterness and retaliation perpetuate relational harm.

**Observable domains:** forgiveness interventions, anger, depression, relationship quality, reconciliation, prosocial behavior.

### C5 — Self-control and formation by repeated practice

**Biblical loci:** Proverbs 25:28; 1 Corinthians 9:24–27; Galatians 5:22–23; 1 Timothy 4:7–8; 2 Peter 1:5–8.

**Bounded proposition:** self-control is a virtue with consequences for conduct and can be cultivated within a broader pattern of moral formation.

**Observable domains:** self-regulation, habit formation, delayed gratification, substance use, risky behavior.

### C6 — Meaning, purpose, and transcendent orientation

**Biblical loci:** Ecclesiastes; Matthew 6:19–34; Mark 8:34–37; Philippians 1:20–26; Colossians 3:1–4.

**Bounded proposition:** human flourishing is not exhausted by material acquisition or hedonic satisfaction; durable purpose and orientation beyond immediate self-interest matter.

**Observable domains:** meaning in life, purpose, eudaimonic well-being, materialism, mortality, resilience.

### C7 — Family, fidelity, and intergenerational formation

**Biblical loci:** Genesis 2:24; Deuteronomy 6:4–9; Proverbs 1–7; Ephesians 5–6.

**Bounded proposition:** durable family bonds, fidelity, parental formation, and intergenerational transmission materially shape human life.

**Observable domains:** family stability, attachment, child development, parental investment, intergenerational transmission.

### C8 — Work, responsibility, and contribution

**Biblical loci:** Genesis 1:28; 2:15; Proverbs 6:6–11; Ecclesiastes 3:12–13; Colossians 3:23; 2 Thessalonians 3:10–12.

**Bounded proposition:** purposeful work, stewardship, contribution, and responsibility are ordinarily components of human flourishing.

**Observable domains:** meaningful work, unemployment, purpose, engagement, contribution, well-being.

### C9 — Suffering, endurance, and character

**Biblical loci:** Job; Psalms; Romans 5:3–5; James 1:2–4; 1 Peter 1.

**Bounded proposition:** suffering is real evil/pain and can also become a context in which endurance, hope, wisdom, and character develop.

**Observable domains:** post-traumatic growth, resilience, meaning-making, coping, hope.

**Guardrail:** never imply that suffering automatically produces growth or that victims are responsible when it does not.

### C10 — Religious community and flourishing

**Biblical loci:** Acts 2:42–47; Hebrews 10:24–25; James 1:27; 1 John 3:16–18.

**Bounded proposition:** regular communal worship, mutual care, moral formation, shared meaning, and service can contribute to relational and personal flourishing.

**Observable domains:** service attendance, social integration, purpose, depression, substance use, mortality, volunteering.

## Initial external-evidence baseline

### STP-ANTH-001 — Religious community participation and longitudinal flourishing outcomes

Chen, Kim, and VanderWeele analyzed three large prospective U.S. cohorts spanning young, middle-aged, and older adults. The research examined religious-service attendance against a broad set of later health and well-being outcomes with baseline covariate/outcome control and sensitivity analyses.

Reported associations included lower subsequent depression, smoking, heavy drinking, and mortality and higher purpose, social integration, positive affect, and life satisfaction.

**Relation:** longitudinal correspondence with C3/C6/C10.  
**Confidence:** MEDIUM-HIGH for association; lower for strong causal interpretation because exposure was observational.

Primary scholarly anchor:
- Chen, Y., Kim, E.S. and VanderWeele, T.J. (2020), *International Journal of Epidemiology* 49:2030–2040, DOI 10.1093/ije/dyaa120.

### STP-ANTH-002 — Religious upbringing and later outcomes

Chen and VanderWeele used prospective Growing Up Today Study data to examine adolescent religious involvement and later psychological well-being, mental health, health behavior, physical health, and character outcomes.

**Relation:** longitudinal correspondence relevant to C5/C6/C7/C10.  
**Confidence:** MEDIUM-HIGH for observed associations.

Primary scholarly anchor:
- Chen, Y. and VanderWeele, T.J. (2018), *American Journal of Epidemiology* 187:2355–2364, DOI 10.1093/aje/kwy142.

### STP-ANTH-003 — Forgiveness is empirically tractable

The Harvard Human Flourishing research portfolio includes longitudinal studies of forgiveness and a multi-site randomized REACH forgiveness intervention. This provides a route for testing bounded claims about forgiveness against both observational and intervention evidence.

**Relation:** research-program evidence relevant to C4.  
**Confidence:** HIGH that forgiveness has a substantial empirical literature; claim-level effects require study-by-study extraction.

Seed sources:
- Chen et al., longitudinal forgiveness studies;
- Ho et al. (2024), International REACH Forgiveness Intervention, multi-site randomized controlled trial.

### STP-ANTH-004 — Flourishing research is multidimensional

Harvard's Human Flourishing Program explicitly studies health, happiness, meaning and purpose, character and virtue, close social relationships, work, family, forgiveness, suffering, and religious community rather than reducing flourishing to subjective happiness.

**Relation:** construct-level correspondence with the multidimensional anthropology assumed by wisdom literature.  
**Confidence:** HIGH.

**Qualification:** conceptual similarity does not demonstrate biblical derivation or biblical truth.

### STP-ANTH-005 — Methodological quality matters in religion/flourishing claims

The Human Flourishing Program explicitly acknowledges reverse causation and confounding problems in older religion-health literature and prioritizes longitudinal designs, baseline outcome control, confounder adjustment, sensitivity analysis, and causal-inference methods.

**Relation:** methodological control.  
**Confidence:** HIGH.

**STP significance:** BWM should privilege rigorous longitudinal/intervention evidence and avoid building the confidence case from cross-sectional “religious people are happier” statistics.

## Research-source backbone

Priority sources include:

- Harvard Human Flourishing Program / VanderWeele research group;
- Global Flourishing Study;
- peer-reviewed longitudinal and intervention studies;
- systematic reviews/meta-analyses in psychology, epidemiology, behavioral science, and sociology;
- primary empirical papers rather than press summaries.

The Global Flourishing Study is especially important as a future source because it follows more than 200,000 participants across 22 countries longitudinally, providing a substantially broader cultural base than older U.S.-centric cohorts.

## First research sequence

1. Build the **human moral inconsistency** ledger from behavioral ethics, motivated reasoning, self-deception, and moral psychology.
2. Build **pride/status/envy/partiality** from social psychology and behavioral economics.
3. Build **forgiveness** using systematic reviews, longitudinal studies, and RCTs.
4. Build **self-control** with attention to replication problems surrounding famous findings.
5. Build **meaning/purpose** using longitudinal evidence.
6. Build **community/religious participation** across multiple countries, explicitly testing generalizability.
7. Build **family and work** separately.
8. Preserve null/contrary evidence in every stream.
9. Only after the ledgers exist, synthesize the biblical-anthropology confidence argument.

## Provisional STP claim

The target is not:

> “Psychology proves the Bible.”

The target is:

> **Scripture presents a coherent and durable account of human beings as morally conflicted, relational, purpose-seeking, habit-forming, status-sensitive, capable of virtue and vice, vulnerable to self-deception, and dependent upon forms of community, meaning, forgiveness, responsibility, and hope. Where these claims are independently investigable, they can be compared with empirical knowledge of human behavior. Persistent explanatory correspondence contributes to a cumulative warrant for taking Scripture seriously as a source concerning human reality.**
