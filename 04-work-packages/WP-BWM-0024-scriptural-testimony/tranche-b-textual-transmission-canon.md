# WP-BWM-0024 — Tranche B: Textual Transmission and Canon

Status: active research
Date opened: 2026-09-21

## Purpose

Evaluate the recoverability, transmission, and canonical reception of the biblical text as a distinct strand of the Scriptural Testimony Principle (STP).

This tranche begins with a categorical distinction:

```text
Recoverability of the text
        ≠
Historical truth of the text
        ≠
Divine inspiration of the text
```

Textual criticism principally addresses the first question: **What text is recoverable from the surviving witnesses?** Historical investigation addresses whether claims in that text correspond to history. Inspiration and revelation are theological/epistemological claims requiring their own argument.

A strong transmission case must never be presented as though manuscript abundance alone proves inspiration or historical truth.

## Research questions

1. How early, numerous, geographically distributed, and textually useful are the surviving biblical witnesses?
2. How well can the wording of the Hebrew Bible and New Testament be reconstructed from those witnesses?
3. What kinds of textual variation exist, and where do meaningful uncertainties remain?
4. What does comparison across long transmission intervals show?
5. How did the recognized canon emerge, and what distinctions are needed between composition, circulation, recognition, collection, and formal listing?
6. What confidence is warranted by the evidence, and what claims exceed it?

- orthographic/spelling;

- transpositions;

## Initial evidence baseline

### STP-TEXT-001 — Dead Sea Scrolls and the antiquity of Hebrew biblical witnesses

The Israel Museum describes the Dead Sea Scrolls as including the oldest known biblical manuscripts. Approximately 200 biblical manuscripts were found at Qumran, representing almost all books of the Hebrew Bible, with Nehemiah and Esther excepted in the museum's summary.

**Relation:** direct manuscript evidence for Hebrew biblical texts centuries before the principal medieval Masoretic codices.  
**Confidence:** HIGH.

**What it establishes:** substantial portions of the Hebrew Bible circulated in textual forms during the Second Temple period.

**What it does not establish:** perfect letter-for-letter uniformity across all textual traditions or the inspiration of the texts.

Primary/institutional anchor:
- Israel Museum Digital Dead Sea Scrolls.

### STP-TEXT-002 — Great Isaiah Scroll and long-range textual continuity

The Great Isaiah Scroll preserves all 66 chapters of Isaiah and dates to the Second Temple period. The Israel Museum states that its text generally conforms to the later Masoretic/traditional version while also containing textual differences.

**Relation:** direct comparative manuscript evidence across roughly a millennium of transmission.  
**Confidence:** HIGH.

**What it establishes:** substantial continuity of the Isaiah textual tradition over a very long copying interval.

**Qualification:** “generally conforms” must not be inflated into “identical.” The differences are part of the evidence and should be classified.

### STP-TEXT-003 — Textual plurality in the Judean Desert

The Dead Sea Scrolls also demonstrate that ancient Hebrew biblical transmission was not monolithic. Some readings align with the later Masoretic tradition, others with textual forms reflected in the Septuagint or Samaritan Pentateuch, and some are distinct.

**Relation:** direct evidence concerning the complexity of transmission.  
**Confidence:** HIGH.

**Importance to STP:** confidence should rest on recoverability through evidence, not on a false premise that no meaningful textual variation ever existed.

### STP-TEXT-004 — Modern direct access to Greek New Testament manuscript evidence

CSNTM has collaborated with more than forty institutions across four continents and produced hundreds of thousands of manuscript images. It makes many Greek New Testament witnesses directly available for scholarly inspection and has identified previously uncatalogued manuscripts.

**Relation:** research-infrastructure evidence supporting transparent inspection of the manuscript tradition.  
**Confidence:** HIGH.

**Importance:** BWM can route readers beyond apologetic summaries to actual manuscript images and catalog data.

Primary/institutional anchor:
- Center for the Study of New Testament Manuscripts.

### STP-TEXT-005 — Critical reconstruction is an active scholarly discipline

The Nestle-Aland tradition, INTF work, Editio Critica Maior, and independent projects such as the Tyndale House Greek New Testament demonstrate that reconstruction of the earliest recoverable New Testament wording is an active evidence-driven scholarly discipline.

The Tyndale House Greek New Testament explicitly re-examined early manuscripts, scribal habits, spelling, paragraphing, and textual decisions rather than simply reproducing a received printed text.

**Relation:** methodological corroboration of recoverability through critical comparison.  
**Confidence:** HIGH.

**Qualification:** disagreement between critical editions is evidence of remaining judgment calls at particular variants. It should be represented rather than hidden.

## Reader-facing thesis under investigation

A responsible confidence statement should eventually look approximately like:

> **The Bible has not reached us through an evidence-free chain. We possess manuscript witnesses from different periods, regions, languages, and textual families that allow the history of the text to be examined. The witnesses contain real variants, including several important disputed passages. Precisely because those differences survive in the evidence, textual criticism can compare them rather than hiding them. The relevant question is how much of the text can be responsibly recovered, where uncertainty remains, and whether those uncertainties materially undermine the message carried by the textual tradition.**

This language is provisional until the evidence ledger is populated.
