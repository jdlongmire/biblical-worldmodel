# WP-BWM-0025 — Genesis Systems Architecture Viewpoint

Status: proposed / concept captured

## Purpose

Develop and test the proposition that the inspired Genesis creation narrative can be formally represented as a high-level systems architecture without attributing modern systems-engineering terminology to Moses or allowing the engineering model to control the text.

The central distinction is between **terminology** and **architecture**. Genesis does not use modern terms such as system boundary, functional allocation, interface, verification, or operational state. The research question is whether the textual structure instantiates realities that contemporary systems-engineering methods can represent faithfully.

## Doctrinal grounding

The authorship model assumed by BWM is plenary divine inspiration with genuine human authorship. Moses is the human author of the Pentateuch, and Scripture was written as its authors were "carried along by the Holy Spirit" (2 Peter 1:21; cf. 2 Timothy 3:16).

Accordingly, Genesis must not be artificially restricted to a reconstructed ancient intellectual horizon. The inspired text may communicate systematic implications whose later formal description uses categories unavailable to the human author.

This does **not** imply that Moses consciously practiced modern systems engineering.

## Governing methodological rule

> **Systems architecture is an analytical language for formally representing what the inspired text says. It is not a technical vocabulary retroactively attributed to Moses and must not become a controlling hermeneutic over Scripture.**

Every architectural assertion must therefore be traceable to the text and classified as:

1. **Explicit** — directly stated in the text.
2. **Strongly implied** — follows closely from textual relations, sequence, or assigned function.
3. **Analytical abstraction** — a modern formal representation that organizes textual content without adding a new textual claim.

## Research question

> **Does the inspired Genesis narrative exhibit a coherent system architecture that can be formally represented using contemporary systems-engineering methods without importing content foreign to the text?**

## Initial architectural hypothesis

Genesis 1 may be representable at high level as:

```text
Divine Intent
    ↓
Divine Speech
    ↓
Actualization
    ↓
Differentiation
    ↓
Organization / Domain Formation
    ↓
Functional Assignment
    ↓
Population / Integration
    ↓
Governance
    ↓
Evaluation
    ↓
Commissioned Order
```

A candidate state-transition abstraction is:

```text
Initial state
 → differentiation
 → structure
 → population
 → governance
 → validation
 → operational / commissioned state
```

These are hypotheses to be tested against the canonical text rather than presumed as findings.

## Candidate viewpoints

### Structural viewpoint
Identify domains, distinctions, boundaries, entities, containment relations, and ordering.

### Functional viewpoint
Identify explicitly assigned functions and purposes, including light/dark cycles, signs, seasons, fruitfulness, dominion, and cultivation.

### Behavioral viewpoint
Represent the ordered creation sequence as state transitions while preserving the text's temporal claims rather than reducing them to functional categories.

### Information viewpoint
Examine divine speech, naming, classification, kinds, signs, commands, and information-bearing relationships.

### Governance viewpoint
Represent sovereignty, delegated authority, dominion, stewardship, prohibition, accountability, and human vocation.

### Verification and validation viewpoint
Examine the repeated "good" evaluations and the culminating "very good" assessment as textual evaluation statements. Determine carefully whether systems V&V terminology illuminates these statements without exceeding them.

### Lifecycle / commissioning viewpoint
Test whether Genesis 1:1–2:3 supports a movement from creation/initiation through ordered readiness to completion and Sabbath, and relate this cautiously to the existing Day 6 commissioning boundary.

### Human-system integration viewpoint
Examine Genesis 2 at increased resolution: environment, human role, task, constraint, relationship, naming, responsibility, and Creator-creature interaction.

## Initial day-pairing hypothesis

Test the familiar literary correspondence as an architectural relation:

| Domain formation | Domain population / governance |
|---|---|
| Day 1: light / darkness | Day 4: luminaries governing cycles |
| Day 2: waters / expanse | Day 5: aquatic / aerial creatures |
| Day 3: land / vegetation | Day 6: terrestrial creatures / humanity |

The package must distinguish what the text explicitly assigns from later structural interpretation.

The viewpoint may provide textual architecture relevant to DFM's concept of functional maturity. In particular, it should investigate whether created entities are presented as entering a coordinated, functioning order with assigned relations and purposes.

It must not use the architecture analogy as independent proof of DFM, nor infer physical mechanisms absent from the text.

## Guardrails

- Scripture interprets Scripture first.
- Do not collapse inspiration into dictation or erase genuine human authorship.
- Do not constrain divine communicative intent to a reconstructed ancient conceptual vocabulary.
- Do not claim modern engineering terminology is present in the Hebrew text.
- Do not import systems-engineering constructs unless they demonstrably clarify textual relations.
- Do not let the model override grammatical, literary, historical, or canonical evidence.
- Preserve distinctions among textual assertion, theological inference, and analytical abstraction.
- Do not use architectural symmetry to dissolve chronology.
- Do not infer a physical mechanism merely from a functional description.
- Record failed mappings and counterexamples rather than forcing architectural completeness.
