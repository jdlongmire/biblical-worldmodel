# WP-BWM-0021 — Andrew Snelling Geology and Geochronology Harvest

## Purpose

Harvest Andrew A. Snelling's strongest empirical and methodological material for BWM while independently testing the evidential chain behind each substantive claim.

The central research question is:

> Which observations and datasets in Snelling's work remain significant when separated from his preferred young-earth, Flood-geology, and RATE explanatory mechanisms, and can any of them improve or constrain BWM/DFM models?

This is a research package. It does not presuppose that Snelling's conclusions, RATE's accelerated nuclear decay hypothesis, or conventional geological chronology is correct.

## Governing method

Apply the BWM Observation-to-History stack:

`O -> M -> R -> W`

- **O** = direct observation or measurement
- **M** = model transformation or analytical inference
- **R** = reconstructed geological history or chronology
- **W** = worldview-level interpretation

For every major claim, identify where the contested proposition enters.

Source priority:

1. Original peer-reviewed paper, thesis, technical report, dataset, or laboratory result.
2. Primary author technical monograph or research report when original data are presented.
3. Secondary literature with explicit citation to primary material.
4. Popular apologetic or critical treatments only as research leads.

When a primary source cannot be accessed, record that fact. Do not silently substitute a secondary source.

## Research lanes

### RL-1 — Snelling corpus and provenance

Construct a bibliographic inventory spanning:
- early professional geology and uranium exploration;
- Koongarra uranium/daughter disequilibrium and radionuclide migration;
- creation-geology publications;
- RATE technical contributions;
- Grand Canyon field and petrographic work;
- major books and synthesis works.

Tag each source by publication type, evidential status, availability, BWM domain, and whether it contains original data.

### RL-2 — Uranium systems, disequilibrium, and radionuclide migration

Examine Snelling's professional work on uranium deposits and natural-analogue radionuclide migration.

Questions:
- What was directly measured?
- Which isotope systems were demonstrably open?
- What transport mechanisms were identified?
- What timescale assumptions entered the interpretation?
- What constraints follow for geochronological closure assumptions?
- Can these mechanisms produce meaningful retrodictive-age offsets without changing nuclear decay constants?

### RL-3 — RATE audit

Decompose Snelling's RATE work and related RATE claims into independent propositions.

At minimum evaluate:
- radioisotope discordance;
- radiohalos;
- fission tracks;
- helium retention/diffusion in zircons;
- residual carbon-14;
- accelerated nuclear decay.

For accelerated nuclear decay explicitly assess:
- required change in decay rate;
- energy release and radiogenic heat;
- radiation dose;
- neutrino/nuclear implications where applicable;
- mineral and lattice consequences;
- whether the mechanism has independent physical support.

DFM baseline for this package: accelerated decay is not assumed.

### RL-4 — Radiocarbon beyond conventional range

Audit claims of measurable C-14 in diamonds, coal, graphite, fossils, and other conventionally ancient materials.

For each dataset capture:
- sample provenance;
- pretreatment;
- AMS laboratory;
- measured fraction modern / pMC or equivalent;
- process blank;
- instrument background;
- contamination controls;
- reported uncertainty;
- calibration/correction procedure;
- independent replication.

Determine whether the residual signal exceeds demonstrated laboratory and preparation backgrounds.

### RL-5 — Radiometric discordance and reconstruction

Build a model ledger for cases in which dating methods disagree.

Represent the observed isotope state schematically as:

`S_obs = H(S_0, D, T, F, A, C)`

where:
- `S_0` = initialized isotopic/mineral state;
- `D` = radioactive decay;
- `T` = transport and migration;
- `F` = fractionation;
- `A` = alteration/metamorphism;
- `C` = closure/reopening history;
- `H` = combined historical transformation.

The conventional age inversion and any DFM reconstruction must state which variables are constrained by observation and which are assumed.

Key question:

> Does a discordant date identify a failure of decay physics, a failure of system-history assumptions, an unconstrained initial state, or some combination?

### RL-6 — Catastrophic and Flood geology

Harvest Snelling's field evidence for:
- rapid sedimentation;
- catastrophic erosion;
- volcanism;
- tectonic deformation;
- rapid metamorphic or intrusive processes;
- large-scale stratigraphic relationships.

Keep observed process rates and geological structures distinct from assignment to a particular Flood phase or chronology.

### RL-7 — Grand Canyon deformation

Examine the Tapeats Sandstone, Bright Angel Formation, Muav Formation, and major fold studies, including Carbon Canyon, Monument, and Whitmore Helipad.

For each disputed structure create:

`observation | Snelling interpretation | conventional interpretation | discriminating observation`

Audit petrography, cement relationships, fractures, grain deformation, pressure solution, cleavage, recrystallization, and structural context.

Include substantive published criticism and Snelling's technical responses. Do not treat either side's characterization of the other as evidence.

### RL-8 — DFM/BWM harvest

For every retained observation ask whether it bears on:
- initialization conditions;
- retrodictive versus actual elapsed chronology;
- open-system isotope behavior;
- catastrophic Earth-history processes;
- Flood boundary conditions;
- the reconstruction problem;
- discriminating predictions.

Candidate DFM framing:

`observed isotope ratios -> initial state + lawful decay + transport/fractionation/alteration + closure history -> retrodictive age`

The research task is to determine which terms can be independently constrained.

## Acceptance criteria

- **AC-01:** Bibliography includes Snelling's major primary technical corpus and clearly marks inaccessible primary sources.
- **AC-02:** Every elevated scientific proposition is traceable to primary evidence or explicitly marked secondary-only.
- **AC-03:** Empirical observations are separated from Snelling/RATE interpretation.
- **AC-04:** RATE accelerated-decay claims receive a quantitative physical-consequence audit.
- **AC-05:** Radiocarbon claims include laboratory-background and contamination analysis.
- **AC-06:** Grand Canyon claims include both the strongest Snelling case and substantive technical criticism.
- **AC-07:** DFM alternatives do not use unconstrained initialization as an ad hoc universal escape; proposed explanations must identify independent constraints or discriminators.
- **AC-08:** Candidate BWM propositions include falsifiers or discriminating observations where feasible.
- **AC-09:** Final disposition distinguishes retained evidence, qualified evidence, rejected arguments, unresolved questions, and canonicalization candidates.
- **AC-10:** JD reviews any proposed canonical integration.

## Initial hypothesis to test

Snelling's empirical work may be more valuable to BWM than wholesale adoption of his explanatory model. In particular, his work on open isotope systems, radionuclide migration, geological catastrophe, and rapid deformation may constrain DFM/Flood models even if accelerated nuclear decay is rejected.

That proposition is a hypothesis of this work package, not a conclusion.
