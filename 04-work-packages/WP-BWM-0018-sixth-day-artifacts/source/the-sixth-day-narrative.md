# The Sixth Day

> Source artifact preserved under WP-BWM-0018. This version is retained as received for revision provenance. It is a narrative reconstruction and includes chronology/staging details not explicitly stated by Genesis.

The ground was already full of life when the LORD God turned to the dust.

Earlier that day, at his word, the earth had brought forth livestock and creeping things and beasts, each according to its kind, *and God saw that it was good.* The land was loud with them now. Somewhere in the east, in Eden, a garden stood planted and waiting, with no one yet to tend it.

For everything else, God had spoken. For the man, he stooped. *The LORD God formed the man of dust from the ground and breathed into his nostrils the breath of life, and the man became a living creature.*

His first breath came from the One who made him.

---

It was barely past sunrise when God took him into the garden. Trees on every side, *pleasant to the sight and good for food*, and a river running through the middle of it before dividing into four as it left. At the center stood two trees that mattered more than all the rest.

The charge was plain. Work the garden. Keep it. Eat freely from every tree, with one exception. *Of the tree of the knowledge of good and evil you shall not eat, for in the day that you eat of it you shall surely die.*

A man only hours old, and already he had a vocation and a boundary. He had work before he had a wife, and a command before he had anyone to share it with.

Then God said something Adam could not yet see for himself: *It is not good that the man should be alone.*

Six days of "good," and here was the first "not good." God knew it. By afternoon, Adam would know it too.

---

They came two by two.

*Out of the ground the LORD God had formed every beast of the field and every bird of the heavens and brought them to the man to see what he would call them.*

First the heavy ones: cattle lumbering past, horses tossing their heads, a pair of great cats moving low and easy. Wolves came side by side. Birds dropped out of the sky onto the grass in front of him and lifted away again. Somewhere in the procession a creature passed whose neck rose above the trees, and its mate after it.

Adam watched each pair for a few breaths. How it moved. What it did with its mouth, its feet, its eyes. How the two kept close to each other. Then he named it, and the name held. *Whatever the man called every living creature, that was its name.*

The sun climbed. It passed its height and started down. Kind after kind, pair after pair, each one turning back into the garden together.

Five hours of it.

*The man gave names to all livestock and to the birds of the heavens and to every beast of the field. But for Adam there was not found a helper fit for him.*

He had named everything that walked past him. He had seen himself in none of it.

---

*So the LORD God caused a deep sleep to fall upon the man, and while he slept took one of his ribs and closed up its place with flesh.*

The word the text uses is *built.* God built the rib into a woman. And then he did the same thing he had done all morning: *he brought her to the man.*

Adam woke to one more creature standing before him. This time there was no pair walking away. This time the counterpart was his.

After a whole day of naming, he gave one more name, and this one came out as a cry:

*This at last is bone of my bones and flesh of my flesh; she shall be called Woman, because she was taken out of Man.*

*At last.* Five hours of looking had taught him what the words meant.

The narrator steps in to say what this moment would mean for every generation after: *Therefore a man shall leave his father and his mother and hold fast to his wife, and they shall become one flesh.* The two of them stood in the garden, *both naked and not ashamed.*

---

The light was lengthening when God spoke to them together.

*And God blessed them. And God said to them, "Be fruitful and multiply and fill the earth and subdue it, and have dominion over the fish of the sea and over the birds of the heavens and over every living thing that moves on the earth."*

Every creature Adam had named that morning was now entrusted to two.

Then God looked over all of it: the land and the sea, the lights overhead, the animals settling in the garden, and the man and woman standing among them.

*And God saw everything that he had made, and behold, it was very good.*

The "not good" of the morning had been answered before sundown.

*And there was evening and there was morning, the sixth day.*
