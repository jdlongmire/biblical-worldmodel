# Boundary-Sensitive Retrodiction

**Work package:** WP-BWM-0018  
**Status:** Working methodological synthesis  
**Origin:** PFH / chronology discussion, 2026-09-14

## Core observation

Several BWM research lines converge on the same methodological problem: backward extrapolation can remain mathematically valid while ceasing to entail actual antecedent history once the reconstruction crosses a genuine state-transition boundary.

The relevant question is therefore not merely whether a present regularity can be extrapolated backward. The question is whether the assumptions that make that extrapolation valid remain warranted across the entire interval being reconstructed.

## Generic form

Let:

- `O(t0)` = present observations at time `t0`;
- `L` = currently warranted operational regularities or laws;
- `B` = assumed historical boundary conditions;
- `R` = retrodictive model;
- `H*` = reconstructed antecedent history;
- `T` = a state-transition boundary.

Then:

`H* = R(O(t0), L, B)`

The reconstruction is evidentially strong only to the extent that `L` and `B` remain warranted over the interval being reconstructed.

If the reconstruction crosses a boundary `T` such that the relevant state variables, constraints, or causal regime differ materially on the far side of `T`, then the inference becomes boundary-sensitive. Continued backward extrapolation may still generate a calculable pre-boundary trajectory, but that trajectory does not automatically entail actual pre-boundary history.

## BWM applications

### 1. DFM initialization boundary

Designed Functional Maturity posits an initialization boundary at creation. Operational regularities observed after initialization may be projected backward mathematically, but a retrodicted pre-initialization trajectory is not thereby established as actual history.

This is the formal version of the existing DFM maxim:

> Operational regularity warrants defeasible extrapolation; it does not entail generation of `S0`.

### 2. PFH / Fall boundary

The post-Fall chronology problem appears structurally similar.

Empirical evidence from human remains and radiocarbon chronologies can motivate a question about the temporal placement of human history. Scripture then determines which chronological constraints are explicit.

Current PFH work distinguishes:

- creation of Adam and Eve;
- an unspecified pre-Fall interval;
- the Fall as a covenantal and mortality boundary;
- post-Fall genealogical age accounting and human death chronology.

The key methodological point is that post-Fall age accounting cannot simply be projected backward through the Fall unless Scripture warrants doing so.

The empirical observation can generate the question. Scripture constrains the admissible answer.

### 3. Flood / geological catastrophe boundary

Historical geology similarly depends on assumptions about continuity of rates, boundary conditions, and depositional regimes. A global catastrophic event, if historical, would create a major state-transition boundary.

The BWM question is therefore not whether present processes are relevant to past reconstruction. They are. The question is whether ordinary-rate extrapolation remains warranted through intervals containing regime-changing events.

### 4. Broader historical inference

The same logic applies wherever a reconstruction assumes continuity across:

- phase transitions;
- initialization events;
- catastrophic resets;
- covenantal or biological state changes;
- changes in operating conditions that materially alter the inference model.

## Epistemic ordering

Boundary-sensitive retrodiction fits the BWM hierarchy:

1. Scripture establishes hard historical and theological constraints where it speaks.
2. Nature supplies real but interpretation-dependent evidence.
3. Operational regularities constrain models.
4. Retrodictive histories remain defeasible where antecedent boundary conditions are not directly observed.
5. Apparent conflict should first trigger examination of assumptions before reinterpretation of explicit biblical language.

This distinguishes two very different responses to empirical tension.

One response permits current scientific reconstruction to control the semantic reading of explicit biblical text. The other asks whether the conflict arises because an unstated assumption has been imported into either the scientific or biblical interpretation.

PFH arose through the second path: an empirical chronology tension prompted examination of whether Scripture actually fixes the duration between creation and Fall. The resulting hypothesis remains constrained by exegesis rather than generated by the empirical datum alone.

## Provisional BWM principle

**Boundary-Sensitive Retrodiction Principle:**

> A retrodictive inference remains warranted only across intervals for which the model's governing regularities and boundary assumptions remain independently justified. Crossing a genuine state-transition boundary without revalidating those assumptions converts entailment into conditional reconstruction.

## Consequences

This principle may provide a common methodological layer connecting:

- DFM initialization;
- PFH and post-Fall chronology;
- CHFM / Flood catastrophe modelling;
- radiometric and stratigraphic reconstruction;
- any BWM comparison where a model infers antecedent states from present regularity.

It should eventually be evaluated for promotion into the Foundations / Integration architecture after cross-checking against the existing observation-history-purpose method and evidence-ledger model.

## Open questions

1. Can the principle be formalized without becoming so broad that every inconvenient datum is assigned to a boundary?
2. What independent evidence is required to posit a state-transition boundary?
3. How should BWM distinguish a biblically explicit boundary from a scientifically hypothesized one?
4. What falsifiers prevent DFM, PFH, or Flood models from using boundaries ad hoc?
5. Can the principle be expressed in Bayesian terms as a change in conditional likelihoods across regime boundaries?
6. How does this relate to change-point detection, nonstationary processes, and structural-break analysis in operational science?

## Guardrail

A boundary is not an explanatory escape hatch. It incurs an evidential burden. BWM must identify the boundary independently, specify what changes across it, and state which retrodictive assumptions cease to be warranted. Otherwise the proposal is merely ad hoc insulation.
