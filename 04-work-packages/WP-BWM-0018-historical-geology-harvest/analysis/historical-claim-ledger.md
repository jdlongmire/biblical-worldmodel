## Method

This ledger separates the transcript's historical claims from its geological, theological, and rhetorical conclusions. A transcript quotation is not treated as authenticated merely because it appears in the source video. Each material quotation should be traced to the earliest accessible primary source. If only a secondary source is available, record it as secondary and use `as cited in` where appropriate.

Confidence labels for completed verification:

- **HIGH** — verified against an accessible primary source or a scholarly transcription/edition of the primary document.
- **MEDIUM** — supported by a reliable secondary source with usable citation detail.
- **LOW** — tertiary or paraphrastic support only.
- **UNCERTAIN** — evidence insufficient or conflicting.

## Verified primary-source notes

### Hodge

Primary work: Charles Hodge, *Systematic Theology*, vol. 1, creation discussion, pp. 570–571 in commonly cited editions. Accessible transcription: Christian Classics Ethereal Library. The transcript accurately captures the core quotation, but its parenthetical substitution of `millions of years` for Hodge's `facts` is editorial, not part of Hodge's text.

### Lyell

Primary-document edition: K. M. Lyell (ed.), *Life, Letters and Journals of Sir Charles Lyell, Bart.*, vol. 1 (London: John Murray, 1881), especially pp. 268–271. The volume reproduces Lyell's correspondence. Google Books and Wellcome Collection provide digitized copies. The correspondence verifies both the `Mosaic geology` wording and the strategy of avoiding irritation while attracting bishops and `enlightened saints`.

### Darwin

Primary manuscript transcription: Darwin Correspondence Project, letter no. 9105, Charles Darwin to George Howard Darwin, 21 October 1873, source manuscript DAR 210.1:14. The DCP transcription directly verifies the Deluge statement and the `slow & silent side attacks` postscript. The DCP editorial note independently connects the Lyell remark to Lyell's stated hope of `freeing the science [geology] from Moses`, citing the 1881 Lyell correspondence edition.

Darwin's own autobiography states that Henslow advised him to study Lyell's first volume before the Beagle voyage and that his observations at St. Jago convinced him of the superiority of Lyell's views. Darwin's letter to Leonard Horner, 29 August 1844 (Darwin Correspondence Project, letter no. 771), says his books seemed to come `half out of Lyell's brains` and that the *Principles* altered the whole tone of his mind. These are direct primary witnesses to Lyell's intellectual influence.

## BWM analytical connection

The first primary-source passes strengthen a narrower historical thesis: significant nineteenth-century actors were consciously negotiating the relationship between Mosaic geology, emerging geological reconstruction, public persuasion, and theological reception. Hodge expressly allowed what he regarded as geological facts to constrain the semantic choice among possible readings of `day`. Lyell expressly discussed setting down Mosaic geology without giving offence and avoiding rhetoric that would irritate churchmen. Darwin later reported Lyell's belief that avoiding direct biblical attack had been more effective in weakening faith in the Deluge and himself commended indirect religious criticism in advising his son. Darwin's own autobiography and correspondence independently show how deeply Lyell's framework shaped his scientific reasoning.

These sources support a BWM case study in epistemic ordering and rhetorical history. They do **not**, without additional evidence, establish every stronger causal or intentional claim made in the source video. In particular, the primary evidence presently warrants `deliberate displacement of Mosaic geology through strategically indirect presentation` and `major Lyellian influence on Darwin`; it does not yet warrant the larger claim of a coordinated conspiracy to destroy Christianity.
