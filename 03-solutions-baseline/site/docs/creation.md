# Creation and functional maturity

This section introduces questions about initial conditions, functional maturity, and the interpretation of apparent history. It is a public orientation to a research programme, not a statement that its proposed mechanisms have been established.

The canonical source is [Designed Functional Maturity](https://github.com/jdlongmire/designed-functional-maturity). Consult that repository for its actual claims, tests, limitations, and current status.

## More than mechanical sufficiency

Within the Biblical WorldModel, *function* should not be reduced to the minimum physical machinery required to keep a system operating. Scripture repeatedly presents creation as something perceived and contemplated as well as used: the heavens declare God's glory (Psalm 19:1), the psalmist considers the heavens and is moved to wonder (Psalm 8:3–4), Psalm 104 celebrates the abundance and variety of the created order, Job 38–41 confronts the observer with its scale and complexity, and Romans 1:20 treats created things as disclosive of divine power and nature.

BWM therefore recognizes an **aesthetic-revelatory function** alongside mechanical and life-permitting function:

> **Aesthetic-revelatory function:** the intentional capacity of the created order to be apprehended by rational creatures as ordered, abundant, intelligible, beautiful, and wonder-producing, thereby serving the scriptural role of creation as a disclosure of divine power, wisdom, and glory.

This is not an argument that beauty by itself proves design, nor does it convert aesthetic judgment into a substitute for physical explanation. It identifies a broader design objective supplied by the biblical texts. If creation is intended to be observed, contemplated, investigated, and experienced as declaring its Creator, then observational richness and grandeur are not automatically superfluous merely because they exceed a proposed minimum for biological survival or mechanical operation.

The distinction also constrains Designed Functional Maturity. Aesthetic or revelatory purpose cannot license **fabricated chronicle**. If information-bearing radiation represents a particular astronomical event, saying that the night sky was intended to produce wonder does not by itself justify treating that event as nonexistent. The event and its represented causal sequence remain subject to the same reality constraint described in the [objection / response register](objection-response-register.md#bwm-obj-003-trickster-god-last-thursdayism-and-distant-supernovae).

A useful working taxonomy is therefore:

1. **Functional maturity:** initialized state required for a created entity or system to perform its intended functions.
2. **Causal maturity:** real causal events, relations, and consequences integrated into the initialized system without assuming that post-initialization runtime duration reconstructs the creation boundary.
3. **Aesthetic-revelatory function:** features whose intended role includes apprehension, intelligibility, abundance, beauty, wonder, or disclosure to rational observers.
4. **Chronicle maturity:** traces that purport to record particular events that did not occur. DFM does not treat this as a general-purpose solution to contrary evidence.

These categories can overlap. A star may contribute to physical conditions, participate in a real causal network, and belong to the observable grandeur of the heavens. The aesthetic category broadens the question of purpose; it does not weaken the requirement for causal coherence.

A fuller reader-oriented treatment remains in development. See [methodology](methodology.md) for the distinction between observations and historical inference.
