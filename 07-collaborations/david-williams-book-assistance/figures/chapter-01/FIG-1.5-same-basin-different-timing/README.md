# FIG-1.5: Same Basin, Different Timing

**Chapter:** 1, `The Timing Problem`  
**Type:** three-panel conceptual paleostructural sequence  
**Priority:** HIGHEST  
**Status:** design specification  
**Role:** candidate signature figure for Chapter 1 and the book's central trap-timing thesis

## Figure purpose

Show, in one page, why present-day trap geometry is insufficient to reconstruct petroleum charge history. Two traps may look comparably favorable today while having had different outcomes because only one existed during the principal migration interval.

The figure must make the temporal claim visually explicit without presenting the hypothesis as an observed universal rule.

## Core proposition represented

Where a petroleum system experiences a principal charge interval before all present-day traps have become effective, an early-existing trap can receive petroleum while a later-forming trap can remain barren, absent later charge, remigration, or a separate petroleum system.

This is a hypothesis schematic. It is not a claim that all barren traps formed too late.

## Required three-panel sequence

### Panel A: Before principal migration

Working label: **A. Trap availability differs before charge**

Show:

- a source interval at depth;
- a carrier/pathway interval connected updip;
- **Trap A** already developed as an effective closure with reservoir and seal;
- the future location of **Trap B** lacking effective closure or still structurally open;
- no large petroleum accumulation yet;
- a simple time marker indicating this is earlier than the principal charge interval.

Interpretive point: present-day Trap B must not be drawn retrospectively into this panel as though it already existed.

### Panel B: Principal migration interval

Working label: **B. Petroleum moves while Trap A is available**

Show:

- source generating/expelling petroleum;
- migration through the carrier/pathway system;
- petroleum entering and accumulating in Trap A;
- petroleum continuing past or through the future location of Trap B because no effective closure exists there yet;
- a clearly marked **principal charge interval**.

Migration arrows in this panel represent the conceptual hypothesis, not a specific field observation, and should therefore use the project's hypothesized-pathway visual treatment.

### Panel C: Present-day geometry

Working label: **C. Both structures now look favorable**

Show:

- Trap A still present and charged;
- Trap B now developed as an apparently valid reservoir/seal closure but barren;
- source and carrier system in their later/present configuration;
- reduced or inactive principal migration pathway unless later charge is explicitly shown;
- a present-day time marker.

Primary visual message: **modern geometry alone does not reveal which traps were available when petroleum moved.**

## Required labels

Use concise labels only:

- Source rock
- Carrier/pathway
- Reservoir
- Seal
- Trap A: early-effective
- Trap B: later-effective
- Principal charge interval
- Petroleum accumulation
- Barren / uncharged
- Earlier -> Later -> Present

Avoid paragraph-length annotation inside the figure.

## Visual semantics

Follow `../../README.md`.

Specific requirements for this figure:

- Present/observed-style structures in Panel C use solid treatment.
- Historical structures in Panels A-B must read as reconstructed states.
- Hypothesized migration arrows use dashed treatment.
- Petroleum accumulation should be visually distinct from water-filled reservoir space without implying a specific fluid composition unless the caption says `petroleum` generically.
- Trap B must visibly develop between Panels B and C.
- Keep stratigraphic architecture similar across panels so structural evolution, rather than an unrelated change in geology, explains the comparison.
- Do not include quantitative ages, depths, migration rates, or overburden thresholds in v1.

## What the figure must NOT imply

The figure must not imply:

1. all petroleum migration is early;
2. all dry traps are late traps;
3. compaction is the only cause of changing permeability;
4. a single migration pulse occurs in every basin;
5. later charge or remigration is impossible;
6. Trap A and Trap B represent a specific named field unless a later evidence-based variant is created;
7. the unverified Adkins quantitative threshold.

## Alternative mechanisms acknowledged

The caption or adjacent manuscript text must make clear that later charge, remigration, fault reactivation, generation-induced fracturing, seal failure, and breach can alter the simple sequence shown here. The figure isolates one testable timing relationship for conceptual clarity.

## Caption draft

**Figure 1.5. Same basin, different timing.** Conceptual illustration of the trap-timing hypothesis developed in this book. (A) Before the principal charge interval, Trap A is already an effective closure while the future location of Trap B is not. (B) During principal petroleum migration, Trap A receives charge while petroleum can pass the future Trap B location. (C) By the present day, both structures may appear geometrically favorable, yet Trap B can remain barren if it became effective after the principal charge interval and received no later charge or remigration. The schematic illustrates a hypothesis to be tested against paleostructure, source maturity, migration pathways, diagenesis, and charge history; it does not imply that all barren traps formed too late.

## Manuscript placement

Primary placement: Chapter 1, immediately after or within the section `The Trap-Timing Hypothesis`.

The figure should be introduced in prose before the reader encounters it. The surrounding text should explain that charge timing is already a standard petroleum-system concern and that this book asks whether the relevant migration interval in many systems occurred earlier than modern geometry might suggest.

## Evidence dependencies

This v1 is conceptual and does not require field-specific quantitative data.

Before any field-specific derivative is made, verify through the evidence ledger:

- source assignment;
- timing of generation/expulsion;
- paleostructural reconstruction;
- reservoir/seal availability;
- charge timing;
- evidence that the barren structure was uncharged rather than breached.

## Review questions

Before acceptance, ask:

1. Can a reader understand the timing hypothesis without reading the chapter?
2. Is it visually obvious that Trap B does not yet exist during principal migration?
3. Does the figure avoid implying that late traps are the only cause of barren structures?
4. Are hypothesis and observation visually distinguishable?
5. Is the present-day structural similarity between A and B clear enough to make the historical contrast meaningful?
6. Does the caption preserve the qualifications needed by the chapter?

## Acceptance criterion

FIG-1.5 is manuscript-ready when the rendered figure communicates the temporal argument in under approximately ten seconds of inspection, preserves the stated epistemic boundaries, remains readable at publication scale, and is accepted by David Williams as a fair representation of the trap-timing hypothesis.
