## Verification notes

### DW-E004
Jarvie, D. M., Hill, R. J., Ruble, T. E. & Pollastro, R. M. (2007), `Unconventional shale-gas systems: The Mississippian Barnett Shale of north-central Texas as one model for thermogenic shale-gas assessment`, AAPG Bulletin, 91(4), 475-499. DOI: 10.1306/12190606068.

Verified source statements include an estimated total generation potential of 820 bcf/section, approximately 60% expelled, and a likely expulsion-efficiency range of 50-70%. The same paper states that conversion of kerogen to hydrocarbons yields a net porosity increase of 4.3-4.6% under the stated assumptions. These are source results and should be distinguished from Williams' inference about when migration had to occur.

### DW-E005 to DW-E007: Monterey cluster

Source-derived findings:

- Isaacs (1988/1989), USGS publication record, describes the Miocene Monterey Formation as important both as petroleum source and reservoir and states that it is thought to have generated much of the petroleum in California coastal basins.
- USGS petroleum-geology work on the Santa Maria province describes Monterey as the principal source and reservoir of petroleum in the onshore and offshore Santa Maria basins.
- The USGS 2025 Santa Maria Basin assessment states that Monterey organic-rich lithologies have the highest volumes of oil generated, migrated, and trapped in the province. It also recognizes possible subsidiary source rocks, so manuscript wording should use `principal` or `dominant`, not `exclusive`.
- SPE/JPT (2013), `The Mighty Monterey: California's Primary Petroleum Source Rock`, reports an estimate of 38 billion barrels produced from fields whose source rock is Monterey. This is currently secondary support for the quantitative cumulative-production claim.

Important numerical caution:

The Williams notes state that Monterey has been estimated to contain more than 500 billion barrels original oil in place. Current verification did not recover a source for that number. It must not be conflated with EIA's 2011 estimate of 15.4 billion barrels of technically recoverable Monterey/Santos shale oil, which is a different resource category. The >500 billion figure remains quarantined pending source recovery.

### DW-E009
Current bibliographic trail:

- Wilson (1990), `The Case for Early Generation and Accumulation of Oil`, Journal of Petroleum Geology, cites: Adkins, W. S. (1956), `Age of Folding in East Texas Salt Domes`, 20th International Geological Congress (Mexico), Abstracts, p. 24.
- Houston Geological Society Bulletin, June 1992, reproduces the same citation.
- Keith Young's biographical entry for Walter Scott Adkins reports that an important Adkins work, `Time of Origin and Migration of Oil`, was abstracted at the 1956 International Geological Congress in Mexico.

Until the congress abstract/proceedings are inspected directly, do not collapse these titles into one work and do not assert that Adkins demonstrated the `200 salt domes / 2,000 feet` threshold.

## Verification rules

1. Search for the primary source first.
2. Never expand an author/year fragment into a full citation without verification.
3. When a primary source is accessible only through a secondary source, record it as `as cited in`.
4. Separate observation from Williams' interpretation and from any BWM interpretation.
5. Record contradictory literature rather than suppressing it.
6. Confidence labels: HIGH = verified primary; MEDIUM = reliable secondary with page-level support; LOW = tertiary/paraphrase; UNCERTAIN = not yet assessed.
