# Chronological Contamination

Status: Proposed canonical BWM requirement
Work package: `WP-BWM-0011-chronological-contamination`

## Purpose

Biblical WorldModel treats the biblical historical sequence as part of its hard core. Human death and burial belong to post-Fall history. Therefore, remains established as genuinely human, including Neanderthal and Denisovan populations where they are treated as members of the Adamic human lineage, cannot have an actual burial age older than the permitted post-Fall chronology.

This requirement governs how conflicting dating outputs are classified and investigated inside BWM.

## Hard-core constraint

**BWM-CHR-001 — Adamic chronology constraint**

All genuinely human remains derive from Adam and belong to post-Fall history. A dating result that places such remains outside the permitted biblical chronology shall not be accepted as actual elapsed human history within BWM.

## Derived interpretation rule

**BWM-CHR-002 — Chronological contamination rule**

When a dating method returns an age older than the chronology permitted by BWM for a demonstrably human specimen or its burial context, BWM shall treat the excess age as evidence of **chronological contamination** until a specific mechanism is identified and tested.

For this programme, chronological contamination means:

> Any inherited, introduced, altered, mixed, or mis-modelled state variable that causes a dating procedure to return a retrodictive age older than the actual elapsed duration of the object, event, or deposit being dated.

The term is broader than laboratory contamination. Candidate mechanisms may include:

- incorporation or reworking of older material;
- inherited isotopic or daughter-product inventories;
- uranium uptake, loss, or open-system behavior;
- reservoir effects;
- inherited or incompletely reset luminescence dose;
- thermal-history error;
- groundwater, mineral-binding, or hydrochemical alteration;
- catastrophic depositional or geochemical restructuring;
- stratigraphic mixing or correlation error;
- calibration error;
- sample contamination;
- inherited system state from an earlier initialized or catastrophically altered substrate;
- model misspecification that maps a measured present state to an invalid elapsed-time history.

## Epistemic separation

BWM shall keep the following categories distinct:

1. **Observation** — the presently measurable state of a specimen or deposit.
2. **Operational measurement** — quantities obtained by an instrument or laboratory procedure.
3. **Retrodictive age** — elapsed time calculated by applying a historical model to present measurements.
4. **Actual elapsed age** — time since the historical event itself.
5. **Inherited/system-state age** — chronological information encoded in a system state that need not equal the elapsed duration of the specimen or event under examination.

A retrodictive age is not treated as identical to actual elapsed age merely because multiple measurements converge. Convergence must be evaluated for shared assumptions and common-mode dependencies.

## Research obligation

The hard-core constraint does not license dismissal of anomalous dates without analysis. For each conflicting case, the programme shall ask:

1. What physical quantity was actually measured?
2. What historical assumptions convert that measurement into elapsed time?
3. Which candidate chronological-contamination mechanisms could bias the result old?
4. Are the supposedly independent dating methods genuinely independent at the level of historical assumptions?
5. Can a proposed contamination mechanism be independently measured, bounded, reproduced, or falsified?
6. Does the BWM-compatible chronology predict any distinctive observable consequence?

Where no plausible mechanism has yet been established, the correct programme status is **unresolved chronological contamination**, not a fabricated explanation.

## Initial case study

Sima de los Huesos, Atapuerca, Spain, is the initial high-value case. Conventional chronology places the human remains at roughly 430 ka, while ancient nuclear DNA places the specimens on the Neanderthal lineage. Under the BWM Adamic chronology constraint, the remains are post-Fall. The research question is therefore:

> What measured variables and historical assumptions generate the approximately 430 ka retrodictive age, and what chronological-contamination mechanism or combination of mechanisms can account for the discrepancy with post-Fall elapsed history?

The case should be decomposed by dating method, including luminescence, uranium-series, paleomagnetic, stratigraphic, and associated contextual constraints, with primary-source documentation for each claim.

## Falsifiability and protective-belt discipline

The Adamic chronology constraint is a hard-core commitment of BWM. Particular contamination mechanisms belong to the protective belt and remain defeasible. A proposed mechanism that fails quantitative or experimental testing shall be rejected and replaced rather than protected ad hoc.

The programme therefore distinguishes the fixed theological boundary from empirical hypotheses used to explain apparently conflicting retrodictive ages.
