# Argument from Intelligible Actuality

Status: candidate canonical Foundations artifact
Classification: Foundations > Ontology / Metaphysics
Public name: Argument from Intelligible Actuality
Internal pattern: LIA Actualization / State-Selection Argument

## Purpose

This argument asks a question logically prior to BWM's historical frameworks:

> Why is there an actual, logically ordered, informationally determinate, causally efficacious reality rather than nothing, mere possibility, or inert structure?

DFM addresses the initialized state of creation. PFH addresses pre-Fall history. CHFM addresses catastrophic Flood history. This argument addresses the metaphysical ground required for there to be any actual and intelligible reality for those frameworks to describe.

It interfaces with TRT, LRT, and Semantic Actualism without treating any one research model as established merely by the argument.

## Terms

- **Logic (L):** irreducible rational constraint, including identity, non-contradiction, distinction, and valid inference.
- **Information (I):** determinate form or content by which a state is this rather than that and is therefore structured and intelligible.
- **Action (A):** causal efficacy or actualizing power by which a determinate state obtains.
- **Actualization (chi):** the obtaining of a determinate state rather than its remaining merely representable or possible.
- **Possibility:** a state that may be representable and logically admissible without thereby being actual.
- **Derivative:** dependent for its actuality or efficacy upon something more fundamental.
- **Ultimate ground:** a non-derivative explanatory ground sufficient for the reality under consideration.

## LIA role distinction

The primitives perform different explanatory roles:

- Logic constrains what is admissible.
- Information specifies determinate content.
- Action actualizes.
- Actualization is the resulting actuality.

In the existing TRT notation:

`chi = A(I | L)`

This should be read as a role relation rather than as an ordinary physical equation: action actualizes informational content under logical constraint.

Semantic Actualism supplies a complementary modal distinction:

`representable -> logically admissible -> actualizable -> actual`

The central problem arises at the final transition. Logical admissibility does not entail actuality. A description of a possible state does not instantiate that state.

## Transcendental minimum

1. An actual, intelligible, information-bearing, causally active reality exists.
2. Denial of intelligibility, logical distinction, or truth necessarily employs intelligible assertion, distinction, and truth-claiming and is therefore performatively self-undermining.
3. An intelligible actuality minimally presupposes logical constraint, determinate content, and actuality or efficacy.
4. Nothing, mere possibility, and wholly inert abstraction contain no sufficient actualizing efficacy.

Therefore, actual intelligible reality presents an explanatory demand that cannot be discharged by appeal to nothing or possibility as such.

This stage establishes a minimum. It does not by itself establish classical theism or Christianity.

## Metaphysical grounding argument

1. Actual intelligible reality exists.
2. Actual intelligible reality includes real logical constraint, determinate informational content, and actualization.
3. Contingent, derivative, or otherwise non-self-explanatory actuality requires an adequate ground.
4. Mere possibility cannot actualize itself, because describing what could obtain does not cause it to obtain.
5. A wholly derivative series of actualizers does not, merely by being extended, explain why actualizing efficacy exists at all.
6. Therefore, actuality requires a non-derivative ground adequate to intelligibility, determinacy, and efficacy.

Conclusion:

> There exists a non-derivative ground adequate to logic, informational determinacy, and actuality.

This is the principal metaphysical conclusion of the argument. The theological identification is a further stage.

## Ground-to-agency bridge

The next question is what properties such a ground must possess.

The argument should distinguish at least three phenomena:

1. **Logical normativity:** inference can be valid or invalid and propositions can be consistent or inconsistent.
2. **Semantic determinacy:** content can be about something and can represent correctly or incorrectly.
3. **Actualizing efficacy:** one determinate actuality obtains rather than actuality remaining merely possible.

Physical configurations can instantiate patterns and Shannon information. That fact is granted. Pattern alone, however, does not yet explain truth-directed semantic content, norm-governed inference, or why any possible lawlike order is actual.

The candidate bridge claim is therefore:

> A ground sufficient for logical normativity, semantic determinacy, and actualizing efficacy must be rational in respect of intelligibility and efficacious in respect of actuality.

Further work must establish which additional properties follow, including necessity, unity, intentionality, personality, aseity, and the relation between rationality and agency. These properties must not be smuggled into the definitions.

## Theological identification

The Argument from Intelligible Actuality should not move directly from "intellect-like and power-like" to "therefore the Christian God."

The identification should proceed in stages:

1. non-derivative ground of actuality;
2. rational adequacy to logic and semantic content;
3. actualizing efficacy;
4. argument for intentional agency and the additional divine attributes;
5. classical-theist identification;
6. Christian identification through revelation and the historical case for Christ.

Within BWM, the final Christian identification is naturally integrated with LRT's Logos grounding and the canonical witness concerning the Logos, creation, and sustaining action. The philosophical argument remains distinguishable from the revelatory identification so that each inferential burden remains visible.

## State-selection formulation

A stronger cross-cutting formulation is:

> Given a space of logically admissible informational states, why is any determinate state actualized at all?

Abstractly:

`possibility space -> constraint -> state specification -> actualization`

In LIA terms:

`L + I + A -> chi`

This is not intended as arithmetic. It is an explanatory-role schema.

The state-selection problem recurs at multiple BWM interfaces:

- foundational metaphysics: why actuality rather than possibility alone;
- cosmology: why these initial conditions and this actual cosmic order;
- creation / DFM: what is specified at initialization and why;
- abiogenesis: how chemically possible states become functionally integrated living organization;
- consciousness: how physical descriptions relate to determinate semantic and phenomenal states.

These are not asserted to be identical problems. The common pattern is a research heuristic: distinguish possibility, constraint, specification, and actualization rather than allowing one category to silently perform the work of another.

## Restricted sufficient-reason principle

The argument does not require the claim that "everything needs a cause."

Use instead:

> Every contingent, derivative, or otherwise non-self-explanatory feature of reality requires an adequate ground or explanation.

A critic may reject this and terminate explanation in brute actuality. That response is logically available. The resulting cost should be stated accurately: explanation terminates at the existence of the very logical, informational, and causal order that makes rational explanation possible.

## Guardrails

Do not claim:

- that logic is an arbitrary object created by God;
- that all information is semantic information or requires a human-style coder;
- that action means intentional agency by definition;
- that physical science cannot explain causal processes;
- that the argument by itself establishes the Trinity, Incarnation, Resurrection, or the full Christian doctrine of God;
- that an infinite temporal regress is the central issue, since the grounding question applies even to a temporally beginningless universe.

Maintain distinctions among Shannon information, functional specification, semantic content, logical normativity, causal efficacy, and intentional agency.

## Debate-ready core

> Reality is actual, intelligible, determinate, and causally active. Logic constrains what can coherently obtain. Information supplies determinate content. Action supplies actualizing efficacy. Nothing and mere possibility cannot perform that actualizing role. If the actuality and efficacy we encounter are derivative, extending the derivative chain does not by itself explain actuality as such. The inquiry therefore presses toward a non-derivative ground adequate to intelligibility, determinacy, and actuality. The next philosophical task is to determine what properties such a ground must possess and whether rational agency provides the best adequate account.

## One-sentence question

> Given logically admissible informational possibilities, why is any determinate reality actual at all, and what is sufficient to ground that transition from possibility to actuality?
