# Biblical WorldModel Cross-Domain Invariants

Status: Architecture baseline
Authority context: BWM component architecture under `WP-BWM-0013`

## Purpose

Define the cross-domain invariants that constrain Biblical WorldModel (BWM) reasoning across Foundations, Historical Frameworks, World Domains, and Integration.

An invariant is a commitment that remains governing when evidence or hypotheses move between domains. It is not necessarily a complete doctrine or model. Its architectural role is to prevent one domain from silently assuming premises that contradict another domain or the governing foundations.

Invariants shall be applied as constraints, interface conditions, or falsification gates. They do not eliminate domain-specific analysis.

## Invariant classes

BWM recognizes four architectural classes:

1. **Epistemic invariants**: govern how claims are warranted.
2. **Ontological invariants**: govern what kinds of entities, causes, and states are admissible.
3. **Historical invariants**: govern boundary events and chronological relations.
4. **Covenantal/redemptive invariants**: govern representative, curse, promise, and restoration relations.

## I-01 Scripture-primary epistemic ordering

**Statement**

Scripture is the primary epistemic authority for biblical-historical claims. Nature is real revelation and legitimate evidence, but natural evidence is interpreted within rather than placed above canonical scriptural constraints.

**Constrains**

All Foundations, Historical Frameworks, and World Domains.

**Prevents**

- importing a scientific or historical reconstruction as the controlling interpretation of Scripture;
- treating methodological naturalism as a neutral metaphysical authority;
- allowing an empirical model to define what Scripture is permitted to mean.

**Interface consequence**

Domain work must identify the scriptural boundary before using secondary natural evidence to populate, test, or constrain a model.

## I-02 Scripture-interprets-Scripture canonical control

**Statement**

Immediate context and broader canonical warrant control theological and biblical-historical interpretation before external reconstruction.

**Constrains**

Hermeneutics, PFH, CHFM, anthropology, chronology, covenant/redemptive history, and public doctrinal synthesis.

**Prevents**

- isolated proof-texting;
- local readings that contradict later canonical interpretation;
- external reconstructions overriding explicit canonical relations.

## I-03 Logic, information, and agency are irreducible explanatory categories

**Statement**

BWM admits rational constraint, information/content, and causal agency as fundamental explanatory categories. Physical description alone is not assumed to exhaust causation or actuality.

**Constrains**

Foundations, cosmology, biology, anthropology, consciousness, abiogenesis, information theory, and model comparison.

**Prevents**

- defining information solely as observer-relative statistical description when semantic or functional content is at issue;
- assuming agency can be excluded before argument;
- treating physical state-transition equations as self-explanatory causes of their own initial states or laws.

**Source authority**

TRT/LRT remain authoritative in their external source repository and are consumed by BWM through pinned interfaces.

## I-04 Created functional maturity

**Statement**

God may initialize created systems in states sufficient for their commissioned functions. Functional maturity need not be produced by an antecedent natural history.

**Constrains**

DFM, cosmology, biology, geology, anthropology, chronology, and all retrodictive age arguments that cross an initialization boundary.

**Prevents**

- assuming every mature state must have been generated by the same processes that govern subsequent operation;
- treating reconstructed antecedent states as automatically historical.

## I-05 Retrodictive age does not entail actual antecedent history

**Statement**

A calculable retrodictive age is conditional on the assumed generative history and boundary conditions. Operational regularity warrants defeasible extrapolation but does not by itself entail that the extrapolated path was actually traversed.

**Constrains**

Cosmology, radiometric chronology, geology, archaeology, biology, and any inference from present state to origin history.

**Prevents**

- collapsing model age into elapsed history;
- using successful forward or backward calculation as proof of historical generation;
- ignoring initialization, catastrophe, inheritance, reset, or contamination boundaries.

**Canonical BWM maxim**

> Operational regularity warrants defeasible extrapolation; it does not entail generation of the initial state.

## I-06 Boundary events can change valid inference regimes

**Statement**

Creation/initialization, the Fall, the Flood, and other established historical transitions can alter which extrapolations, clocks, environmental assumptions, or causal regimes are valid across the boundary.

**Constrains**

DFM, PFH, CHFM, chronology, geology, archaeology, anthropology, biology, and integration ledgers.

**Prevents**

- assuming uniform boundary conditions across discontinuities;
- extrapolating a post-boundary regime through an event that the model itself says altered the system;
- treating all clocks as globally continuous absent independent warrant.

## I-07 Human death belongs to the post-Fall mortal order

**Statement**

Human bodily death, remains produced by death, and burial belong to the post-Fall mortal chronology. Total human existence and mortal human chronology are distinct categories.

**Constrains**

PFH, anthropology, archaeology, chronology, human-origins interpretation, and WP-BWM-0014.

**Prevents**

- placing genuinely human death before the Fall;
- equating archaeological evidence of human activity with evidence of human mortality;
- assuming Adamic or human antiquity is exhausted by the post-Fall mortality ledger.

**Interface ownership**

PFH governs possible pre-Fall human existence and activity. WP-BWM-0014 governs the post-Fall human mortality chronology and dating conflicts involving human death, remains, and burial.

## I-08 Adamic federal headship has corporate and domain consequences

**Statement**

Adam's covenantal failure has consequences for humanity under his representative headship and for the terrestrial creation entrusted to human stewardship. Adam's personal culpability must remain distinguishable from the corporate consequences of his breach.

**Constrains**

PFH, anthropology, covenant/redemptive history, human mortality, creation curse, and restoration.

**Prevents**

- reducing the Fall to a merely private moral failure;
- assuming corporate consequence requires biological transmission of Adam's personal guilt;
- separating the curse on humanity from the curse/futility affecting the created domain under Adamic stewardship.

## I-09 The Fall is a covenantal-historical boundary, not merely a date marker

**Statement**

The Fall terminates the unfallen covenantal administration and introduces curse, exile, human mortality, and the promise of the Seed. It therefore changes the theological significance of subsequent human chronology and genealogy.

**Constrains**

PFH, chronology, anthropology, covenant/redemptive history, genealogy, and human-origins interpretation.

**Prevents**

- treating Genesis 3 as an isolated moral episode without systemic consequences;
- assuming pre-Fall and post-Fall human history are governed by identical conditions;
- interpreting post-Fall genealogy without the curse/promise context.

## I-10 Post-Fall genealogy is redemptively structured

**Statement**

After Genesis 3:15, genealogy is not merely demographic accounting. The curse makes human life mortal and the promise makes human descent messianic. The biblical genealogical trajectory ultimately identifies the promised Redeemer.

**Constrains**

PFH, Genesis 4-5 interpretation, biblical chronology, covenant/redemptive history, Christology, and canonical integration.

**Prevents**

- treating genealogy as a purely secular population ledger;
- assuming every unrecorded person or interval is therefore absent from history;
- severing Adamic history from the canonical trajectory to Christ.

## I-11 Christ is the successful federal head and terminus of the Adamic problem

**Statement**

Christ as the Last Adam succeeds where Adam failed. Redemption therefore answers both the human and creation-wide consequences of Adamic breach and culminates in resurrection, restored inheritance, and liberation from curse.

**Constrains**

Covenant/redemptive history, anthropology, eschatology, creation restoration, and whole-BWM synthesis.

**Prevents**

- treating the Redeemer as an appendage to origins rather than the canonical resolution of the Adamic problem;
- separating human redemption from the restoration trajectory of creation;
- constructing a BWM historical narrative that terminates at catastrophe rather than consummation.

## I-12 Cross-domain claims must preserve category distinctions

**Statement**

BWM must distinguish at minimum: explicit scriptural statement, necessary inference, permissible inference, model hypothesis, empirical proposition, observation, retrodiction, chronology assignment, and unresolved tension.

**Constrains**

All integration work, evidence ledgers, public synthesis, model comparison, and publication.

**Prevents**

- promoting hypotheses to doctrine by repetition;
- treating observations as theory-free;
- treating permissible readings as established history;
- hiding unresolved contradictions behind aggregate confidence language.

## Operating rule for new claims

For any substantive new BWM claim, ask:

1. Which Foundation, Historical Framework, and World Domain owns the claim?
2. Which invariants constrain it?
3. Does it cross a boundary event?
4. What is its epistemic status?
5. What other domain commitments would it change if accepted?
6. What observation or canonical text could falsify or force revision of it?

A claim that cannot answer these questions should remain provisional and should not be promoted into canonical BWM requirements.

## Change control

An invariant is stronger than an ordinary domain hypothesis because other components depend on it. Therefore:

- changing an invariant requires explicit dependency analysis;
- conflicting downstream artifacts must be identified before acceptance;
- invariants derived from external authoritative programmes must retain pinned provenance;
- an invariant may be downgraded to a hypothesis if its warrant weakens;
- no invariant is protected from falsification by classification alone.
