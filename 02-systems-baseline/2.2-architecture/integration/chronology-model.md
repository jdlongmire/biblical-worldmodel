# BWM Chronology Integration Model

Status: Active Integration model
Type: Integration

## Purpose

Provide a common chronology vocabulary across DFM, PFH, CHFM, World Domains, and canonical chronology requirements without collapsing retrodictive age into actual elapsed history.

## Core distinctions

BWM chronology work shall distinguish at minimum:

- scripturally fixed sequence or boundary;
- biblically open interval;
- actual elapsed chronology;
- measured operational interval;
- retrodictive/model-derived age;
- observability or method horizon;
- disturbed/reset/inherited chronological indicator;
- unresolved chronological inference.

No one category may be silently substituted for another.

## Historical backbone

Current architectural sequence:

`creation/initialization -> operational created order -> human creation -> PFH -> Fall -> post-Fall/pre-Flood history -> Flood/CHFM -> post-Flood history -> covenant/redemptive history -> consummation`

This sequence establishes ordering, not numeric duration for every interval.

## Framework ownership

- **DFM** governs chronological inference across the initialization boundary.
- **PFH** governs the human-creation-to-Fall interval and its open duration questions.
- **BWM-CHR-001 / 002** govern human mortality chronology and dating conflicts involving human death/remains.
- **CHFM** governs chronological disturbance associated with the Flood catastrophe where mechanism/evidence support it.
- **World Domains** contribute discipline-specific evidence; they do not independently determine epistemic authority.

## Current integrated chronology state

### Creation / initialization

Numeric elapsed age before or across the initialization boundary is not inferred merely from mature present state. DFM owns this boundary question.

### Human creation to Fall

PFH treats elapsed duration as an open variable. No canonical BWM numeric interval is currently assigned.

### Fall boundary

Human mortality enters the post-Fall order under BWM-CHR-001. This fixes sequence and category, not a complete numeric chronology.

### Post-Fall human mortality

Human death, bodily remains resulting from death, and burial events belong to post-Fall chronology. Retrodictive ages that exceed the permitted chronology require method-specific analysis under BWM-CHR-002.

### Flood / CHFM

CHFM may explain disturbance, reset, inheritance, or mixing in terrestrial chronological indicators only where an explicit physical mechanism and evidence support the claim.

### Radiocarbon method horizon

The approximately 50–60 ka practical radiocarbon horizon is treated as a method/observability horizon rather than a biblical chronological boundary. Any relationship to PFH duration remains an open hypothesis.

## Chronology record schema

Each integrated chronology claim should identify:

1. event or interval;
2. framework owner;
3. scriptural status;
4. empirical source/method;
5. quantity reported;
6. whether that quantity is elapsed, measured, modeled, calibrated, lower-bound, or upper-bound;
7. assumptions required to translate measurement into elapsed history;
8. known disturbance/contamination/reset mechanisms;
9. confidence/status;
10. competing interpretation;
11. source provenance;
12. current BWM disposition.

## Governing rules

Operational regularity may warrant defeasible extrapolation within an established regime. It does not by itself establish generative continuity across an initialization, Fall-related, catastrophic, or other relevant historical boundary.

Where BWM rejects a retrodictive age as actual elapsed history, it incurs a research obligation to identify and test the physical or inferential source of the discrepancy. "Unresolved" remains a valid status.
