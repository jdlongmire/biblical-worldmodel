## Historical jurisdiction

`pre-Flood terrestrial order -> Flood catastrophe -> immediate post-Flood terrestrial state`

CHFM supplies BWM's principal current physical mechanism framework for investigating the historical global Flood and its terrestrial consequences.

## BWM-adopted interface propositions

At the architectural level BWM consumes CHFM for:

- treatment of the Flood as a real historical global catastrophe;
- explicit separation of geological observations from historical reconstruction;
- a mechanism-bearing catastrophic model that is quantitatively testable and defeasible in its protective belt;
- analysis of tectonic translation, sedimentation, fossilization, hydrothermal/geochemical alteration, and immediate post-catastrophe state;
- potential disturbance, reset, inheritance, or mixing of geological/geochemical chronological indicators where specifically demonstrated.

## Boundary

BWM shall preserve CHFM's own confidence labels and unresolved problems. Specific hydroplaning parameters, subduction-channel mechanisms, stratigraphic boundary candidates, isotope-perturbation hypotheses, and chronology estimates are not promoted to BWM hard-core status merely by this interface.

## Domain interfaces

Primary:

- Earth history and geology;
- archaeology/chronology.

Supporting:

- biology/paleontology;
- climatology and biogeography;
- anthropology where Flood survival/dispersal is implicated;
- covenant/redemptive history at the Noahic transition.

## Relationship to DFM and PFH

DFM governs initialization questions that precede ordinary historical development. PFH governs the human-creation-to-Fall interval. CHFM governs the later Flood catastrophe. Evidence may cross these jurisdictions, so BWM Integration must identify which boundary or framework is actually being invoked in any chronological explanation.
