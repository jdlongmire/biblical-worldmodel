# Anthropology

Type: World Domain

## Scope

This domain owns BWM claims concerning human nature, identity, agency, and distinctively human capacities.

Primary topics include:

- imago Dei;
- Adam and Eve;
- human nature and personhood;
- consciousness and mind;
- language and symbolic reasoning;
- culture, engineering, architecture, and teleological construction;
- Neanderthal and Denisovan classification;
- human/non-human boundary questions;
- reproduction, kinship, and demographic capability where anthropologically relevant.

## Primary framework interfaces

- DFM, for created human maturity;
- PFH, for pre-Fall human history and development;
- CHFM, for Flood-era survival/dispersal where relevant;
- Covenant/redemptive history, for post-Fall theological identity and vocation.

## Boundary

Anthropology asks what humans are and how human capacities are expressed. Archaeology/Chronology asks when material traces and events belong. These domains interface closely but should remain distinct.
