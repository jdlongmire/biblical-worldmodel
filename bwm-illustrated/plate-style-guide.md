## 2. Visual language

- **Paper:** antique warm ivory / off-white; restrained aged parchment and very subtle foxing. Abundant light negative space. Avoid saturated yellow or orange parchment, thick gold framing, and dark full-bleed backgrounds.
- **Illustration:** fine copperplate or wood-engraving linework, delicate crosshatching, softly hand-tinted watercolor. Muted sage and olive, slate and Prussian blue, dusty terracotta, restrained ochre, warm sepia. Rich illustrative detail but subdued overall chroma.
- **Border:** thin, elegant sepia ornamental frame with modest corner filigree and botanical sprigs. Avoid heavy baroque scrolls, excessive gilding, glossy effects, and ornament that competes with the illustration.
- **Type:** classical engraved serif, book typography; generous spacing; readable dark brown-black ink. Title hierarchy, small caps, italic subtitle, restrained rules, numbered callouts. Final text is typeset, never baked into generated art (section 5).
- **Composition:** an editorial page, not a modern infographic. Clear header; dominant engraved central illustration; a legible supporting chronology or process sequence; short bottom explanatory compartments. Strong margins and an aligned column grid.
- **Tone:** scholarly, devotional, observational, visually inviting. Original illustrations only; never copies of reference-book photographs or layouts.

## 3. Human figures and safeguards

- Adam and Eve may be portrayed as dignified, tastefully nude in pre-Fall scenes, using seated poses, three-quarter or back views, and natural occlusion by posture or foliage; no eroticization and no modern clothing.
- No human likeness of Christ, and no visual depiction of God the Father.
- Do not imply that human ethnic identity is biblically specified.
- Do not imply physical geocentrism: where orbital mechanics are shown (Day 4), use correct heliocentric explanatory geometry; earth may be central as the narrative viewpoint only.
- Steampunk or mechanical devices, where used, are visual metaphor, not assertions about historical technology, and are labelled as symbolism in the companion record.

## 4. Claim discipline

Every label on a plate is one of six classes, the same classes the spread template already uses:

- **T - Text:** directly stated in Scripture.
- **E - Exegesis:** interpretation of the text.
- **O - Observation:** empirically observed datum.
- **M - Model:** a structured reconstruction.
- **H - Hypothesis:** a tentative causal or explanatory proposal.
- **U - Unresolved:** an open question.

Model timing, created-kind counts, historical geography, asynchronous cosmology, and illustrative anatomy are never presented as directly stated in Scripture. Reconstructed times in a chronology are labelled as a proposed model on the plate itself. ESV is the preferred translation; a quotation is printed only when its exact wording and attribution have been independently verified, otherwise the plate paraphrases and cites.

## 5. Text and traceability integrity

- Artwork and text are separate layers: an art-only master, with all headings, quotations, labels, and footnotes typeset in an editable layout. AI image text is never carried into a production plate.
- The plate carries no internal programme codes. Work-package and research traceability is kept in the companion record, where a reader of the record can follow it.
- Provenance, generation history, and human edits are recorded per plate in its companion record (see the plate register and the production checklist).
