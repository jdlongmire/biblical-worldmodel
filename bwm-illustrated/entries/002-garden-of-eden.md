# Entry 002: The Garden of Eden

**Status:** Draft editorial prototype. **Primary text:** Genesis 2:4–25; 3:1–24.

## Spread thesis

The narrative depicts a prepared garden, a human vocation to cultivate and keep it, divine command, and the boundary introduced by disobedience. Distinguish the garden's narrated features from attempts to locate it on a modern map.

## Left plate: Garden and vocation

Illustrate an original, non-cartographic garden panorama with the man tending vegetation, trees and a river. Insets: tree of life; tree of the knowledge of good and evil; cultivation and keeping. Numbered references to Genesis 2:8–17. **Caption: imaginative reconstruction, not archaeological survey.**

## Right plate: Rivers, command and consequence

Diagram the four named rivers as a textual relationship schematic, explicitly not to geographic scale. Include a sequence strip: placement, command, temptation, disobedience, expulsion. Avoid depicting God in bodily form. Reference Genesis 2:10–14 and 3:1–24.

## Claim ledger

- [T] Garden planted in Eden, human placed to work and keep it (Gen. 2:8, 15).
- [T] One river divides into four named branches (Gen. 2:10–14).
- [T] Specific command concerning a tree (Gen. 2:16–17).
- [T] Disobedience, judgment and expulsion (Gen. 3).
- [H/U] Precise modern geographic identification of Eden remains unestablished here.
- [M] Functional-maturity implications require independent argument and attribution.

## Research and art gate

Validate Hebrew and exegetical details, historical-geography proposals, and any chronology before final copy. Do not place speculative modern locations on an authoritative map.
